## 凡例

**一、** 本文事实性陈述尽可能落到一手来源：官方统计（皮尤研究中心、各国普查与住户调查）、官方文件（国家宗教事务局、国务院新闻办公室白皮书）、司法通报、机构官方发布，以及同行评议的学术研究。正文以 **A**（一手）、**B**（学术）、**C**（权威新闻与机构消息稿）三级标注。

**二、** 教团或机构对自身体量与成效的陈述，一律标 **【自述】**。此类数字未必虚假，但未经独立核实，且同一机构的数字常因年份、部门与口径不同而互相参差。

**三、** 证据不足、或本身存在多方异说者，标 **〔待核〕〔存疑〕**，并列异说而不裁断。凡查不到的，就说查不到——不以推测填补。

**四、** 属推论、评估、预测与主张者，标 **（判断）**，与事实陈述分开。

**五、** **D** 级材料（各类百科、博客、自媒体）只作检索线索，不作论据。

> **EN对应 · Note on Sources**
>
> **1.** Factual statements rest wherever possible on primary sources: official statistics (Pew Research Center; national censuses and household surveys), official documents (the State Administration for Religious Affairs; the State Council Information Office white paper), judicial notices, institutional releases, and peer-reviewed scholarship. Grades **A** (primary), **B** (academic), and **C** (authoritative news and institutional releases) appear throughout.
>
> **2.** An order's or institution's own account of its size and effectiveness is marked **【self-reported】**. Such figures are not necessarily false, but they are not independently verified, and the same institution's numbers often diverge across years, departments, and definitions.
>
> **3.** Where evidence is insufficient or sources disagree, the text marks **〔pending verification〕** or **〔disputed〕** and sets the accounts side by side without adjudicating. Where nothing could be found, the text says so rather than filling the gap with conjecture.
>
> **4.** Inferences, assessments, forecasts, and assertions are marked **（判断）** (judgment), separated from statements of fact.
>
> **5.** **Grade D** material (general encyclopedias, blogs, self-published media) serves only to locate sources, never as grounds for argument.

## 导论

一九九八年二月，印度菩提伽耶。一百三十二位女性登上同一座戒坛，领受具足戒。她们来自二十二个国家；其中多数人所归属的传统，已经将近九百年不曾出现过比丘尼。为她们传戒的，是一个源自汉地、二十世纪中叶流散至台湾、再由此散向五大洲的僧团。

这件事在汉传佛教对外的自我叙述里，位置并不显眼。

把它放在开头，是因为它恰好标出了本文要处理的那个错位。汉传佛教对世界佛教最实质的一项贡献——一份南传与藏传都已失传的完整比丘尼戒统——在自己讲述自己的时候，几乎不被提起。而被反复讲述的，是另一些东西：多少座道场，覆盖多少国家，多少信众。

### 三条通道

「汉传佛教的全球传播」，这句话在日常语言里几乎不需要解释：有一个主体，朝一个方向，以一定的规模在移动。

稍加分辨就会发现，它不是一件事，是至少三件事。

一条是**教团**。台湾系跨国教团、海外华人道场、人间佛教系统的据点。有僧团，有信众，有晨钟暮鼓与一日三餐。这条通道能真正完成传法，代价是慢。

一条是**文本**。汉文大藏经、禅宗文献、经论的学术译介。它的体量其实远大于第一条，但其中大部分经由日本、韩国、越南与西方学界转手，与汉传教团本体几乎没有往来。一个西方学者可以终身研究《大智度论》，而不曾踏入一座汉传寺院。

一条是**符号**。脱离教团语境的 Zen、禅意、正念。影响最广，门槛最低，离佛法本体最远。

三条通道的强弱次序，与「传法」的实质程度恰好相反：越是全世界都乐于接受的，越是脱离教团本体；越是能承担传法之实的，越是走不出去。许多关于「汉传影响世界」的叙述，都是在把这三层含义混着说——说「遍及百余国家」时，指的可能是某国某城一家禅意咖啡馆，而非一座寺院。（判断）

### 四件事

本文的基本判断有四条，先陈其要。

**其一，母体在收缩，海外的增量主要靠移民而非皈依。** 全球佛教徒从二〇一〇年的三亿四千三百万降至二〇二〇年的三亿二千四百万，是唯一在绝对数上下降的主要宗教。美国十年增长百分之二十二，但增长几乎全部来自亚洲移民，而非宗教转换。

**其二，瓶颈不在教义吸引力。** 皮尤的调查里有一个数字很说明问题：亚裔美国人中，出于家庭或文化原因「亲近佛教」的占百分之二十一，正式认同佛教的只占百分之十一——亲近者是认同者的将近两倍。好感是有的，路没有修好。

**其三，真正卡住的是四件事**：治理公信、语言与人才断层、政治耦合带来的跨境信任成本、典籍与概念的可及性赤字。这四件事没有一件涉及「佛法讲得好不好」，全都涉及组织可不可信、语言通不通达、身份清不清白、典籍拿不拿得到。

**其四，最有杠杆的方向是公共产品化与基础设施先行。** 汉传在海外最被认可、争议最小的贡献，几乎无一例外是非传教性的公共产品：比丘尼戒统、人道救援、禅修训练、典籍翻译与数字化。AI 是其中最大的变量，也是最大的治理难题。（判断）

### 全文之序

第一部看现状——家底有多少，谁在传，传到哪儿，怎么走到今天。数字难看的照样写。

第二部谈病。九处，一处一节。治理那一节最难写，也最短。

第三部看手里的新工具：AI 能做什么，不能做什么，哪些事看起来能做其实不能做。

第四部谈路。这一部最重，因为「应该如何」才是本文真正要回答的问题。

> **EN对应 · Introduction**
>
> In February 1998, at Bodh Gaya in India, one hundred and thirty-two women stepped onto a single ordination platform and received full ordination. They came from twenty-two countries, and the tradition to which most of them belonged had not seen a Buddhist nun in nearly nine hundred years. The sangha that ordained them was one that originated in China, was scattered to Taiwan in the middle of the twentieth century, and from there dispersed across five continents.
>
> That event occupies no prominent place in Chinese Buddhism's accounts of itself abroad.
>
> It opens this report because it marks out exactly the dislocation the report exists to examine. The most substantial contribution Chinese Buddhism has made to world Buddhism—a complete bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage lost in both the Theravāda and Tibetan traditions—is hardly mentioned when Chinese Buddhism tells its own story. What gets told instead is a set of other things: how many temples, how many countries, how many adherents.
>
> ### Three Channels
>
> "The global transmission of Chinese Buddhism" reads, in ordinary usage, as though it needed no explanation: one subject, one direction, one scale of movement.
>
> A little scrutiny shows that it is not one thing but at least three.
>
> One is **the order**: transnational Taiwanese Buddhist orders, overseas Chinese temples, the strongholds of Humanistic Buddhism. There are monastic communities, lay followers, morning bells and evening drums, three meals a day. This channel can actually transmit the dharma. It is slow.
>
> One is **the text**: the Chinese canon, Chan literature, scholarly translation of the treatises. Its volume is in fact far larger than the first channel's, but most of it passes through Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, and Western scholarship, with almost no contact with the Chinese Buddhist orders themselves. A Western scholar may spend a lifetime on the *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra* without ever setting foot in a Chinese Buddhist temple.
>
> One is **the sign**: Zen, Zen aesthetics, mindfulness, all detached from any monastic context. Widest reach, lowest threshold, furthest from the dharma itself.
>
> The order of strength among the three runs exactly opposite to the substance of transmission: what the world accepts most readily is what has departed furthest from the lineage; what can actually carry the dharma is what cannot get out. Much talk of "Chinese Buddhism's global influence" blends the three together—"present in over a hundred countries" may mean a Zen-themed café in some city, not a temple. (Judgment)
>
> ### Four Judgments
>
> **First, the mother body is contracting, and overseas growth comes from immigration rather than conversion.** The global Buddhist population fell from 343 million in 2010 to 324 million in 2020—the only major religion to decline in absolute numbers. American Buddhism grew 22 percent over the decade, but that growth came almost entirely from Asian immigration, not from religious conversion.
>
> **Second, the bottleneck is not the appeal of the teachings.** One Pew figure is telling: among Asian Americans, 21 percent feel "close to Buddhism" for family or cultural reasons, while only 11 percent formally identify as Buddhist—nearly twice as many feel close as belong. The goodwill exists. The road is not built.
>
> **Third, four concrete things are stuck**: the credibility of governance, the gap in language and personnel, the cross-border trust cost created by political entanglement, and the deficit in access to texts and concepts. Not one of the four concerns whether the dharma is taught well. All four concern whether the organization can be trusted, whether the language reaches, whether the identity is clean, and whether the texts can be obtained.
>
> **Fourth, the highest-leverage direction is public goods and infrastructure first.** The contributions for which Chinese Buddhism is most recognized abroad and least contested are almost without exception non-proselytizing public goods: the bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage, humanitarian relief, meditation training, translation, and digitization. AI is the largest variable among them—and the hardest to govern. (Judgment)
>
> ### How the Report Proceeds
>
> Part One surveys the present: what we hold, who transmits it, where it has reached, and how it got here. Unflattering numbers are included.
>
> Part Two examines the illnesses—nine of them, one to a section. The section on governance is the hardest to write and the shortest.
>
> Part Three looks at the new tools at hand: what AI can do, what it cannot, and what it appears able to do but cannot.
>
> Part Four considers the road. It carries the most weight, because "what should be done" is the question this report exists to answer.

# 第一部　把家底摊开

## 一、三个数字与一个空缺

皮尤研究中心二〇二五年六月九日的报告，给出一件不太体面的事实：全球佛教徒从二〇一〇年的三亿四千三百万，降至二〇二〇年的三亿二千四百万（A 级，[Pew, *Buddhist population change*](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/06/09/buddhist-population-change/)）。

百分之五。听上去不多。但它是主要宗教中唯一一个绝对数在下降的。

降幅高度集中在中国与日本：前者减少约两千三百万，后者约七百万，五个主要国家合计减少三千二百万，占该地区佛教人口的百分之二十二。原因并不神秘——脱离宗教认同、信众老龄化、生育率低，三样叠在一起。九成八的佛教徒生活在亚太；北美增至约五百万（增百分之二十七），欧洲约二百五十万（增百分之二十六），拉美约三十三万（减百分之三）。皮尤对中国二〇二〇年的估计是五千三百万，占其人口百分之四。

这组数字用起来要当心两处。其一，皮尤统计的是**自我认同者**。一个人可以在家里供着观音、逢年过节去寺里上香、遇事念几句佛号，问卷上却勾「无宗教信仰」——对汉传这种信仰实践与身份认同长期分离的传统，这个口径尤为不利。其二，皮尤不区分传统：它数的「佛教徒」，是汉传、藏传、南传、日系、韩系、越系的总和。

### 百分之四与百分之三十三

中国大陆的佛教人口，可以从百分之四一直数到百分之三十三。差异不在事实，在定义。

自我认同为佛教徒的，约百分之四（二〇一八年 CGSS，约四千二百万成年人；二〇二一年仍是百分之四）。「相信佛／菩萨」的，百分之三十三（二〇一八年 CFPS，约三亿六千二百万），其中只信佛而不信其他神明的占百分之十六。（均为 A 级，皮尤对 CGSS 与 CFPS 原始数据的再分析：[中国六事](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/21/6-facts-about-buddhism-in-china/)、[佛教](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/08/30/buddhism/)）

中国佛教协会的口径又是另一回事。二〇一七年两会上，时任会长学诚答问时说：「据不完全统计，目前中国的佛教徒人数有**近二亿**」（C 级，[贵州省民宗委转载](https://mzw.guizhou.gov.cn/xwzx/yw/201703/t20170314_10581823.html)）。

百分之四与百分之三十三之间隔着八倍。这不是统计误差，是两种存在方式的距离：一端是「我是一名佛教徒」这样的身份声明，另一端是「我相信佛菩萨」这样的信仰实践。**汉传在大陆的真实形态，大量存在于这两个数字之间的地带。**

一个「核心薄、外围厚」的母体，后果是可以推想的。外围再厚，也难以直接变成译经的译师、跨语的法师、可审计的治理。教团通道靠的从来不是外围的人数，是核心的人才——而核心恰恰是薄的那一头。（判断）

### 三万三千五百座与二十二万人

二〇一八年国务院新闻办公室《中国保障宗教信仰自由的政策和实践》白皮书给出的两组数字（A 级，[人民网全文](http://cpc.people.com.cn/n1/2018/0404/c64387-29906950-2.html)）：官方登记且带僧团的佛教寺院约**三万三千五百座**，其中汉传约两万八千余座；登记佛教教职人员约**二十二万二千人**，其中汉传约七万二千、藏传约十四万八千、南传约两千。经国家宗教事务局批准的宗教院校共九十一所，其中佛教四十一所（截至二〇一七年九月）。

第二组数字里有个结构值得看一眼。汉传的寺院数量是藏传的数十倍，登记教职人员却只有藏传的约一半。这个比例未必直接反映真实的僧才对比——登记口径与藏传的学经制度都会影响统计，不宜简单解读——但它至少提示：汉传手里**硬件远多于软件**。一座寺院三年可以建成，一位法师要二十年。

### 那个空缺

三个数字之后，有一个空缺。

**全球汉传佛教信众究竟有多少，没有数字。**

这不是检索不力。皮尤不按传统拆分，各国普查也不按传统拆分，各教团的自述口径互不相通，且不对外审计。一个流传了两千年、遍布五大洲、有数万座寺院和数十万僧尼的传统，**说不清自己有多少人**。

这个空缺本身就是一个发现。它会在第十二章再出现一次——那时它是作为一项能力缺陷出现的。

> **EN对应 · One: Three Numbers and a Gap**
>
> The Pew Research Center's report of 9 June 2025 delivers a fact that is not flattering: the global Buddhist population fell from 343 million in 2010 to 324 million in 2020 (Grade A, [Pew, *Buddhist population change*](https://www.pewresearch.org/2025/06/09/buddhist-population-change/)).
>
> Five percent. It does not sound like much. But it is the only absolute decline among the major religions.
>
> The fall is heavily concentrated in China and Japan—about 23 million fewer in the former, 7 million in the latter, with five major countries together losing 32 million, or 22 percent of the region's Buddhist population. The reasons are not mysterious: detachment from religious identity, an ageing sangha, low fertility, stacked together. Ninety-eight percent of Buddhists live in Asia-Pacific; North America rose to about 5 million (+27 percent), Europe to about 2.5 million (+26 percent), Latin America to about 330,000 (−3 percent). Pew estimates China's Buddhist population in 2020 at 53 million, four percent of the population.
>
> Two cautions apply. First, Pew counts **self-identification**. A person may keep a Guanyin image at home, visit a temple at the new year, and recite a Buddha's name when trouble comes, yet tick "no religion" on the form—a definition that works against a tradition in which practice and identity have long been separated. Second, Pew does not disaggregate by tradition: its "Buddhists" are the sum of Chinese, Tibetan, Theravāda, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese Buddhism combined.
>
> **Four Percent and Thirty-Three Percent.** The Buddhist population of mainland China can be counted as anything from 4 percent to 33 percent. The difference lies not in fact but in definition.
>
> Those who identify as Buddhist: about 4 percent (CGSS 2018, roughly 42 million adults; still 4 percent in 2021). Those who "believe in Buddhas and bodhisattvas": 33 percent (CFPS 2018, roughly 362 million), of whom 16 percent believe in Buddhas alone and in no other deities. (Both Grade A, from Pew's reanalysis of the original CGSS and CFPS data: [Six facts about Buddhism in China](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/09/21/6-facts-about-buddhism-in-china/); [Buddhism](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/08/30/buddhism/).)
>
> The Buddhist Association of China uses yet another figure. At the 2017 sessions, the then-president Shi Xuecheng answered a question with: "by incomplete count, China's Buddhists number **close to two hundred million**" (Grade C, [Guizhou provincial commission reprint](https://mzw.guizhou.gov.cn/xwzx/yw/201703/t20170314_10581823.html)).
>
> Between 4 percent and 33 percent lies an eightfold gap. This is not statistical error but the distance between two ways of existing: at one end a declaration of identity, at the other a practice of belief. **The actual shape of Chinese Buddhism in mainland China lies largely in the territory between these two numbers.**
>
> The consequences of a mother body thin at the core and thick at the periphery can be inferred. However thick the periphery, it does not convert directly into translators, multilingual monastics, or auditable governance. The order channel has never depended on the size of the periphery but on the talent at the core—and the core is the thin end. (Judgment)
>
> **Thirty-Three Thousand Five Hundred Temples and Two Hundred Twenty Thousand Clergy.** Two sets of figures from the State Council Information Office's 2018 White Paper (Grade A, [full text at People's Daily Online](http://cpc.people.com.cn/n1/2018/0404/c64387-29906950-2.html)): about **33,500** registered Buddhist temples with monastic communities, of which roughly 28,000 are Chinese Buddhist; and about **222,000** registered Buddhist clergy—some 72,000 Chinese Buddhist, 148,000 Tibetan, and 2,000 Theravāda. Of 91 religious colleges approved by the State Administration for Religious Affairs, 41 are Buddhist (as of September 2017).
>
> One structural feature of the second set deserves a look. Chinese Buddhist temples outnumber Tibetan ones several dozen times over, yet registered Chinese Buddhist clergy amount to about half the Tibetan figure. The ratio does not necessarily reflect the real balance of monastic training—registration practice and the Tibetan scholastic system both affect the count—but it points at least to this: Chinese Buddhism holds **far more hardware than software**. A temple can be built in three years. A monk takes twenty.
>
> **The Gap.** After three sets of figures comes a gap.
>
> **How many Chinese Buddhists there are in the world: there is no number.**
>
> This is not a failure of searching. Pew does not disaggregate by tradition; national censuses do not disaggregate by tradition; the orders' self-reported figures are mutually incomparable and not externally audited. A tradition that has run for two thousand years, spread across five continents, and holds tens of thousands of temples and hundreds of thousands of monastics **cannot say how many people it has**.
>
> The gap is itself a finding. It will appear once more in Chapter Twelve, where it figures as a capacity deficit.

## 二、谁在传

汉传佛教在海外的教团通道，主体几乎全是台湾系。

**佛光山**一九六七年五月十六日开山，是这条通道上体量最大的一家。关于它的规模，有一个数字上的麻烦，值得先说清楚，因为它几乎概括了汉传自我表述的全部习惯：官网今述「海内外**二百多所**道场」（A 级·【自述】，[fgs.org.tw](https://www.fgs.org.tw/career/career_global.aspx)），宗团文献中另有「三百余所」之说，而流传最广的「四十多国」「一百多国家」，实为**国际佛光会**的口径混入——佛光会官方自述「五大洲百余个国家地区、三百万会员」（A 级·【自述】，[blia.org.tw](https://www.blia.org.tw/about-us/)）。道场数与会员覆盖国数是两个量，混着说，会凭空放大一个数量级。截至二〇二六年，佛光山官方没有「N 国 N 座」的精确数字。

佛光山另有一项常被忽略的事实：它办有五所高等教育机构——美国西来大学（一九九〇）、台湾佛光大学（一九九三）、南华大学（一九九六）、澳洲南天大学（二〇一一）、菲律宾光明大学（二〇一四）（A 级，[佛光山教团系统大学](https://website.fgu.edu.tw/zh_tw/about/eduinstitutions)）。汉传佛教第一次成建制地进入世俗高等教育体系，靠的就是这五所。

海外枢纽西来寺一九七八年筹、一九八六年动工、一九八八年七月二十四日开光、十一月二十六日落成，是海外第一座别院与美洲总部（A 级，[西来寺官网](http://hsilai.org/about-us-origin/)）。此处有一处常见的年份混淆需要辨正：「国际佛光会一九九一年在西来寺成立」一语并不准确——一九九一年二月三日成立的是**中华佛光协会**，世界总会要到一九九二年五月十六日。

创办人星云大师一九二七年八月十九日生，二〇二三年二月五日圆寂，**享耆寿九十七岁**（周岁九十五）（A 级，[佛光山讣闻](https://www.fgs.org.tw/branch/activity_detail?BrID=D50601&LstID=6&ItmID=43&news_no=20230206000004&)）。流传材料中常见的「享年九十五」，与官方口径不符。

**慈济**一九六六年创立。官方二〇二五年口径为「志工分布六十八国、逾六百据点、援助足迹一百三十六国」（A 级·【自述】，[global.tzuchi.org](https://global.tzuchi.org/words-from-our-ceo)；二〇二五年年报导读已更新至一百三十九国）。二〇一〇年七月十九日获联合国经社理事会「NGO in Special Consultative Status」，持有主体是「佛教慈济基金会」整体，不是它的美国分会（A 级，[慈济基金会](https://www.tzuchi-org.tw/en/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=636&catid=116&Itemid=324&lang=en)）。

**法鼓山**走的是一条与上述两家都不同的路。圣严法师一九六九年赴日（立正大学），一九七一年获硕士，一九七五年以《明末中国佛教之研究》获文学博士、同年赴美；一九七九年在纽约创立禅中心（一九八一年命名东初禅寺）；一九九七年设纽约上州象冈道场（A 级，[圣严法师年谱](https://shengyen.org/bio.php)）。法鼓山本体的创建则要到一九八九年。

法鼓山最特别的地方，是它把传承交给了西方人。圣严法师的五位西方在家法子是 John Crook（一九九三）、Simon Child 与 Max Kalin（二〇〇〇年十二月一日）、Žarko Andričević（二〇〇一）、Gilbert Gutierrez（二〇〇二）（A 级，[圣严法师研究论文](https://www.shengyen.org.tw/userdata/upload/papers/paper-9.pdf)）。汉传的世系表上出现西人名字，这样的例子在历史上并不多。顺带辨正：常被列入这份名单的果谷（Jimmy Yu）并不是西方在家法子——他是圣严的剃度弟子（一九九一年出家、二〇〇〇年还俗），且不是西方人。

**中台禅寺**创办人惟觉长老一九二八至二〇一六（二〇一六年四月八日圆寂）。依中台官方《落成十周年回顾》，一九九二至二〇一〇年间海内外精舍达一百零八家（A 级·【自述】，[chungtai.org](https://www.chungtai.org/dialogue/2011/2011-08/10thAnniversary/sub04-gb.htm)）。

**法界佛教总会**走的是另一条路：严守戒律，加上译经。它的成立年份需要辨正——前身「中美佛教总会」一九五九年创立（一九六八年法人化），一九八四年才改现名；金山寺一九七〇年设于旧金山；万佛圣城一九七四年购地、一九七六年成立；佛经翻译委员会一九七〇年成立，一九七二年起出版。至于「万佛圣城是美国最早的禅宗道场之一」一说，也不确切：北美更早的还有旧金山禅中心（一九六二）、罗切斯特禅中心（一九六六）、金山寺（一九七〇）。

### 一件被自己忽略的事

汉传佛教对世界佛教最实质的一项贡献，是比丘尼戒统的回传。

佛教史上，比丘尼传承五世纪自师子国（斯里兰卡）传入中国：四二九年首批师子国比丘尼抵建康，四三三至四三四年间，铁索罗（Devasara）等人在南林寺依二部僧戒为三百余人授具足戒，僧伽跋摩任戒师（B 级，学界通说）。而斯里兰卡本土的比丘尼传承约在十一世纪断绝——一说一〇一七年朱罗入侵之后，具体年份学界有异说。

也就是说：**南传与藏传都已失传的比丘尼戒统，完整地保存在汉传手里。**

一九九八年二月十四至二十三日，由星云大师与佛光山主办，在印度菩提伽耶举行的国际比丘尼具足戒戒会，做的就是回传这件事。与会规模各说并存：二十二国、一百四十六人（其中一百三十二位女众）（Thubten Chodron 记述，[原文](https://www.thubtenchodron.org/zh-CN/1998/02/restoring-bhikshuni-ordination/)）；二十余国、一百五十余人（佛光山口径）；三十二国、一百五十人（韩国比丘尼会口径）。三说并列，本文不作裁断。

更早的一次是一九八八年十一至十二月，西来寺举办「万佛三坛大戒」，首次有南传出家众于大乘寺院受戒，藏传弟子亦来求戒，南传比丘尼登坛，是西方首次三坛大戒（A 级，人间社）。

把这两件事并置，汉传在全球佛教中的位置有一个侧面就清楚了：在戒统这件事上，它一直是给出的一方。而这件事在汉传自己的对外叙述里，位置并不显眼。

### 论坛、博鳌与少林

大陆官方主导的这条线，近二十年有三个标志性节点。

**世界佛教论坛**。首届二〇〇六年四月十三至十六日，杭州开幕、舟山普陀山闭幕（与会规模有「三十四国千余人」与「三十七国千二百余人」两说）；第二届二〇〇九年三月二十八日无锡开幕、四月一日台北闭幕，近五十国一千七百余位；第六届二〇二四年十月十五至十七日于宁波雪窦山举行，七十二国约八百人，另有「约七十国」说（C 级，[中新网](https://www.chinanews.com/gn/2024/10-14/10301492.shtml)、[中国佛教协会](https://chinabuddhism.com.cn/web/details/76260)）。从首届到第六届，规模收窄而国别增多。

**博鳌宗教分论坛**。二〇二五年三月二十八日，博鳌亚洲论坛年会举办第十届「宗教和合与文明互鉴」分论坛，主题是「百千法门 同归方寸——佛教典籍数字化工程的探讨与展望」。中国佛教协会会长演觉在总结发言中说，佛教典籍「是人类共同的文化遗产」（C 级，[中新网](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2025/03-28/10390689.shtml)）。

**少林寺**。释永信被称为「CEO 和尚」。二〇一五年二月二十三日，他赴澳洲新南威尔士州肖尔黑文市（Shoalhaven），向市长 Joanna Gash 交付支票，结清购地尾款 **四百一十六万二千澳元**（约合人民币二千零四十万元、约三百二十万美元；中文媒体另有四百一十二万澳元与「逾三百万澳元」等异说）。地块 Comberton Grange 约一千二百四十八公顷，项目总预算三亿六千万至三亿八千六百万澳元（C 级，[ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2015-02-23/shaolin-temple-abbot-pays-cheque/6227950)、[新华网](http://www.xinhuanet.com//world/2015-03/03/c_1114503265.htm)）。至于少林「在四十多个国家有商业与文化合作」一语，更准确的说法是释永信本人所述「四十多个海外文化机构／文化中心」（二〇一一、二〇一五年语）；少林寺二〇二二年数据另称海外中心二百多个、遍布五十多个国家和地区。两组数字年份不同、口径不同，不宜合用。

### 数字底座：最不该缺席者的缺席

汉传佛教在世界佛典数字化版图上的位置，与它自己的典籍体量很不相称。

**BDK 英译大藏经**由日本佛教传道协会自一九八二年起推进。其二〇一七与二〇一九两版丛书前言**都称第一辑选定的七千一百八十五页已完成约百分之六十五**。同一个数字出现在相隔两年的两篇前言里，是沿用旧文还是进度确实停滞，本文未能核实，标〔待核〕。

**Dharmamitra** 由加州大学伯克利分校 BAIR 的 Keutzer 与 Nehrdich 于二〇二三年四月创立，提供巴利、梵、藏与佛教汉语的机器翻译（MITRATranslate）、跨语种语义检索（MITRASearch）与 OCR（MITRA-OCR）。

**CBETA** 是汉文大藏经数字化的主干，长期采用 TEI/XML 结构化编码，使经典不仅能被阅读，也能被机器处理、引用与分析。它一九九八年由中华佛学研究所支持成立，圣严法师指示全力支持；二〇二三年法人化时，法鼓山佛教基金会捐资一千三百万，是最大的捐助者（A 级，[CBETA](https://cbeta.org)）。

**灵隐寺**开发了「基于人工智能的古籍 OCR 引擎」与古籍数字化生产平台，推进《径山藏》《永乐北藏》《思溪藏》；二〇二六年五月的研讨会上，「杭州云林数字佛典研究院」揭牌，同时发布的「如是佛典」公布了《径山藏》的数字化成果（A 级，[杭州佛协](https://www.hzfjxh.com)、[灵隐寺](https://www.lingyinsi.org)）。

**佛光山**二〇二五年四月十八日于国际佛光会北美洲联谊会（奥斯汀）发布「Vurbo.ai 佛教专业版（Buddhist 4.0）」，由国际佛光会世界总会、佛光山人间佛教研究院与爱比科技（IPEVO）合作，整合一万条佛教术语（C 级，[TechOrange](https://techorange.com/2025/04/22/vurbo-ai-blia/)）。「全球首套佛教专用翻译系统」系主办方自述。

把这五项并列：《大正藏》的英译由日本机构主导，佛教多语机器翻译由美国大学主导，汉文佛典的结构化由台湾一个基金会数十年撑着，大陆的进展近两年才在灵隐寺这样的单点上冒出来。

### 宗派的不对称

汉传佛教在海外呈现的面孔，其实只是它众多宗派中的一支。

教团型对外传播几乎由禅宗主导——法鼓山（圣严）、法界佛教总会（宣化）、中台（惟觉），无一不是禅门。净土在华人社群内部极为普遍，海外的能见度却低。华严、天台、唯识主要经由学术与译介传播，而非教团实践。

天台的一心三观、华严的事事无碍、唯识的心识分析，在义理上最具深度与对话潜力，反而最少以「活的修行传统」的形态被世界看见。个中缘由不难推想：禅宗本不重经论而重实证，与西方「灵性而非宗教」的口味天然合拍；教下诸宗以义理与次第立身，反而更依赖语言与教学系统——而出海最缺的恰恰是这两样。（判断）

> **EN对应 · Two: Who Transmits It**
>
> The order channel of overseas Chinese Buddhism is made up almost entirely of Taiwanese orders.
>
> **Foguang Shan** opened on 16 May 1967 and is the largest body on this channel. Its numbers carry a difficulty worth stating plainly, because it almost encapsulates the whole habit of Chinese Buddhist self-description: the website says "over **two hundred** temples worldwide" (Grade A, self-reported, [fgs.org.tw](https://www.fgs.org.tw/career/career_global.aspx)); order literature elsewhere says "over three hundred"; and the widely circulated "40-plus countries" and "over 100 countries" in fact belong to the **Buddha's Light International Association**, which reports "over a hundred countries and regions across five continents, three million members" (Grade A, self-reported, [blia.org.tw](https://www.blia.org.tw/about-us/)). Temple count and member-country reach are different quantities; run together, they inflate the picture by an order of magnitude. As of 2026 Foguang Shan has published no precise "N countries, N temples" figure.
>
> One fact about Foguang Shan is regularly overlooked: it runs five institutions of higher education—the University of the West (1990), Fo Guang University (1993), Nanhua University (1996), Nan Tien Institute (2011), and Guang Ming College (2014) (Grade A, [Foguang Shan system universities](https://website.fgu.edu.tw/zh_tw/about/eduinstitutions)). Chinese Buddhism entered the secular higher education system as an institution for the first time through these five.
>
> Its overseas hub, Hsi Lai Temple, was planned in 1978, begun in 1986, consecrated on 24 July 1988, and completed on 26 November that year (Grade A, [Hsi Lai Temple](http://hsilai.org/about-us-origin/)). One common confusion of dates needs correcting here: the statement that "the Buddha's Light International Association was founded at Hsi Lai in 1991" is imprecise—what was founded on 3 February 1991 was the **Buddha's Light Association of the Republic of China**; the world headquarters came on 16 May 1992.
>
> The founder, Master Hsing Yun, was born on 19 August 1927 and died on 5 February 2023, **aged 97 by Chinese reckoning** (95 by Western count) (Grade A, [Foguang Shan obituary](https://www.fgs.org.tw/branch/activity_detail?BrID=D50601&LstID=6&ItmID=43&news_no=20230206000004&)). The commonly circulated "aged 95" does not match the official account.
>
> **Tzu Chi** was founded in 1966. Its 2025 figures are "volunteers in 68 countries, over 600 offices, relief reach in 136 countries" (Grade A, self-reported, [global.tzuchi.org](https://global.tzuchi.org/words-from-our-ceo); the 2025 annual report preface has updated the reach to 139). On 19 July 2010 it obtained "NGO in Special Consultative Status" with the UN Economic and Social Council; the holder is the Buddhist Tzu Chi Foundation as a whole, not its American chapter (Grade A, [Tzu Chi Foundation](https://www.tzuchi-org.tw/en/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=636&catid=116&Itemid=324&lang=en)).
>
> **Dharma Drum Mountain** took a route unlike either. Master Sheng Yen went to Japan in 1969 (Rissho University), took his master's in 1971, received his doctorate in 1975 for a study of late-Ming Chinese Buddhism, and went to the United States the same year; he founded the Chan Center in New York in 1979 (renamed the Eastern Dharma Center of the Chung-Hwa Institute of Buddhist Culture in 1981) and established the upstate New York retreat in 1997 (Grade A, [Sheng Yen chronology](https://shengyen.org/bio.php)). The Dharma Drum organization itself dates to 1989.
>
> What makes Dharma Drum distinctive is that it handed the transmission to Westerners. Sheng Yen's five Western lay heirs are John Crook (1993), Simon Child and Max Kalin (1 December 2000), Žarko Andričević (2001), and Gilbert Gutierrez (2002) (Grade A, [Sheng Yen research paper](https://www.shengyen.org.tw/userdata/upload/papers/paper-9.pdf)). Names of Westerners on the transmission chart of Chinese Buddhism are not common. One correction: Jimmy Yu (果谷), often listed among them, is not a Western lay heir—he was Sheng Yen's ordained disciple (ordained 1991, returned to lay life in 2000) and is not a Westerner.
>
> **Chung Tai Shan** was founded by Master Weijue (1928–2016; died 8 April 2016). By the order's own *Tenth Anniversary Review*, its temples and meditation centres in Taiwan and abroad reached 108 between 1992 and 2010 (Grade A, self-reported, [chungtai.org](https://www.chungtai.org/dialogue/2011/2011-08/10thAnniversary/sub04-gb.htm)).
>
> **The Dharma Realm Buddhist Association** took yet another route: strict discipline, plus translation. Its founding year requires correction—the predecessor, the Sino-American Buddhist Association, was founded in 1959 (incorporated 1968) and renamed only in 1984; Gold Mountain Monastery was established in San Francisco in 1970; the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas bought its land in 1974 and was established in 1976; the Buddhist Text Translation Society was founded in 1970 and began publishing in 1972. Nor is it accurate that the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas was "one of the earliest Chan centres in the United States": earlier North American foundations include the San Francisco Zen Center (1962), the Rochester Zen Center (1966), and Gold Mountain Monastery (1970).
>
> **A Contribution We Ourselves Overlook.** The most substantial contribution Chinese Buddhism has made to world Buddhism is the return transmission of the bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage.
>
> In Buddhist history the bhikṣuṇī lineage came to China from Sri Lanka in the fifth century: in 429 the first Sinhalese nuns arrived at Jiankang, and in 433/434 Devasara and others conferred full ordination on more than three hundred women at Nanlin Temple according to the dual-sangha procedure, with Saṅghavarman as preceptor (Grade B, scholarly consensus). The bhikṣuṇī lineage in Sri Lanka itself died out around the eleventh century—one account places it after the Chola invasion of 1017; scholarly opinion differs on the exact date.
>
> That is to say: **the bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage, lost in both the Theravāda and Tibetan traditions, survives intact in Chinese Buddhism.**
>
> The international bhikṣuṇī ordination held at Bodh Gaya from 14 to 23 February 1998, convened by Master Hsing Yun and Foguang Shan, was that lineage being given back. Accounts of its size differ and are set side by side here: 22 countries and 146 participants, of whom 132 were women (Thubten Chodron's account, [original](https://www.thubtenchodron.org/zh-CN/1998/02/restoring-bhikshuni-ordination/)); over 20 countries and more than 150 (Foguang Shan's figure); 32 countries and 150 (the Korean Bhikṣuṇī Association's figure). This report does not adjudicate among them.
>
> The earlier occasion was the "Ten Thousand Buddhas Triple Platform Ordination" at Hsi Lai Temple in November–December 1988—the first time Theravāda monastics received ordination in a Mahāyāna temple, with Tibetan disciples also coming to seek ordination and Theravāda nuns taking the platform. It was the first triple platform ordination in the West (Grade A, Humanistic Buddhism News Agency).
>
> Set the two events side by side and one facet of Chinese Buddhism's position in world Buddhism becomes clear: in the matter of ordination lineage, it has consistently been the giving party. Yet in Chinese Buddhism's own accounts of itself abroad, the matter occupies no prominent place.
>
> **The Forum, Bo'ao, and Shaolin.** Three markers stand out over the past two decades on the line led by mainland officialdom.
>

The second opened in Wuxi on 28 March 2009 and closed in Taipei on 1 April, with nearly 50 countries and over 1,700. The sixth was held at Mount Xuedou in Ningbo, 15–17 October 2024, with 72 countries and about 800 (another account says "about 70") (Grade C, [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com/gn/2024/10-14/10301492.shtml); [Buddhist Association of China](https://chinabuddhism.com.cn/web/details/76260)). From the first to the sixth, the scale narrowed while the number of countries grew.

The president of the Buddhist Association of China, Yanjue, said in his closing remarks that Buddhist texts are "a common cultural heritage of humankind" (Grade C, [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2025/03-28/10390689.shtml)).
>
> **Shaolin Temple.** Shi Yongxin has been called the "CEO monk." On 23 February 2015 he travelled to Shoalhaven in New South Wales and handed the mayor, Joanna Gash, a cheque settling the balance on a land purchase of **A$4.162 million** (about RMB 20.4 million, roughly US$3.2 million; Chinese-language media also give A$4.12 million and "over A$3 million"). The two sets come from different years and different definitions and should not be run together.

The same figure appears in prefaces two years apart; whether this is a carried-over text or a genuine plateau, this report could not verify. Marked 〔pending verification〕.
>
> **Dharmamitra** was founded in April 2023 at UC Berkeley's BAIR by Kurt Keutzer and Sebastian Nehrdich, and provides machine translation for Pali, Sanskrit, Tibetan, and Buddhist Chinese (MITRATranslate), cross-lingual semantic search (MITRASearch), and OCR (MITRA-OCR).
>
> **CBETA** is the backbone of Chinese canonical digitization, long using TEI/XML structured encoding so that texts can not only be read but processed, cited, and analysed by machine. Founded in 1998 with the support of the Chung-Hwa Institute of Buddhist Studies at Master Sheng Yen's instruction, it received NT$13 million from the Dharma Drum Mountain Buddhist Foundation at incorporation in 2023—the largest single contribution (Grade A, [CBETA](https://cbeta.org)).
>
> **Lingyin Temple** in Hangzhou has developed an "AI-based OCR engine for ancient books" and a digitization production platform, working through the *Jingshan*, *Yongle Northern*, and *Sixi* canons. At a workshop in May 2026 the Hangzhou Yunlin Digital Buddhist Canon Research Institute was inaugurated, and a project named Rushi Fodian released its results for the *Jingshan Canon* (Grade A, [Hangzhou Buddhist Association](https://www.hzfjxh.com); [Lingyin Temple](https://www.lingyinsi.org)).
>
> **Foguang Shan** released "Vurbo.ai Buddhist Edition (Buddhist 4.0)" on 18 April 2025 at the BLIA North America conference in Austin, developed jointly by the BLIA World Headquarters, the Foguang Shan Institute of Humanistic Buddhism, and IPEVO, integrating ten thousand Buddhist terms (Grade C, [TechOrange](https://techorange.com/2025/04/22/vurbo-ai-blia/)). "The world's first Buddhist-specific translation system" is the organizers' own claim.
>
> Set the five side by side. The English translation of the Taishō canon is led by a Japanese body; multilingual Buddhist machine translation is led by an American university; the structuring of the Chinese canon has been carried for decades by a single Taiwanese foundation; and the mainland's progress has emerged only in the past two years, at single points such as Lingyin Temple.
>
> **The Asymmetry among Schools.** The face Chinese Buddhism shows abroad is in fact only one of its many schools.
>
> Order-based transmission abroad is dominated almost entirely by Chan—Dharma Drum Mountain (Sheng Yen), the Dharma Realm Buddhist Association (Hsuan Hua), and Chung Tai Shan (Weijue) are all Chan houses. Pure Land is extremely widespread within Chinese communities but has low visibility overseas. Huayan, Tiantai, and Yogācāra travel mainly through scholarship and translation rather than through living communities.
>
> Of all the schools, Tiantai's "one mind, three contemplations," Huayan's "unobstructed interpenetration of phenomena," and Yogācāra's analysis of consciousness are doctrinally the deepest and richest in dialogical potential—and they are precisely the ones least often seen abroad as living practice traditions. The reason is not hard to reconstruct: Chan has never emphasised scholastic texts and prizes direct realisation, which fits the Western taste for "spirituality rather than religion"; the doctrinal schools rest on doctrine and graduated stages, and therefore depend more heavily on language and teaching systems—exactly the two things scarcest overseas. (Judgment)

## 三、地图

### 新加坡的一个数字

二〇二五年新加坡住户调查中，十五至二十四岁的人里，有百分之二十六点八宣称自己没有任何宗教信仰；五十五岁以上的人里，这个比例是百分之十九点四（A 级，[SingStat](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/news/general-household-survey-25)）。

七个百分点，一代人的距离。

新加坡的佛教徒占比是百分之三十点九，无宗教信仰者百分之二十三点九——后者在二〇二〇年是百分之二十，二〇一〇年是百分之十七。佛教、道教、基督教、伊斯兰教的占比都在下降。这个城市国家的宗教版图正在被一种更彻底的东西侵蚀：不是另一个宗教，是不需要宗教。

马来西亚的佛教徒占全国人口百分之十八点七，约六百一十万，绝大多数为华裔（A 级，DOSM MyCensus 2020）。台湾的情形不同：皮尤二〇二四年七月二十九日的调查显示，佛教徒、无宗教信仰者、道教徒各约四分之一（A 级，[Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/29/4-facts-about-religion-and-diversity-in-taiwan/)）——三分天下，是这个岛屿的基本盘。

### 美国：百分之二十一与百分之十一

皮尤二〇二六年三月十一日的数据：美国佛教徒约四百四十万，占总人口百分之一点三，十年增长百分之二十二；成年佛教徒中百分之五十二出生于亚洲（越南、中国、泰国为最常见的三个出生地），百分之六十四为亚裔；百分之四十八是皈依者——但**百分之五十五的「自幼佛教徒」已不再自认佛教**。华裔美国人百分之五十六无宗教归属；越南裔有百分之三十七认同佛教（A 级，[Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/11/5-facts-about-buddhists-in-the-united-states/)）。

还有一组，我以为它是理解汉传海外处境的钥匙：亚裔中，出于家庭或文化原因「亲近佛教」的占百分之二十一，正式认同佛教的仅占百分之十一。

**亲近者是认同者的将近两倍。**

一个华裔青年可能在春节随家人上香、在家里摆一尊观音、搬家时请人看方位；但你问他是不是佛教徒，他会说不是。这中间差的不是好感，是一条路——从「我家里有这个」走到「我是这个」，需要教理入门、需要共修的社群、需要有人用他听得懂的话讲第一课。这条路汉传在北美还没有铺完。（判断）

### 澳洲：一个不说英语的移民宗教

二〇二一年人口普查：佛教徒约六十一万五千八百人，占百分之二点四，四分之三出生于海外，逾百分之八十在家说非英语（A 级，[Buddhist Council of NSW 转述 ABS 2021](https://buddhistcouncil.org/get-involved/buddhism-in-australia/)）。

这两个比例，是理解澳洲汉传的关键。它是一个移民宗教，而且首先是一个「不说英语的」移民宗教。

### 欧洲、拉美与非洲

欧洲：二〇二一年英格兰与威尔士普查显示佛教徒占百分之零点五（约二十七万三千），苏格兰二〇二二年为百分之零点三，全英约百分之零点四至零点五（A 级，ONS 2021 普查 TS030）。拉美与非洲体量极小，靠几个标志性据点撑着存在感。

**南非南华寺**的缘起需要准确记载：一九九二年三月八日，由布朗贺斯特市（Bronkhorstspruit）**市议会**签约捐地三公顷，当场增为六公顷，其后有十二、十四公顷诸说（A 级，[南华寺官网](https://www.nanhuatemple.org)）。流传说法多作「南非政府捐赠」，与史实不符。寺方的表演团体实名为「天龙队／Nan Hua Performing Arts Group」，二〇一四年依星云大师慈谕成立。

**巴西如来寺**的「如来之子教养计划」由觉诚法师二〇〇三年于圣保罗 Cotia 如来寺发起，后设如来之子基金会与光明教育中心，内容含课后辅导、道德教育、音乐、足球，以及木工、水电、面包等职业技能培训（C 级，中新社、星洲日报报道）。项目本身不大，形态却值得看一眼：它把佛教的慈悲，转成了当地社会一眼就能认出的东西——一个贫民窟少年学会一门手艺。

**东南亚与南亚**。在缅甸这样南传佛教占绝对优势的国家，汉传大乘僧伽——特别是比丘尼——通过在宗派边缘的位置重新协商自己的合法性。这是汉传与南传在同一片土地上长期共处的少数实例之一。

需一并说明的是：中日韩越之间的汉传互动，本文因语种限制未能展开。日本的黄檗宗、韩国的曹溪宗、越南的竹林禅派都是汉传法脉的分支，它们与华人汉传之间的关系，是一个重要而未及处理的题目。

> **EN对应 · Three: The Map**
>
> **A Singapore Number.** In Singapore's 2025 General Household Survey, 26.8 percent of those aged 15 to 24 declared no religious affiliation. Among those over 55, the figure was 19.4 percent (Grade A, [SingStat](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/news/general-household-survey-25)).
>
> Seven percentage points: the distance of one generation.
>
> Buddhists make up 30.9 percent of Singapore's population and the unaffiliated 23.9 percent—the latter was 20 percent in 2020 and 17 percent in 2010. Buddhism, Daoism, Christianity, and Islam all declined. The religious map of this city-state is being eroded by something more thorough than another religion: the absence of need for one.
>
> Malaysia's Buddhists are 18.7 percent of the population, about 6.1 million, overwhelmingly ethnic Chinese (Grade A, DOSM MyCensus 2020). Taiwan is different: Pew's survey of 29 July 2024 found Buddhists, the unaffiliated, and Daoists each accounting for about a quarter of adults (Grade A, [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/29/4-facts-about-religion-and-diversity-in-taiwan/))—three-way division is the island's basic condition.
>
> **The United States: Twenty-One Percent and Eleven Percent.** Pew's figures of 11 March 2026: about 4.4 million Buddhists, 1.3 percent of the population, up 22 percent over the decade; 52 percent of adult Buddhists were born in Asia (Vietnam, China, and Thailand being the three most common birthplaces), 64 percent are Asian American; 48 percent are converts—yet **55 percent of those raised Buddhist no longer identify as such**. Among Chinese Americans, 56 percent claim no religious affiliation; among Vietnamese Americans, 37 percent identify as Buddhist (Grade A, [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/11/5-facts-about-buddhists-in-the-united-states/)).
>
> One more set, which I take to be the key to Chinese Buddhism's situation abroad: among Asian Americans, 21 percent feel "close to Buddhism" for family or cultural reasons, and only 11 percent formally identify.
>
> **Nearly twice as many feel close as belong.**
>
> A young Chinese American may burn incense with the family at the new year, keep a Guanyin figure at home, and consult a feng shui master when moving house—yet asked whether he is a Buddhist, he will say no. What is missing is not goodwill but a road. Going from "my family has this" to "I am this" requires an introduction to doctrine, a community to practise with, and someone to teach the first lesson in words he understands. That road is not finished in North America. (Judgment)
>
> **Australia: An Immigrant Religion That Does Not Speak English.** The 2021 census records about 615,800 Buddhists, 2.4 percent of the population; three-quarters were born overseas and more than 80 percent speak a language other than English at home (Grade A, [Buddhist Council of NSW citing ABS 2021](https://buddhistcouncil.org/get-involved/buddhism-in-australia/)).
>
> Those two ratios are the key to Chinese Buddhism in Australia. It is an immigrant religion, and above all a non-English-speaking one.
>
> **Europe, Latin America, Africa.** Europe: the 2021 census for England and Wales records Buddhists at 0.5 percent (about 273,000); Scotland's 2022 census gives 0.3 percent, putting the UK at roughly 0.4 to 0.5 percent (Grade A, ONS 2021 Census TS030). Latin America and Africa are very small in scale, their presence carried by a handful of landmark sites.
>
> The origin of **Nan Hua Temple** in South Africa needs accurate recording: on 8 March 1992 the **Bronkhorstspruit town council** signed over three hectares, immediately increased to six, with later accounts giving twelve or fourteen (Grade A, [Nan Hua Temple](https://www.nanhuatemple.org)). The circulating version usually says "donated by the South African government," which does not match the record. The temple's performance troupe is properly the Nan Hua Performing Arts Group, established in 2014 at Hsing Yun's instruction.
>
> The **"Children of Tathāgata"** programme at **Templo Zu Lai** in Brazil was begun in 2003 by Ven. Juecheng at the temple in Cotia, São Paulo, and later established as a foundation with a learning centre. Its content includes after-school tutoring, moral education, music, football, and vocational training in carpentry, plumbing, and baking (Grade C, China News Service and Sin Chew Daily). The programme is not large, but its form is worth a look: it turned Buddhist compassion into something the surrounding society could recognise on sight—a boy from a favela learning a trade.
>
> **Southeast and South Asia.** In countries where Theravāda holds overwhelming dominance, such as Myanmar, Chinese Mahāyāna monastics—nuns in particular—renegotiate their legitimacy from a position at the margins of the sectarian order. This is one of the few instances of Chinese and Theravāda Buddhism coexisting over the long term on the same soil.
>
> It must also be said that interaction among Chinese-lineage Buddhism in China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam could not be developed here, for the language limitations noted in the Note on Sources. The Ōbaku school in Japan, the Jogye Order in Korea, and the Trúc Lâm tradition in Vietnam are all branches of the Chinese lineage; their relationship to Chinese Buddhism in Chinese-speaking communities is an important subject this report leaves untouched.

## 四、怎么走到今天的

汉传佛教的全球传播大致可分四段。前两段的分期参照既有的台湾博士论文研究，后两段为本文所补。

**移民与血缘纽带**（十九世纪中叶至二十世纪初）。汉传佛教作为华侨移民的精神寄托，以会馆、祖籍地寺院分支的形式存在，封闭、以丧葬仪式为主，与当地主流社会基本不相往来。

**现代制度化与跨国网络**（二十世纪中叶至二十世纪末）。随内战离散的僧俗把人世性质的佛教带到台港，再连接南洋与北美。此后进入以台湾教界为首、以人间佛教为旗帜的国际推展期。大学、基金会、国际组织、多语出版——汉传的组织形态在这一段里彻底改变了。

**文化／宗教外交期**（二〇〇六年以来，判断）。以大陆官方主导的世界佛教论坛为标志，汉传作为文化外交资源的角色凸显。

**数字与 AI 期**（二〇二〇年代以来，判断）。从单纯的「上网」转向知识基础设施建设，AI 成为结构性变量。这一段还在展开中，本文第三部处理。

### 五种范式

把这几十年的实践归类，大致有五种。

**人间佛教网络**，代表是佛光山与慈济，机制是现代道场加文化教育加慈善医疗，经社区服务与文化教育融入当地。

**典籍译传与律制修持**，代表是法界佛教总会与佛经翻译委员会，机制是严格戒律加英文大藏经翻译出版，靠学术译经与严谨的僧伽生活建立权威，吸引的是西方本土知识分子与深修型信众。

**高等教育与学术建制**，代表是西来大学、南天大学、法界佛教大学，经学位课程与国家教育体系认证进入主流学术界。

**公共外交与区域多边**，代表是中国佛教协会与南海佛教深圳圆桌会，经多边会议、联合人道援助、高僧互访塑造宗教外交名片。

**入世正念与禅修创新**，代表是梅村与西方禅修中心，经剥离复杂宗教仪式实现跨文化普及。

入世佛教一支需要单说。一行禅师一九八二年于法国创立梅村传承，把汉传禅宗义理与西方心理学、生态主义接在一起，发展出「互即互入」（interbeing）与「五项正念修习」（Five Mindfulness Trainings）的全球形态（A 级，[Plum Village](https://plumvillage.org/de/die-funf-achtsamkeitsubungen)）。

五种范式里，前四种的传播半径都受制于语言与人力；第五种的半径几乎无限。而它离汉传佛教本体最远。这个悖论留到第九章去说。

> **EN对应 · Four: How We Got Here**
>
> The global transmission of Chinese Buddhism falls roughly into four phases. The first two follow the periodization of an existing Taiwanese doctoral study; the latter two are added here.
>
> **Immigration and blood ties** (mid-nineteenth to early twentieth century). Chinese Buddhism served as the spiritual anchor of overseas Chinese immigrants, existing as guild-hall shrines and branch temples of ancestral places—closed, centred on funerary ritual, and largely out of contact with the surrounding society.
>
> **Modern institutionalization and transnational networks** (mid- to late twentieth century). Monastics and laypeople scattered by the civil war brought an engaged form of Buddhism to Taiwan and Hong Kong, and from there connected to Southeast Asia and North America. A period followed in which the Taiwanese Buddhist world led and Humanistic Buddhism served as the banner. Universities, foundations, international associations, multilingual publishing—the organizational form of Chinese Buddhism was utterly remade in this span.
>
> **The cultural and religious diplomacy phase** (from 2006; judgment). Marked by the mainland-led World Buddhist Forum, during which Chinese Buddhism's role as a cultural diplomacy resource came to the fore.
>
> **The digital and AI phase** (from the 2020s; judgment). A shift from simply "going online" toward building knowledge infrastructure, with AI as a structural variable. This phase is still unfolding and is taken up in Part Three.
>
> **Five Paradigms.** Sorted, the practice of recent decades falls into five.
>
> The **Humanistic Buddhist network**, represented by Foguang Shan and Tzu Chi: modern temples plus cultural education plus charitable medicine, entering local society through community service and cultural programmes.
>
> **Textual translation and disciplined practice**, represented by the Dharma Realm Buddhist Association and its translation society: strict precepts plus English translation of the canon, authority built on scholarly translation and rigorous monastic life, attracting Western intellectuals and serious practitioners.
>
> **Higher education and academic institution**, represented by the University of the West, Nan Tien Institute, and Dharma Realm Buddhist University: degree programmes and national accreditation bringing Buddhism into mainstream academia.
>
> **Public diplomacy and regional multilateralism**, represented by the Buddhist Association of China and the South China Sea Buddhist Roundtable: multilateral conferences, joint humanitarian relief, and exchanges of senior monastics building a religious-diplomacy calling card.
>
> **Engaged mindfulness and contemplative innovation**, represented by Plum Village and Western meditation centres: cross-cultural reach achieved by stripping away complex religious ritual.
>
> The Engaged Buddhism strand deserves separate mention. Thich Nhat Hanh founded the Plum Village tradition in France in 1982, joining Chinese Chan doctrine to Western psychology and ecology and developing the global form of "interbeing" and the Five Mindfulness Trainings (Grade A, [Plum Village](https://plumvillage.org/de/die-funf-achtsamkeitsubungen)).
>
> Among the five, the first four have a reach limited by language and personnel. The fifth has almost no limit at all—and stands furthest from the body of Chinese Buddhism. That paradox is taken up in Chapter Nine.

## 五、母体收缩：亲近与归属之间

皮尤二〇二六年三月十一日的《东亚佛教的近况》给了两个数字：日本与韩国「自幼佛教徒」中，分别有百分之四十与百分之四十二现已无宗教归属（A 级，[Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2026/03/11/buddhisms-recent-decline-in-east-asia/)）。两国年轻人去寺庙、拜佛的比例，明显低于长辈。

而在韩国、日本、台湾，无宗教者中至少三分之一对佛教「生活方式」怀有个人认同。

这两组数字放在一起，勾出一个共同的形状：**亲近感还在，归属在流失。**

汉传面临的主要风险，不是被别的宗教取代，是被世俗生活方式吸收。一个年轻人可以喜欢寺院的安静、认同素食、用「随缘」作口头禅、失眠时听诵经音频，却从不认为自己是佛教徒，也从不考虑皈依。他不反对。他只是没有那个必要。

于是问题落在几个具体得不能再具体的机制上：教理入门有没有？共修社群有没有？家庭传承断在哪里？这三样，恰好是汉传在海外的三处空缺。一场法会可以容纳一千人，但从「来过一次」到「每周来」的那道门槛，通常没人扶。

## 六、治理公信

这一节谈制度，不谈案情。

近十年有两起事件对汉传公信力的冲击最大：二〇一八年的学诚事件，与二〇二五至二〇二六年的释永信案。两案的经过、时间、金额与判决，本文在附录二中如实记录；正文只取一个共同的结构性事实——**在两起事件中，问题的暴露都不是由教内监督机制完成的。**

学诚案的起点是两名前僧人的举报材料；释永信的相关指控早在二〇一五年就已由内部人士公开。也就是说，在长达数年的时间里，教内的监督机制未能运转，最终是由外部力量——举报外流、官方调查、司法程序——推动了处理。这不是两起孤立的道德事故，是一个制度信号。（判断）

这类事件对宗教组织的杀伤力，比外部观察者预想的更大，原因是它同时击中三重合法性：**戒律**（教内承认的）、**财务透明**（现代公益承认的）、**官方背书**（政治承认的）。平常三重合法性互相支撑；一出事则同时受损，且互相拖累。信众想到的是「连戒都守不住」，社会想到的是「钱去哪了」，官方想到的是「我给你背书，你却出事」。

对海外传播而言，成本是具体的。一处海外道场要进入当地社会，需要的是信任；信任的建立需要二十年，摧毁只需要一次头条。而海外华人社群的信息传播网络，恰恰比国内更依赖口耳相传与社群媒体——**坏消息在海外传得更快，不是更慢。**

应当承认，制度补课在教内并非没有讨论。二〇二〇年十二月的中国佛教协会第十次全国代表会议通过了章程修订，明确「抵制商业化不良影响」；二〇二五年十二月佛协召开教育活动推进会，强调严肃处理违规犯戒行为（C 级，[中新网](http://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2020/12-02/9352528.shtml)）。方向是清楚的。难点在落地：财务审计披露、独立申诉渠道、住持任期与交棒机制、董事会或多住持制——这些在现代非营利组织里是常规，在汉传丛林制度里却没有现成的对应物。

这也就意味着，汉传要补的这一课，答案在自家的戒律里：**把羯磨、揭露、僧团议事这些丛林制度里本来就有的东西，在现代组织条件下重新长出来**，比学西方更根本。（判断）羯磨法本是一套完整的决策与处置程序：什么事需要白一羯磨，什么事需要白四羯磨，犯戒到什么程度当如何处置，皆有明文。这套程序比许多现代组织的议事规则更精密；问题只在它长期未被用于财务与人事这类新问题上。

## 七、政治耦合与跨境信任成本

宗教组织的跨国可信度，很大程度上取决于它看起来有多「非国家」。这是台湾系教团在海外的结构性优势，也是大陆体系需要弥补的短板。（判断）

二〇二六年有学术研究比较了中印两国对佛教外交的运用，认为两国在南亚与东南亚的佛教外交不只是文化交流，还关乎区域秩序与影响力竞争（B 级，Raj 等，*Frontiers in Political Science* 8, 2026）。同期另有分析指出：二〇二五年五至六月印度向越南出借佛陀圣物，吸引近一千五百万信众；中国则把佛教外交编入「一带一路」，并提出「南海佛教」作为共享遗产；分析者并称中国因达赖喇嘛问题缺席了二〇一一年与二〇二三年印度主办的全球佛教活动。这些属分析者观点，本文标为 B 级学术来源与分析性判断，不作事实陈述。

台湾系教团虽是民间身份，也难免政治化。美联社曾报道星云大师与政界联系密切、倡导两岸交流，因此被部分人视为统一倡导者；而他本人与各党派均保持关系。

地缘现实绕不开，这是必须承认的。但这里有一个常被忽略的区分：**佛教的跨宗教、跨传统合作，与佛教作为国家外交工具，是两件事，而它们在国际场合经常被混为一谈——这种混淆对双方都不利。**（判断）对大陆体系而言，混淆会持续抬高其海外信任成本；对台湾系教团而言，被误读为工具，会损耗其数十年积累的民间信誉。

## 八、语言、人才与本土化

这是四件事里最实在的一件。

**教内的一手调研。** 一位在佛光山澳洲、美国道场服务近二十年的法师，曾问卷调查佛光山美国各分院的出家众，发现：西方人士接受度近五年有所增长；中华文化活动与禅修是吸引本地人入寺的最佳方便；弘法人员最缺外语能力；中西文化差异是本土化的最大困难之一。这是小样本自陈数据，不可外推，但方向与其它材料一致。

**二代问题。** 亚裔美国佛教徒中美国出生者仅百分之二十，低于亚裔整体的百分之三十二；百分之二十一的亚裔佛教徒选择用非英语问卷作答。Chenxing Han 访谈八十位年轻亚裔美国佛教徒后指出，「东方和尚」「迷信的移民」等刻板印象使这一群体被边缘化（B 级，*Be the Refuge*）。

**创始人时代的终结。** 圣严二〇〇九年、惟觉二〇一六年、星云二〇二三年相继圆寂，证严已近九十岁。未来十年的关键问题是：组织能否脱离个人魅力持续运转。（判断）

二〇一七年两会上，星云大师说过一段话，被视为汉传本土化最直接的依据：印度僧人来华多从事翻译，寺院建设交给中国僧人；若不融入当地文化、培养本地弟子，便没有今日的中国佛教。佛光山的本土化框架包括尊重包容、经典翻译、宣教人员本土化、伦理理念本土化、内容与方式多样化。

这段话的逻辑是：**汉传自己就是本土化的产物，所以没有理由认为它在别处不能本土化。** 这个论证很有力，但它有一个前提常被忽略——汉传当年之所以能本土化，是因为有鸠摩罗什、真谛、玄奘、义净这样一批人，用几百年的时间译经、注疏、建立教学体系。翻译、本地弟子、社会支持，三样缺一不可。当代海外传播恰恰缺前两样。

海外道场外语人才的短缺，不是「多招几个会英语的」所能解决。它要求的是一个能持续产出高水平双语僧才的教育体系——而这个体系目前只在零星的地方存在（见第二十八章关于学修次第的讨论）。

## 九、产品错位：Zen 品牌的遮蔽

法界佛教总会的一篇文章曾指出：铃木大拙的著作深刻影响了西方知识界，但也使人们把佛教主要理解为坐禅，少涉教义、仪式、戒律与道德。这种印象在美国至今存在。

我以为这个观察比它表面看起来更重要。汉传的强项——净土念佛、忏仪法会、丛林与戒律、人间佛教的社会服务——与西方「佛教现代主义」（以冥想、心理、伦理为主）的接口存在错位；而实际的入口往往是「中华文化活动加禅修」。（判断）

这一错位的深层机制值得说清楚，因为它决定了后面所有的策略。

西方现代佛教的形成，本身就是一次筛选。它从亚洲各传统中挑选了那些与启蒙理性、个人主义、去仪式化相兼容的部分——冥想（可私人实践）、心理（可科学化）、伦理（可世俗化）——并搁置了那些不相容的部分：仪式、戒律、僧团、轮回、功德。

**被挑中的那些，恰恰是汉传最不以其立身的；被搁置的那些，恰恰是汉传保存得最完整的。**（判断）

结果是三家的位置各自清楚：藏传因有一套完整可讲解的学修次第（五部大论）而进入西方精英学术与高端修行市场；南传因有一套清晰可操作的禅修技术（内观）而占据了西方大众禅修市场；汉传手里最成熟的是「完整形态」——戒定慧一体、解行并重、僧俗共住——而完整形态在西方恰恰是最难出售的产品。

于是汉传在西方处在一个尴尬位置：论个人冥想，它不如南传直接；论学术体系，它不如藏传成建制；论社会参与，它做得极好，却常被归类为「慈善组织」而非「佛教传统」。

**错位不是产品不好，是接口不对。** 而接口是可以改的——这正是第二十六章与第三十章要谈的。

> **EN对应 · Part Two: Nine Places**
>
> *This part concerns illness. The point of discussing illness is to treat it, so each section aims to locate the malady in a specific mechanism rather than stopping at the word "problem."*
>
> **Five: A Contracting Mother Body — Between Closeness and Belonging.** Pew's report of 11 March 2026 gives two figures: 40 percent of those raised Buddhist in Japan and 42 percent in South Korea no longer claim any religion (Grade A, [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2026/03/11/buddhisms-recent-decline-in-east-asia/)). Young people in both countries visit temples and worship far less than their elders did.
>
> And yet, in Korea, Japan, and Taiwan, at least a third of the religiously unaffiliated express a personal affinity for Buddhism "as a way of life."
>
> Together the two produce a single shape: **the closeness remains; the belonging drains away.**
>
> The chief risk facing Chinese Buddhism is not replacement by another religion. It is absorption by a secular way of life. A young person may like the quiet of a temple, approve of vegetarianism, use "whatever comes, let it come" as a verbal tic, listen to sutra chanting when unable to sleep—and never once consider himself a Buddhist, never once think of taking refuge. He is not opposed. He simply has no occasion to be anything else.
>
> So the problem lands on mechanisms as concrete as they come: Is there an introduction to doctrine? Is there a community to practise with? Where did the family transmission break? These three are precisely the three gaps overseas. A dharma assembly can hold a thousand people, but the threshold between "came once" and "comes every week" is usually unattended.
>
> **Six: Governance Credibility.** This section concerns institutions, not cases.
>
> Two events in the past decade have done the most damage to Chinese Buddhist credibility: the Xue Cheng affair of 2018, and the Shi Yongxin case of 2025–2026. Their course, dates, amounts, and judgments are recorded faithfully in Appendix Two; the main text takes from them only one shared structural fact: **in neither case was the problem brought to light by an internal oversight mechanism.**
>
> The Xue Cheng affair began with a report filed by two former monastics; the allegations against Shi Yongxin had been made public by insiders as early as 2015. Over a period of years the internal oversight mechanism did not function, and the handling was ultimately driven by outside forces—leaked reports, official investigation, judicial process. These are not two isolated moral accidents. They are one institutional signal. (Judgment)
>
> Such events wound a religious organization more deeply than outside observers expect, because they strike three legitimacies at once: **precepts** (recognized within the religion), **financial transparency** (recognized by modern philanthropy), and **official endorsement** (recognized politically). Ordinarily the three support one another; in a scandal they are damaged together and drag each other down. The faithful think, "he could not even keep the precepts." Society thinks, "where did the money go?" Officialdom thinks, "I endorsed you and you failed."
>
> For overseas transmission the cost is concrete. An overseas temple seeking entry into local society needs trust; trust takes twenty years to build and one headline to destroy. And information in overseas Chinese communities travels more through word of mouth and social media than it does at home—**bad news travels faster abroad, not slower.**
>
> It should be acknowledged that institutional remedies are under discussion. The Buddhist Association of China's Tenth National Congress in December 2020 adopted revised statutes explicitly opposing "the harmful effects of commercialization"; in December 2025 the Association convened a meeting on education to stress serious handling of violations (Grade C, [China News Service](http://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2020/12-02/9352528.shtml)). The direction is clear. The difficulty is implementation: audited financial disclosure, independent grievance channels, term limits and succession mechanisms for abbots, boards or multi-abbot arrangements. These are routine in modern non-profits and have no ready counterpart in the Chinese monastic system.
>
> Which means what Chinese Buddhism needs to add is not "what the West does." It is to **regrow, under modern organizational conditions, what the monastic system already contained—karma-vācā, disclosure before the assembly, and communal deliberation.** (Judgment) The karma-vācā procedures are themselves a complete decision-making and case-handling process: what requires a single motion, what requires a motion put four times, how a given degree of offense is handled—all set down in writing. These procedures are more refined than the deliberative rules of many modern organizations. The only problem is that they were long not applied to new problems such as finance and personnel.
>
> **Seven: Political Entanglement and the Cost of Cross-Border Trust.** The transnational credibility of a religious organization depends heavily on how non-state it appears to be. This is the structural advantage Taiwanese orders enjoy overseas, and the shortfall the mainland system must make up. (Judgment)
>
> A 2026 academic study comparing how India and China use Buddhist diplomacy argues that such diplomacy in South and Southeast Asia is not merely cultural exchange but concerns regional order and competition for influence (Grade B, Raj et al., *Frontiers in Political Science* 8, 2026). Analysis from the same period notes that India lent relics of the Buddha to Vietnam in May–June 2025, drawing nearly 15 million devotees, while China incorporated Buddhist diplomacy into the Belt and Road and proposed "South China Sea Buddhism" as shared heritage; the analysis further states that China was absent from globally oriented Buddhist events hosted by India in 2011 and 2023 because of the Dalai Lama question. These are the analysts' arguments, and this report marks them as Grade B academic sources and analytical judgments rather than statements of fact.
>
> Taiwanese orders, though civilian in status, are not immune to politicization. The Associated Press has reported that Master Hsing Yun maintained close ties with political figures and advocated cross-strait exchange, leading some to regard him as a pro-unification advocate; he himself kept relations with all parties.
>
> The geopolitical reality cannot be avoided, and that must be acknowledged. But one distinction is regularly overlooked: **interreligious and inter-traditional cooperation among Buddhists, and Buddhism as an instrument of state diplomacy, are two different things—and in international settings they are constantly conflated, to the detriment of both.** (Judgment) For the mainland system, the conflation keeps raising the cost of overseas trust. For Taiwanese orders, being misread as an instrument erodes civilian credibility built over decades.
>
> **Eight: Language, Personnel, and Localization.** This is the most concrete of the four.
>
> **First-hand research from within the order.** A monastic who served nearly twenty years in Foguang Shan temples in Australia and the United States surveyed ordained members of Foguang Shan's American branch temples and found that Western acceptance had grown over the previous five years; that Chinese cultural activities and meditation were the most effective means of drawing local people into temples; that the greatest shortfall among those who teach the dharma was foreign-language ability; and that East–West cultural difference was one of the chief obstacles to localization. This is small-sample self-reported data and cannot be generalized, but it points in the same direction as other materials.
>
> **The second-generation problem.** Only 20 percent of Asian American Buddhists were born in the United States, against 32 percent of Asian Americans as a whole, and 21 percent of Asian American Buddhists chose to answer questionnaires in a language other than English. Chenxing Han, after interviewing eighty young Asian American Buddhists, reports that stereotypes—"the oriental monk," "the superstitious immigrant"—push this group to the margins (Grade B, *Be the Refuge*).
>
> **The end of the founder era.** Sheng Yen died in 2009, Weijue in 2016, Hsing Yun in 2023, and Cheng Yen is nearly ninety. The key question for the coming decade is whether these organizations can run without the personal charisma of a founder. (Judgment)
>
> At the 2017 sessions, Master Hsing Yun made an argument often cited as the most direct warrant for localization: Indian monks who came to China mostly did translation work, and the building of temples was left to Chinese monks; had they not merged into the local culture and trained local disciples, there would be no Chinese Buddhism today. Foguang Shan's localization framework includes respect and tolerance, canonical translation, localization of teaching personnel, localization of ethical concepts, and diversification of content and method.
>
> The logic runs thus: **Chinese Buddhism is itself the product of localization, so there is no reason to think it cannot localize elsewhere.** The argument is strong, but it rests on a premise often overlooked. Chinese Buddhism localized because it had Kumārajīva, Paramārtha, Xuanzang, and Yijing—men who spent centuries translating, annotating, and building teaching systems. Translation, local disciples, and social support: all three were necessary. Contemporary overseas transmission lacks the first two.
>
> The shortage of foreign-language talent in overseas temples is not solved by "hiring a few more English speakers." It requires an educational system capable of producing high-level bilingual monastics—and at present that system exists only in isolated places (see the discussion of study sequences in Chapter Twenty-Seven).
>
> **Nine: Product Mismatch — How the Zen Brand Obscures.** An article from the Dharma Realm Buddhist Association made this point: D.T. Suzuki's writings deeply influenced the Western intelligentsia, but they also led people to understand Buddhism primarily as seated meditation, with little attention to doctrine, ritual, precepts, or ethics. The impression persists in America today.
>
> I take that observation to be more important than it first appears. The strengths of Chinese Buddhism—Pure Land recitation, repentance liturgies, monastic discipline, the social service of Humanistic Buddhism—mismatch the interface of Western "Buddhist modernism," which centres on meditation, psychology, and ethics; the actual point of entry tends to be "Chinese cultural activity plus meditation." (Judgment)
>
> The deeper mechanism of the mismatch deserves stating, because it determines everything that follows.
>
> The formation of Western modern Buddhism was itself a process of selection. It chose from Asian traditions those elements compatible with Enlightenment rationality, individualism, and de-ritualization—meditation (privately practicable), psychology (scientizable), ethics (secularizable)—and set aside those that were not: ritual, precepts, monasticism, rebirth, merit.
>
> **What was selected is precisely what Chinese Buddhism least rests on. What was set aside is precisely what Chinese Buddhism holds most completely.** (Judgment)
>
> The result is that each tradition's position abroad is clear. Tibetan Buddhism entered Western elite academia and the high-end practice market because it has a fully articulated study sequence (the five great treatises). Theravāda captured the Western mass meditation market because it has a clear, operable technique (vipassanā). What Chinese Buddhism holds in its most mature form is the "complete shape"—precepts, concentration, and wisdom as one; study and practice together; monastics and laity cohabiting—and the complete shape is the hardest product to sell in the West.
>
> Hence an awkward position: in personal meditation, Chinese Buddhism is less direct than Theravāda; in scholastic system, less institutional than Tibetan; in social engagement, it excels, yet is often classified as a charitable organization rather than a Buddhist tradition.
>
> **The mismatch is not that the product is poor. It is that the interface is wrong.** And interfaces can be changed—which is what Chapters Twenty-Five and Twenty-Nine take up.

## 十、典籍与概念的可及性赤字

BDK 的完成度约百分之六十五（见第二章），而且不是汉传教团主导。汉传教团自己的补位——佛经翻译委员会与佛光山术语库——都是局部工程。CBETA 收录的四千八百九十九部典籍，绝大多数没有英译；有英译的，多数出自非汉传机构。

这里有一个必须承认的事实：**至今不存在一份权威、公开、可审计的「汉文佛典英译覆盖率」统计。** 没有这把尺子，翻译规划就无法排序——先译什么、后译什么、哪些已有多个译本、哪些急需重译，全都只能凭感觉。

可及性赤字还有一层更容易被忽略：**概念的可及性。** 汉传的核心概念——如来藏、真如、无住生心、圆融三谛、事事无碍——深度植根于中国传统的阴阳、玄学与儒家语境之中。把它们译成西方语言，遇到的问题不只是找不到对应的词，而是**找不到对应的思想框架**。目前全球范围内没有一套被广泛公认的汉传术语英译体系，许多翻译借用西方哲学或天主教神学术语，造成义理失真。

这两层赤字合起来，构成了汉传海外传播最深的一道坎：**典籍译不出来，概念说不清楚，于是「汉传佛教究竟是什么」这个问题，在海外长期没有令人满意的答案。**（判断）

而那个答案空缺的位置，恰好被第九章所说的「Zen 品牌」填上了——一个简化的、可消费的、去教团化的替代答案。

## 十一、商业化与精神稀释

大陆寺庙商业化的问题，媒体讨论已久：门票过高、香火价格虚高、纪念品过度销售，以及由此引出的「佛教精神是否被腐蚀」的质疑。政府已出台限价、禁止强制捐赠等措施。

少林寺是一个极端样本。它把禅武文化做成了国际品牌，代价是「CEO 和尚」这样的标签，以及第二章所述澳洲购地案引发的持续争议。少林模式的教训不在于「不该做文化输出」，而在于：**当一个宗教品牌的价值主要由其商业估值定义时，它就失去了对自己叙事的控制权。**

也要看到另一面。青年「寺庙热」是真实的：携程数据显示二〇二三年二月寺庙订票同比增百分之三百一十，订票者近半为九〇后、〇〇后（C 级，[证券时报](https://stcn.com/article/detail/826023.html)；该数字的统计窗口为「今年以来」，非单月）。《环球时报》的采访发现动机混杂，并非出于信仰。二〇二五年底的观察也认为，佛教在中国更多呈现为生活方式与身心疗愈，而非宗教。目前没有证据显示这波热潮已转化为身份认同（CGSS 二〇二一年仍为百分之四）。（判断）

**这波热潮是资产还是负债，取决于有没有接得住的人。** 一个年轻人因为拍照好看走进寺院，如果他走出来时只带走一张照片，那是一次消费；如果他在门口被人递上一本讲得明白的小册子，或者被告知每周三晚上有一个给初学者的共修，那可能是一次转折。**目前绝大多数寺院，在这道门口是空的。**（判断）

## 十二、统计盲区

这一章很短，因为它要说的只有一句：**我们说不清自己的家底。**

前面已经出现过一次：全球汉传信众没有数字。这里再加上几条同类型的空缺——马来西亚、印尼、泰国、越南等地汉传社群的分口径数据没有；欧洲佛教徒中汉传所占比例没有；海外汉传僧人总数没有。

同一组织的数字口径相差悬殊：佛光山「两百多所／三百余所道场，四十国／一百多国」，慈济「志工六十八国／援助一百三十六国／一百零三国」。「覆盖 X 国」往往把道场、联络处、活动与援助对象混在一起。

这不只是「数字不好看」的问题，而是一项**能力缺陷**。原因有三层。

其一，**对象本身难界定**。什么算一座汉传道场？共用一个殿堂的联络处算不算？居士林的共修点算不算？以禅修为主的中心算不算？界定不同，数字可以差数倍。

其二，**主体缺位**。没有一个跨教团、跨地区的机构负责这件事。各教团自报口径，互不通约，也不对外审计。

其三，**意愿不足**。数字大一点在募款与传播上有好处；这个动机普遍存在，而它必然侵蚀数据的严肃性。

**一项事业如果无法度量自己，就无法为自己排序。**（判断）翻译、教育、人才培养，哪一样都优先于它，但哪一样都缺它不可。

## 十三、宗派间话语的不对称

全球佛教的对外格局中，藏传与南传各占了一个位置，汉传没有。

藏传凭藉五部大论的格鲁派学修体系、显密双修的完整次第，以及达赖喇嘛等领袖的国际知名度，在西方知识界与精英阶层中确立了高端学术形象。南传凭藉巴利三藏的明晰性与内观禅修的推广，占据了西方大众禅修的主流市场。

汉传拥有天台、华严、唯识、禅、净土等完整的宗派教理体系，在西方却常被理解为「缺乏系统逻辑教理，偏重感性祈福与迷信仪式」。这个印象的成因，第九章已作分析。此处补充一条更少被提及的：**汉传自己在对外介绍时，很少把义理体系讲清楚。**

一个可以对照的细节：藏传在西方传播时，格西学位、五部大论、辩经制度这些制度性的东西被反复介绍，构成了可辨识的学术形象。汉传介绍自己时，讲得最多的是「禅」与「人间佛教」——前者易被简化为冥想，后者易被简化为慈善。**天台的一心三观、华严的四法界、唯识的八识，在中文世界的介绍材料里连篇累牍，在英文世界里却几乎无人讲述。**（判断）

这不是「别人不识货」的问题。一个传统若不能用自己的语言之外的语言把义理讲清楚，它在国际学术与宗教对话场合就没有话语权。话语权从来不是分配的，是挣来的。

> **EN对应 · Ten to Thirteen**
>
> **Ten: The Deficit in Access to Texts and Concepts.** BDK stands at about 65 percent completion (see Chapter Two), and it is not led by a Chinese Buddhist order. Chinese Buddhist orders' own efforts—the Buddhist Text Translation Society and Foguang Shan's terminology system—are local projects. Of CBETA's 4,899 titles, the overwhelming majority have no English translation; of those that do, most were produced by non-Chinese Buddhist institutions.
>
> One fact must be acknowledged: **there exists no authoritative, public, auditable statistic for the English translation coverage of the Chinese Buddhist canon.** Without that ruler, translation planning cannot be prioritized—what to translate first, what later, which texts already have multiple versions, which urgently need retranslation—all of it can only be guessed at.
>
> The access deficit has a second layer that is more easily overlooked: **access to concepts.** The core concepts of Chinese Buddhism—tathāgatagarbha, suchness, the non-abiding mind, the threefold truth in perfect fusion, the unobstructed interpenetration of phenomena—are deeply rooted in Chinese yin-yang cosmology, Dark Learning, and Confucian contexts. Translating them into Western languages encounters not merely a shortage of corresponding words but **a shortage of corresponding intellectual frameworks.** No widely recognized system for rendering Chinese Buddhist terminology into English exists; many translations borrow from Western philosophy or Catholic theology, distorting the doctrine.
>
> Together, the two layers form the deepest ditch in the path of overseas transmission: **the texts cannot be translated, the concepts cannot be explained, and so the question "what is Chinese Buddhism, actually?" has long gone without a satisfactory answer abroad.** (Judgment)
>
> And the vacancy left by that missing answer has been filled by precisely what Chapter Nine described—the Zen brand: a simplified, consumable, de-institutionalized substitute.
>
> **Eleven: Commercialization and the Dilution of Substance.** The commercialization of mainland temples has been discussed in the press for years: inflated admission fees, overpriced incense, aggressive souvenir sales, and the accompanying question of whether Buddhist substance is being corroded. The government has introduced price caps and bans on forced donations.
>
> Shaolin Temple is an extreme case. It turned Chan martial culture into an international brand, at the cost of labels such as "CEO monk" and the continuing controversy over the Australian land purchase described in Chapter Two. The lesson of the Shaolin model is not that cultural export is wrong. It is that **when a religious brand's value comes to be defined chiefly by its commercial valuation, it loses control of its own narrative.**
>
> The other side should be seen as well. The youth "temple boom" is real: Ctrip data show temple ticket bookings up 310 percent year on year in February 2023, with nearly half of purchasers born in the 1990s or 2000s (Grade C, [Securities Times](https://stcn.com/article/detail/826023.html); note the statistical window is "since the start of the year," not a single month). Interviews by the *Global Times* found mixed motives, not faith. Observations at the end of 2025 likewise concluded that Buddhism in China appears more as a lifestyle and a form of healing than as a religion. No evidence yet shows the boom converting into identity (CGSS 2021 remains at 4 percent). (Judgment)
>
> **Whether the boom is an asset or a liability depends on whether anyone is there to catch it.** If a young person walks into a temple because it photographs well and walks out with only a photograph, that was consumption. If someone hands her a clearly written pamphlet at the door, or tells her there is a Wednesday evening gathering for beginners, it might be a turning point. **At present, at most temples, that door is unattended.** (Judgment)
>
> **Twelve: The Statistical Blind Spot.** This chapter is short because it has only one thing to say: **we cannot accurately state what we hold.**
>
> It has appeared once already: there is no global figure for Chinese Buddhist adherents. To that we add more of the same kind—no disaggregated data for Chinese Buddhist communities in Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, or Vietnam; no proportion for Chinese Buddhism within the European Buddhist population; no total for overseas Chinese Buddhist monastics.
>
> Within a single organization, figures vary widely: Foguang Shan at "200-plus / over 300 temples, 40 countries / over 100 countries," Tzu Chi at "volunteers in 68 countries / relief reach 136 / 103." "Present in N countries" routinely blends temples, liaison offices, events, and relief recipients into one number.
>
> This is not merely a matter of unflattering numbers. It is a **capacity deficit**, for three reasons.
>
> First, **the object of measurement is hard to define.** What counts as a Chinese Buddhist temple? A liaison office sharing a hall? A lay practice group? A meditation centre with no monastic? Change the definition and the figure can shift severalfold.
>
> Second, **there is no measuring body.** No cross-order, cross-regional institution is charged with the task. Orders report by their own definitions, the definitions are not commensurable, and nothing is externally audited.
>
> Third, **the will to measure is weak.** Larger numbers help with fundraising and publicity. That motive is widespread, and it necessarily corrodes the seriousness of the data.
>
> **An enterprise that cannot measure itself cannot prioritize itself.** (Judgment) Translation, education, and the training of personnel all take precedence over this—and none of them can proceed without it.
>
> **Thirteen: The Asymmetry of Discursive Power among Traditions.** Tibetan and Theravāda Buddhism each occupy a position in the global Buddhist landscape. Chinese Buddhism occupies none.
>
> Tibetan Buddhism secured a high-end academic image among Western intellectuals and elites through the Gelug study system of the five great treatises, the complete sequence of sutra and tantra, and the international prominence of figures such as the Dalai Lama. Theravāda captured the mainstream Western meditation market through the clarity of the Pali canon and the spread of vipassanā.
>
> Chinese Buddhism possesses complete scholastic systems in Tiantai, Huayan, Yogācāra, Chan, and Pure Land, yet abroad is commonly understood as "lacking systematic doctrinal logic, leaning toward emotional prayer and superstitious ritual." Chapter Nine analysed the causes. One further cause is less often mentioned: **Chinese Buddhism rarely explains its doctrinal systems clearly when presenting itself abroad.**
>
> A detail for comparison: when Tibetan Buddhism spread in the West, the institutional features—the geshe degree, the five great treatises, the debate curriculum—were repeatedly introduced, forming a recognizable academic image. When Chinese Buddhism introduces itself, what gets told most is "Chan" and "Humanistic Buddhism"—the first easily reduced to meditation, the second to charity. **Tiantai's one mind and three contemplations, Huayan's four dharma-realms, and Yogācāra's eight consciousnesses fill volumes of Chinese-language introductory material and are almost nowhere narrated in English.** (Judgment)
>
> This is not a case of others failing to appreciate the goods. A tradition that cannot explain its doctrine in a language other than its own has no voice in international scholarship or interreligious dialogue. A voice is never allocated. It is earned.

# 第三部　变量：新技术与 AI

> 这一部只问一件事：**AI 改变了什么**。具体说，它改变了汉传做哪些事的成本，改变了谁有资格做，改变了做出来给谁看。凡不能落到这三样上的技术描述，一概略过。

## 十四、从数字化到知识工程化

二〇二六年二月二十八日，佛教数字资源中心（BDRC）公告了一项计划：把经过人工转录、OCR、多版本比较与交叉验证的佛教文献，建成适用于 AI 训练与研究的开放语料。项目二〇二五年十二月启动，Khyentse 基金会资助，规模以「数百万页」计。公告后两个月的进度：Gold Standard 语料从一点九 GB 增至三点四 GB，两千六百万余幅图像完成 OCR，约三万五千幅图与转写完成对齐（A 级，[BDRC 公告](https://www.bdrc.io/blog/2026/02/28/)）。

这件事的重要性不在数字，在于它标的是一条路线的选择：**先建数据，再做智能**，而不是先做一个会说话的机器人。

佛教与数字技术的关系，此前已经走过两个阶段。第一阶段是**数字保存**：把纸质经典、碑刻、寺志、图像、音视频转成数字资源，目标是「不丢失」。第二阶段是**数字检索与传播**：让人能在线搜索、阅读、观看、听闻、参与，目标从「不丢失」变成「能取到」。第三阶段正在到来，即**计算性佛学与智能化知识系统**：机器不仅存储文本，而且可以跨文本、跨版本、跨语言、跨人物、跨时空建立关系，帮助研究者提出过去很难提出的问题。目标再从「能取到」变成「能被机器理解和推理」。

实证基础已经比较明确。CBETA 长期采用 TEI/XML 结构化编码，使经典不仅可读，也可被机器处理、引用、分析。东京大学的 SAT 项目把《大正新修大藏经》数字文本与原始页面定位结合，二〇一二年版本起提供原书页面影像，可定位到行（A 级，[SAT](https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/)）。法鼓文理学院的 DILA 综合检索系统整合十多个佛教数字档案，公开检索量超过一亿六千万字符（A 级·【自述】，[DILA iSearch](https://isearch.dila.edu.tw/)）。BDRC 的 BUDA 平台公开提供超过一千七百万页佛教资料（A 级·【自述】〔[bdrc.io](https://www.bdrc.io/)〕；该机构二〇二三年另有「五万九千余册、三千万幅图像」的口径）；二〇二五年三月十四日发布藏文 OCR 桌面工具，免费开源，可批量处理图片与整册 PDF。

把这些并列，可以看到目标已经变了：从**「把佛典放到网上」**，变成**「把佛教文明建成可验证、可关联、可计算、可引用、可翻译、可供 AI 可靠使用的知识体系。」**

对汉传而言，这个转变有一层特殊意义。**知识基础设施是少数几件不依赖「传教」就能做的事。** 它不需要对方先接受你的信仰，不需要跨过身份与政治的关口，也不需要说服任何人「我是好的」。它只需要把典籍整理好、把术语定清楚、把出处标明白——做完了，全世界的学者、译者、开发者、学习者都会用，用的时候自然会知道这是谁做的。（判断）

反过来也成立：**在这件事上落后，不是少了一个宣传渠道，是在别人构建未来佛学知识秩序的时候缺席。** 十年之后，如果全球佛学 AI 的语料、术语、知识图谱、评测基准都已定型，而其中汉传的部分是稀薄甚至错误的，那么后来者能做的只有适配，而不是定义。

## 十五、八层基础设施

一套理想的佛教知识系统，至少要有八层。

| 层级 | 核心内容 | 相关技术 |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1. 原始资料层 | 大藏经、敦煌写本、碑刻、语录、灯录、寺志、地方志、报刊 | 扫描、OCR/HTR、图像识别 |
| 2. 权威文本层 | 不同版本、校勘本、异文、断句、标点 | TEI/XML、版本控制 |
| 3. 元数据层 | 经名、作者、译者、年代、卷次、出处、版本、版权 | Linked Data、标准元数据 |
| 4. 术语层 | 梵／巴利／藏／汉／英术语及异译 | 术语库、本体 |
| 5. 关系层 | 人物、法脉、寺院、文献、概念、历史事件之间的关系 | 知识图谱 |
| 6. 语义层 | 段落、主题、概念、引文、相似文、跨语种对应 | 向量嵌入、语义检索 |
| 7. AI 层 | RAG、LLM、翻译、分类、摘要、问答、智能体 | 大模型、视觉语言模型、Agent |
| 8. 验证层 | 原典出处、版本、页码、证据链、专家修订记录 | 溯源、审计日志 |

CBETA 的 TEI/XML 实践已经证明佛典完全可以成为标准化的数字人文对象；SAT 则进一步强调「数字文本必须能够回到原始页面核验」——这是未来佛教 AI 可信性的基本原则。

由此可以推出一个与流行看法相反的结论：**佛教 AI 的核心竞争力，首先不在「模型有多大」，而在底层语料是否权威、版本是否清楚、出处是否可追溯、术语是否规范、不同传统是否被正确区分。**（判断）

这个判断有一个直接的推论，值得单独说：**训练一个佛教大模型，对汉传而言几乎肯定不是最优投入。** 通用大模型的能力在快速提升，自建模型的门槛与折旧速度都在恶化；而权威语料、规范术语、清晰版本、可追溯出处这些东西，一旦建成，其价值不随模型换代而贬值，反而随每一次模型升级而放大。**把钱花在语料上，比花在参数上更划算。**（判断）

## 十六、AI 辅助佛学研究：放大，而不是替代

AI 在佛学研究上真正有价值的地方，不是替学者思考，是把他从「做不完」里解放出来。

**从关键词检索到语义检索。** 传统检索依赖「你得先知道关键词」。研究者想知道唐宋时期关于「无念」的论述，就必须先想到「无念」这个词，还得想到它的异写、别译、别称。想不全，就搜不全。

语义检索改变的是这一步。研究者可以直接提出：找出唐宋时期关于「无念」概念的论述，并区分禅宗、天台、华严、净土传统。系统先做语义召回，再按年代、宗派、人物、文本类型过滤，最后返回原文、卷页、版本信息。DILA 已有面向多个佛教数字档案的综合检索体系；伯克利 Dharmamitra 项目的 MITRASearch 则把语义检索扩展到了跨语种。

**文本复用：一个过去做不完的问题。** AI 特别适合处理一个传统佛学研究很难大规模完成的问题：**「这句话以前在哪里出现过？」** 把数千万字佛典转成向量之后，跨卷引用识别、同义改写识别、文本复用检测、伪引文识别、引文传播路径分析、注疏之间的思想继承关系分析，都变得可行。

这有可能改变思想史研究的形态。过去一个学者可能花数年追踪一个人物、一部经典、一条引文；未来机器先完成大规模候选发现，再由学者进行解释。佛学研究因此可能从「小规模精读」扩展为「精读加大规模计算发现」。AI 并不替代学者。它把学者从检索工作中解放出来，投到判断工作中去。

**知识图谱。** 把佛教历史重建成动态网络——人物、师承、寺院、地域、著作、讲学活动、法脉、时代、社会网络——之后，可以研究的问题包括：某一禅宗法脉在不同世纪的传播路径；某部经典由哪些人物引用最多；某寺院与哪些学者、僧侣发生过知识交流；某一思想概念在哪些历史时期快速扩散；中国、日本、韩国、越南之间的思想传播网络。

法鼓文理学院二〇二六年五月的数典专案春季成果发表会，已把「圣严法师年谱知识图谱」「佛典语义搜索」「大型语言模型与佛教数字文献」列为实际研究议题，并用六步流程（义项合并、摘要改写、义项分类、关系抽取验证、S-P-O 三元组抽取、实体链接）构建佛教知识图谱；同一场发表会上，有研究者以 Obsidian 与 Claude Code 建立了《俱舍论注杂录》的引文库（A 级，[CBETA 活动报导](https://cbeta.org/post/30491)）。需说明两处：该场发表会的内容包含六百一十四笔引文库、平行文本索引与六版本对勘，流传材料中「《阿含》关联可视化」的表述与该场实际内容有出入；而 CBETA 官网刊载法鼓山数典的内容，源于两者同源——CBETA 一九九八年由中华佛学研究所支持成立，圣严法师指示全力支持；二〇二三年法人化时，法鼓山佛教基金会捐资一千三百万，是最大捐助者。

**一个容易被夸大的边界。** 上述都是真实的能力，但有一条边界必须标明：**知识图谱与语义检索能发现「关联」，不能判定「意义」。** 机器可以告诉你某条引文在二百部文献中出现过，不能告诉你哪一次引用是承袭、哪一次是反驳、哪一次是误读。宗派教义的判定、思想史的因果、文本的思想分量，仍然只能由受过训练的人来做。

## 十七、LLM 能与不能：一份任务分级

把任务列成一张表，比笼统地说「AI 很有用」或「AI 不可靠」要有用得多。

| 任务 | AI 适用程度 | 原因 |
|------|------------|------|
| 文章摘要 | 高 | 可由人工快速核验 |
| 关键词提取 | 高 | 结构化任务 |
| 初步文献综述 | 高 | 适合召回与整理 |
| 相似文寻找 | 高 | 机器优势明显 |
| 多版本比对 | 高 | 可大规模自动化 |
| 跨语种资料发现 | 高 | LLM 的优势领域 |
| 术语候选解释 | 中高 | 必须回原典 |
| 机器翻译初稿 | 中高 | 可明显提高效率 |
| 思想史解释 | 中 | 必须依赖专家判断 |
| 宗派教义判定 | 中低 | 易把不同传统混淆 |
| 经典最终翻译 | 低 | 需专业译者 |
| 戒律判断 | 低 | 涉及传统与具体情境 |
| 指导高风险修行问题 | 很低 | AI 不应承担最终权威角色 |
| 断定「某观点就是佛法真义」 | 很低 | 涉及复杂传统与诠释权 |

这张表的用途很实在：**它把「要不要用 AI」这个笼统问题，换成了「这件事在哪一档」。** 摘要、检索、找相似文、比对版本——放心用。最终翻译、戒律判断、教义裁定、高风险修行指导——不能用，或者只能在人明确担责的前提下用。

需要补充的是这张表之外的一个事实：幻觉并不限于佛学领域。*Nature* 上的一项研究提出用语义熵检测 LLM 幻觉的方法，并指出模型即使在语言流畅度很高时仍会产生无根据的答案（B 级，Farquhar 等，*Nature* 630:625–630, 2024）。而学术引用能力尤其不能盲信：一项跨学科评估发现，ChatGPT 生成的文献引用存在真实度与 DOI 准确性问题，自然科学场景下 DOI 准确率约百分之三十二点七，**人文学科场景下仅百分之八点五**，DOI 幻觉率达百分之八十九点四（B 级，Mugaanyi 等，*J Med Internet Res* 26:e52935, 2024）。

人文学科百分之八点五这个数字，佛学正落在它里面。所以佛学 AI 必须有自己的防幻觉机制——不是「更小心一点」，是结构性的。

## 十八、「可信佛学 AI」的标准工作流

任何一个专业佛学 AI，都应当采用这样的流程：

```
AI 生成 → 检索增强（RAG） → 原典定位 → 卷／页／段落核验
→ 多版本比较 → 第二来源交叉验证 → 专家复核
→ 生成最终答案 → 保留证据链与模型版本
```

关键原则只有一句：

> **AI 可以提出解释，但原典才是证据；AI 可以提出假设，但学者负责判断。**

举一个具体的例子。用户问：「应无所住而生其心」在禅宗意味着什么？

系统不应直接给出一段现代哲学解释。它至少应同时提供：《金刚经》原文；CBETA 或其他权威版本的出处；相关异文；鸠摩罗什译本；代表性古代注疏；禅宗相关解释；现代学术研究；AI 的综合解释——并且明确区分哪一段是「经典原文」、哪一段是「历史注释」、哪一段是「现代学者观点」、哪一段是「AI 综合」。

这个设计的目的很单纯：**把「AI 把自己的解释冒充经典」这个风险，从「依靠模型自觉」降为「依靠产品结构」。** 模型会出错，结构不会撒谎。

84000 对 AI 辅助佛典翻译采取的也是类似思路。其公开立场文件的原文是：「these sacred texts should not be processed using machine translation alone」；「The primary actor in the translation of sacred literature must always be the human mind.」同时它也承认工具的价值：「84000 believes that the use of AI as a tool to assist translation has considerable value」。它列举的用途包括：检查译文是否有遗漏与错误；提供备选译法；在藏文、梵文、汉文原本之间寻找相关段落；识别不同译本之间的不一致；帮助定位人物、地点与术语（A 级，[84000 立场文件](https://84000.co/documents/84000s-position-on-ai-and-the-machine-translation-of-canonical-literature)）。

把它读成「AI 万能论」或「AI 威胁论」，都不对。它其实是一份**职责划分说明书**：哪些交给机器，哪些必须由人负责，写得清清楚楚。汉传若有类似的公开立场，将省去大量重复争论。这些原则与 UNESCO 针对生成式 AI 教育与研究所倡导的「以人为中心」、数据保护、人类监督等方向一致（A 级，[UNESCO 指南](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=253682)，2023 年 9 月 7 日发布）。

> **EN对应 · Part Three, Chapters Fourteen to Eighteen**
>
> *This part asks not "what is AI" but "what has AI changed"—specifically, what it has changed about the cost of doing certain things, about who is qualified to do them, and about whom the results reach. Technical description that does not bear on those three is omitted.*
>
> **Fourteen: From Digitization to Knowledge Engineering.** On 28 February 2026 the Buddhist Digital Resource Center announced a plan to turn human-transcribed, OCR'd, edition-compared, and cross-validated Buddhist literature into open corpora suitable for AI training and research. The project began in December 2025 with funding from the Khyentse Foundation, at a scale measured in millions of pages. Two months of progress: Gold Standard corpora grew from 1.9 GB to 3.4 GB, over 26 million images passed through OCR, and about 35,000 images were aligned with transcriptions (Grade A, [BDRC announcement](https://www.bdrc.io/blog/2026/02/28/)).
>
> The significance lies not in the figures but in the route it marks: **build the data first, then the intelligence**—rather than building a talking robot first.
>
> Buddhism and digital technology have already passed through two stages. The first was **digital preservation**: converting paper canons, steles, temple gazetteers, images, and audiovisual material into digital resources, with the goal of "not losing it." The second was **digital retrieval and dissemination**: letting people search, read, watch, listen, and participate online, with the goal shifting from "not lost" to "retrievable." The third is arriving now: **computational Buddhist studies and intelligent knowledge systems**, in which machines not only store texts but establish relations across texts, editions, languages, persons, and periods, helping researchers pose questions previously very hard to pose. The goal shifts again, from "retrievable" to "machine-understandable and machine-reasonable."
>
> The empirical basis is clear enough. CBETA has long used TEI/XML structured encoding, so that texts can be read and also processed, cited, and analysed by machine. The University of Tokyo's SAT project joins the digital text of the Taishō canon to page images, available since the 2012 version and locatable to the line (Grade A, [SAT](https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/)). Dharma Drum's DILA integrated search brings together more than ten Buddhist digital archives and exposes over 160 million characters to public search (Grade A, self-reported, [DILA iSearch](https://isearch.dila.edu.tw/)). BDRC's BUDA platform publicly offers more than 17 million pages of Buddhist material (Grade A, self-reported, [bdrc.io](https://www.bdrc.io/); the same institution elsewhere gives "over 59,000 volumes and over 30 million images"); on 14 March 2025 it released a free, open-source desktop Tibetan OCR tool capable of batch-processing images and whole PDF volumes.
>
> Set side by side, these show that the goal has changed: from **"putting the canon online"** to **"building Buddhist civilization into a knowledge system that is verifiable, linkable, computable, citable, translatable, and reliably usable by AI."**
>
> For Chinese Buddhism this carries a particular significance. **Knowledge infrastructure is one of the few things that can be done without proselytizing.** It does not require the other party to accept your faith first, or to cross the barriers of identity and politics, or to be persuaded that you are good. It requires only that the texts be properly organized, the terminology clearly fixed, and the sources plainly marked. Do that, and scholars, translators, developers, and learners worldwide will use it—and in using it, they will know who made it. (Judgment)
>
> The converse holds too: **falling behind here is not missing a publicity channel; it is being absent while others build the future order of Buddhist knowledge.** Ten years from now, if the corpora, terminology, knowledge graphs, and evaluation benchmarks of global Buddhist AI have all set hard, and the Chinese Buddhist portion within them is thin or wrong, then latecomers will be able to adapt to that order—but not define it.
>
> **Fifteen: Eight Layers of Infrastructure.** An adequate Buddhist knowledge system needs at least eight layers.
>
> | Layer | Core content | Relevant technology |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1. Source material | Canons, Dunhuang manuscripts, steles, recorded sayings, transmission records, temple gazetteers, local gazetteers, periodicals | Scanning, OCR/HTR, image recognition |
> | 2. Authoritative text | Editions, critical editions, variant readings, punctuation | TEI/XML, version control |
> | 3. Metadata | Title, author, translator, date, fascicle, provenance, edition, rights | Linked Data, standard metadata |
> | 4. Terminology | Sanskrit, Pali, Tibetan, Chinese, English terms and variants | Terminology databases, ontologies |
> | 5. Relations | Relations among persons, lineages, temples, texts, concepts, events | Knowledge graphs |
> | 6. Semantics | Passages, topics, concepts, quotations, parallel passages, cross-lingual correspondences | Embeddings, semantic search |
> | 7. AI | RAG, LLMs, translation, classification, summarization, Q&A, agents | Large models, vision-language models, agents |
> | 8. Verification | Canonical provenance, edition, page, chain of evidence, expert revision records | Provenance tracking, audit logs |
>
> CBETA's TEI/XML practice has already shown that Buddhist texts can be fully standard digital humanities objects. SAT presses the further point that a digital text must be verifiable against the original page. That is the basic principle of credibility for Buddhist AI.
>
> From which follows a conclusion contrary to the prevailing view: **the core competitiveness of Buddhist AI lies first not in how large the model is, but in whether the underlying corpus is authoritative, whether editions are clear, whether provenance is traceable, whether terminology is standardized, and whether the traditions are correctly distinguished.** (Judgment)
>
> One corollary deserves separate statement: **training a Buddhist large model is almost certainly not the optimal investment for Chinese Buddhism.** General model capabilities are improving rapidly; the cost of building one's own model and the speed at which it depreciates are both worsening. Authoritative corpora, standardized terminology, clear editions, and traceable provenance, by contrast, do not lose value when models change—they gain value with every upgrade. **Money spent on corpora is better spent than money spent on parameters.** (Judgment)
>
> **Sixteen: AI in Buddhist Studies — Amplification, Not Replacement.** Where AI genuinely helps Buddhist studies is not in thinking for the scholar but in releasing him from work that cannot be finished.
>
> **From keyword search to semantic search.** Traditional retrieval depends on already knowing the keyword. A researcher wanting to know what was said about "no-thought" in the Tang and Song must first think of the term itself, then of its variant writings, alternate translations, and other names. Fail to think of them all, and the search is incomplete. Semantic search changes that step: the researcher can ask directly, and the system performs semantic recall first, then filters by period, school, person, and text type, returning the original text with fascicle, page, and edition. DILA already offers integrated search across multiple archives; MITRASearch, from the Dharmamitra project at Berkeley, extends semantic search across languages.
>
> **Textual reuse: a question that could not previously be finished.** AI is especially suited to a problem traditional scholarship could never complete at scale: **"Where has this sentence appeared before?"** Once tens of millions of characters are converted to vectors, detecting cross-fascicle quotation, identifying paraphrase, detecting textual reuse, spotting false attributions, tracing the paths by which a quotation travelled, and analysing intellectual inheritance among commentaries all become feasible.
>
> This may change the shape of intellectual history. A scholar might once have spent years tracing one figure, one scripture, one quotation. In future the machine completes the large-scale discovery of candidates first, and the scholar interprets. Buddhist studies may therefore expand from "small-scale close reading" to "close reading plus large-scale computational discovery." This is not AI replacing the scholar. It is releasing the scholar from retrieval and redirecting him to judgment.
>
> **Knowledge graphs.** Once Buddhist history is rebuilt as a dynamic network—persons, teachers, temples, regions, works, teaching activity, lineages, periods, social networks—the questions that become tractable include: the spread of a Chan lineage across centuries; which figures cite a given scripture most; what intellectual exchanges took place between a temple and particular scholars or monastics; in which periods a concept diffused rapidly; and the networks of intellectual transmission among China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam.
>
> At the Digital Classics project's spring results presentation in May 2026 at Dharma Drum Institute of Liberal Arts, "a knowledge graph of Master Sheng Yen's chronological biography," "semantic search of Buddhist texts," and "large language models and Buddhist digital literature" were listed as active research topics, and a six-step process was used to build a Buddhist knowledge graph (merging senses, rewriting summaries, classifying senses, extracting and validating relations, extracting subject-predicate-object triples, and entity linking); at the same presentation, a researcher used Obsidian and Claude Code to build a citation database for the *Abhidharmakośa-bhāṣya-ṭīkā Tattvārthā* (Grade A, [CBETA event report](https://cbeta.org/post/30491)). Two clarifications: the presentation concerned 614 citation records, a parallel-text index, and a six-edition collation, so the circulating description "visualization of Āgama relations" does not match what the session covered; and the reason CBETA's website carries Dharma Drum's Digital Classics material is common origin—CBETA was founded in 1998 with the support of the Chung-Hwa Institute of Buddhist Studies at Master Sheng Yen's instruction, and at incorporation in 2023 the Dharma Drum Mountain Buddhist Foundation donated NT$13 million as its largest contributor.
>
> **One boundary easily overstated.** All of the above are real capabilities, but one limit must be marked: **knowledge graphs and semantic search can discover relations; they cannot determine meaning.** A machine can tell you that a quotation appears in two hundred texts. It cannot tell you which appearance is inheritance, which is refutation, which is misreading. Doctrinal adjudication, causal explanation in intellectual history, and the intellectual weight of a text remain work for trained people.
>
> **Seventeen: What LLMs Can and Cannot Do — A Task Grading.** A table serves better than a blanket claim that AI is useful or unreliable.
>
> | Task | Suitability | Reason |
> |---|---|---|
> | Summarizing articles | High | Quickly verified by a human |
> | Keyword extraction | High | Structured task |
> | Preliminary literature review | High | Suited to recall and organization |
> | Finding parallel passages | High | Clear machine advantage |
> | Comparing editions | High | Automatable at scale |
> | Cross-lingual source discovery | High | An LLM strength |
> | Candidate explanations of terms | Medium-high | Must be checked against the canon |
> | Machine translation drafts | Medium-high | Marked efficiency gains |
> | Explaining intellectual history | Medium | Requires expert judgment |
> | Doctrinal adjudication among schools | Medium-low | Prone to conflating traditions |
> | Final translation of scripture | Low | Requires a professional translator |
> | Vinaya judgments | Low | Depends on tradition and circumstance |
> | Guiding high-risk practice questions | Very low | AI should not hold final authority |
> | Declaring "this is the true meaning of the dharma" | Very low | Involves complex traditions and interpretive authority |
>
> The table's use is practical: **it converts the vague question "should we use AI?" into "which tier does this task fall in?"** Summarizing, retrieval, finding parallel passages, comparing editions—use it freely. Final translation, vinaya judgment, doctrinal adjudication, guidance on high-risk practice—not usable, or usable only where a human explicitly bears responsibility.
>
> One fact outside the table: hallucination is not specific to Buddhist studies. A study in *Nature* proposed detecting LLM hallucination using semantic entropy, noting that models produce unsubstantiated answers even when fluency is high (Grade B, Farquhar et al., *Nature* 630:625–630, 2024). Citation behaviour in particular cannot be trusted blindly: a cross-disciplinary evaluation found that ChatGPT's generated citations have problems of authenticity and DOI accuracy, with DOI accuracy around 32.7 percent in the natural sciences and **only 8.5 percent in the humanities**, and a DOI hallucination rate of 89.4 percent (Grade B, Mugaanyi et al., *J Med Internet Res* 26:e52935, 2024).
>
> Buddhist studies falls inside that 8.5 percent. Buddhist AI therefore requires its own anti-hallucination mechanism—not "greater care," but something structural.
>
> **Eighteen: A Standard Workflow for Trustworthy Buddhist AI.** Any professional Buddhist AI should adopt this pipeline:
>
> ```
> AI generation → Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) → Location in the canon
> → Verification of fascicle, page, and passage → Comparison across editions
> → Cross-validation against a second source → Expert review
> → Final answer generated → Evidence chain and model version preserved
> ```
>
> The governing principle is one sentence:
>
> > **AI may propose an interpretation; the canon is the evidence. AI may propose a hypothesis; the scholar makes the judgment.**
>
> A concrete example. A user asks: "What does 'let the mind arise without abiding anywhere' mean in Chan?"
>
> The system should not answer with a paragraph of modern philosophical explanation. It should at minimum provide, simultaneously: the original text of the *Diamond Sūtra*; the source in CBETA or another authoritative edition; relevant variant readings; Kumārajīva's translation; representative classical commentaries; relevant Chan explanations; modern scholarship; and the AI's own synthesis—with explicit separation of which part is "canonical text," which is "historical commentary," which is "modern scholarly opinion," and which is "AI synthesis."
>
> The purpose of the design is simple: **to move the risk of an AI passing off its own interpretation as scripture from "the model chooses to behave" to "the product is structured so it cannot."** Models err. Structures do not lie.
>
> 84000 takes a similar approach. Its public position states in the original: "these sacred texts should not be processed using machine translation alone"; "The primary actor in the translation of sacred literature must always be the human mind." It also acknowledges the tool's value: "84000 believes that the use of AI as a tool to assist translation has considerable value." The uses it lists include checking translations for omissions and errors; offering alternative translation choices; finding related passages across Tibetan, Sanskrit, and Chinese source texts; identifying inconsistencies across translations; and helping locate people, places, and terms (Grade A, [84000 position paper](https://84000.co/documents/84000s-position-on-ai-and-the-machine-translation-of-canonical-literature)).
>
> The weight of this position lies in the fact that it is neither "AI solves everything" nor "AI is a threat," but a **division-of-responsibility statement.** What goes to the machine, what must remain with a human—stated plainly. If Chinese Buddhism issued a comparable public position, a great deal of repetitive argument would be saved. These principles align with UNESCO's guidance on generative AI in education and research (Grade A, [UNESCO guidance](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=253682), issued 7 September 2023).

## 十九、AI 辅助佛典翻译：最值得突破的传播基础设施

回到第十章那道坎：汉文佛典资源极丰富，而世界上大量潜在读者无法直接阅读古汉语佛典。

**这是汉传全球传播最大的单一结构性障碍，也是最有可能被技术实质性改变的一项。** AI 翻译在这里不是普通的传播工具——它直接改变「可访问性」这个变量，而可访问性是前面所有传播策略的前提。没有可读的译本，禅修、教育、对话、学术交流都无从谈起。

二〇二四年一项专门研究比较了 ChatGPT-4、ERNIE Bot 4 与 Gemini Advanced 对古汉语佛教文本的翻译能力，研究使用来自 CBETA 的佛典与中国佛教文献，并通过人工评价分析（B 级，Xiang Wei, *Religions* 15(12):1559, 2024）。这说明「LLM 加佛典翻译」已经从概念讨论进入可比较评测阶段。同时，专门的佛教翻译模型也已公开：Dharmamitra 项目发布过针对佛教汉文到英文翻译的模型（`buddhist-nlp/mbart-buddhist-chinese-to-eng`），其模型卡坦诚地写明两点——在人物、地点等命名实体上存在机器翻译系统常见的问题，且「有时会表现出幻觉倾向」。

这句自述比任何评测分数都有价值。**一个愿意在模型卡上写明自己会幻觉的项目，比一个宣称准确率百分之九十五的项目更值得信任。**

由此可以推出：真正值得建的，不是「一个会翻译佛经的 ChatGPT」，而是**佛教多语翻译工作台**。

```
原文 → 自动断句 → 术语识别 → 相关经典召回 → 同类译文召回
→ AI 翻译候选 → 多版本对照 → 译者修改 → 专家审校
→ 术语库更新 → 发布 → 新数据反哺模型
```

这个流程里，AI 承担「候选生成」与「一致性检查」，译者承担「判断」，专家承担「定稿」。三者职责不重叠。

### 一个容易做错的设计决定

多数翻译项目在术语上追求「唯一正译」。这个目标听起来正当，实则有害。

理由是：汉传两千年的译经史本身就是「多译并存」的历史。同一个梵文词，鸠摩罗什译作「毕竟空」，玄奘译作「毕竟无」，真谛译作「真实空」。这里没有谁译错。被保留下来的，是不同时代的理解差异。强行统一，等于抹掉这层信息。

更好的做法是把术语库设计成**分层**的：底下一层是「推荐译名」，上面允许多个并存译名，每个译名标注来源——谁译的、哪个时代、依据什么。机器默认输出推荐译名，但保留其余选项与理由。**这样，统一的便利与并存的丰富就都能得到。**（判断）

技术上需要两个前提：术语层要开放、可引用、有版本号；译文要分级标注——未校、已校、权威三级。读者看到一段机器译文时，应当能立刻知道它经过了几道人工校订。

### 覆盖率仪表盘

把 BDK 那个「百分之六十五」变成可审计的指标。具体说，需要一份**按页（或按品）统计的汉文佛典英译覆盖率**——哪部经译了多少、谁在译、什么状态、质量如何。

这件事的技术难度很低，组织难度很高，因为它要求各方公开自己的进度。但它的价值与难度成正比：**没有这把尺子，翻译规划就只能凭感觉排序。**

### 一件被忽略的配套工作

译经之外，还有一件同样重要却几乎无人做的事：**写教科书。**

藏传在西方的成功，很大程度上靠成体系的英文教材——从入门到进阶，有课本、有讲义、有术语表、有练习，学校的课程可以直接采用。汉传在英文世界里几乎没有这样的东西。一位美国大学的宗教学教师想开一门汉传佛教的课，找不到合适的教科书；一位对汉传感兴趣的西方读者想系统入门，找不到一本讲得清楚的导论。

结果是：**汉传在西方学界的入口极窄。** 学者可以研究它，但学生进不来；学生进不来，二十年后就没有学者了。

所以有一个建议，值得排在译经之后、其他所有事情之前：**组织一批真正的学者与法师，写三五本英文的汉传佛教入门教材与课程包**——一本通史、一本教理导论、一本禅修实践手册、一本术语详解。用最好的英文写，配最高质量的中文引文，由大学出版社出版。这件事投入不大，杠杆极高。（判断）

### 一个反直觉的判断

很多人认为，AI 翻译普及之后，译经人才的需求会下降。本文的判断相反：**需求会上升。**

原因是翻译工作分两段。AI 能胜任的是「初译」这一段——把古汉语转成通顺的现代语言。而「审校」这一段不但不能被替代，反而变得更关键：当机器每天产出成千上万段初稿时，**唯一稀缺的是能够判断「这段译文对不对」的人。**

而判断一段佛典译文对不对，需要的恰恰是最难培养的能力：通古汉语、通梵藏、通教理、通宗派差异、还要通目标语言。**AI 让初译变便宜，等于让审校变得更贵。**（判断）

这对僧伽教育的含义是具体的：如果十年后佛典翻译的瓶颈在审校，那么今天开始的译经人才培养，方向应当是**培养能审校的人**，而不是培养能初译的人。

## 二十、修行辅助的四层：学习辅助不等于修行本身

这是整个领域中最需要谨慎的部分。

AI 很适合做的事有一长串：经教学习、每日阅读计划、术语学习、经典问答、修学笔记、共修提醒、禅修时间管理、反思日志、学习路径推荐、多语法义学习、法会信息与课程管理。但这些功能不能等同于「AI 具有修行经验」，更不能把「AI 生成的语言流畅度」误认为「AI 具有证量或悟境」。

一个实用而清晰的分层是：

**第一层，知识型 AI**——回答「什么是十二因缘？」典型的教育任务。风险低，收益直接，应当大力发展。

**第二层，陪伴型 AI**——「今天提醒我静坐二十分钟。」行为支持，工具功能。

**第三层，反思型 AI**——用户输入：「今天我在工作中因为别人否定我而很愤怒。」AI 可以帮助梳理：发生了什么；哪些是事实；哪些是自己的判断；情绪如何产生；可以如何按照已经学过的佛法进行反思。

这一层是「反思镜」，有用，但有一条界线：**它帮助用户使用他自己学过的法义去照见自己，而不是代替他给出结论。** 一旦 AI 开始告诉他「你应该放下执着」「这说明你的我执很重」，它就从镜子变成了判官——而它没有资格做判官。

**第四层，高风险宗教指导**——例如：「我应该不要吃药，专心打坐吗？」「我是不是已经开悟？」「师父这么要求我是否必须服从？」「我出现严重异常体验，是不是进入某种高级禅定？」

这四个问题有一个共同点：**答错了会伤人。** 这一层不应让 AI 做任何判断，而必须设置人工导师介入、专业机构转介与风险升级机制。关于数字精神陪伴的研究也支持这一谨慎：研究发现用户容易向聊天机器人透露高度私密的信息，同时对隐私、错误信息、数据安全与依赖问题抱有担忧（B 级，Kneese、Vecchione、Marwick, *Communication and Change* 1(1), 2025）。

四层之上，有一条贯穿的界线，值得单独标出：

**AI 可以辅助「学」，不能替代「修」。**

学是获取知识、理解义理、建立正见——这些可以被语言承载，因此可以被机器承载。修是亲证、是身心转变、是在具体情境中的抉择——这些不通过语言传递，因此不可能通过语言模型传递。

说「AI 能帮助你学习佛法」是准确的。说「AI 能帮助你修行」，就已经越界。而说「AI 可以指导你的修行」，则不只是越界，是危险。（判断）

## 二十一、「AI 师父」：最需要慎重对待的方向

技术上，一个 AI 完全可以模拟某位高僧的语言风格。这里必须区分三个层次：

**模拟其文风** ≠ **根据其著作回答** ≠ **代表其本人传法**

第一层是风格模仿，风险有限。第二层是有据可查的问答，属于知识服务。**第三层是宗教权威的模拟，风险极高。**

因此更可取的设计是**「传统人物知识代理」**，而不是**「AI 大师人格」**。比较这两句话：

> 「根据《六祖坛经》以及现有学术研究，说明慧能思想。」

> 「我是六祖慧能，请告诉你应该如何修行。」

前者在知识真实性与伦理上都更稳健。它把回答的边界限定在「文献说过的」与「学者研究过的」之内，并且不冒充主体。

二〇二五至二〇二六年已出现一些实验性佛教对话系统，例如围绕亲鸾等净土宗思想家的专门 AI 系统（京都大学熊谷诚慈团队与 Terraverse 于二〇二三年九月推出的「親鸞ボット」，同批还有「世親ボット」）、佛教教学型 AI，以及基于 RAG、能答问时标明经名章偈的佛典问答平台。这类项目很有研究价值，但多数仍处于实验性或项目自述阶段。**「能够生成宗教对话」与「已经拥有宗教权威」之间，隔着不可逾越的距离。**

### 真正的伦理风险

未来最危险的问题，很可能不在「AI 有没有佛性」这一边。更该问的是：**人是否开始把 AI 的输出当成佛法本身？**

具体有五种风险。

**权威幻觉。** AI 的语言非常确定，而这种确定并不等于真实。一个流畅的段落会被读作一个可靠的结论。

**拟人化。** 用户可能逐渐把一个持续回应自己的 AI 当作「懂我」的精神对象。它总在、总答、从不疲倦、从不评判——这些特性恰好构成一种危险的吸引力。

**依赖。** 人可能越来越依赖 AI 反思，而减少真实的师友关系。而修行恰恰是一件必须在关系中发生的事。

**教义扁平化。** LLM 倾向于给出「听起来合理」的综合答案，而佛教内部真实存在巨大的传统差异。把天台、华严、唯识、禅、净土揉成一个「佛教说」，是技术上的便利，也是教理上的失真。

**数据隐私。** 修行日志可能包含身体状态、情绪、家庭关系、创伤经历、性与婚姻、宗教信仰、心理状态。这些数据不能按照普通内容平台的数据逻辑处理。佛教 AI 应当建立比一般教育 AI 更高的隐私标准。

把五种风险归到一处，核心只有一句：

**佛教 AI 的真正风险，与机器人有无灵魂无关。真正的风险是：人把权威交给算法。**（判断）

历代祖师的权威，建立在戒、定、慧的长期实践与僧团共同体的印可之上。这份权威无法被转录为参数，也不应被模拟为一个可以随时提问的对象。技术能做的是把经典整理清楚、把出处标明白、把义理讲通畅——**它做的是「使法可闻」，不是「代法说法」。**

这一区分看起来细微，却决定了整个佛教 AI 事业的方向。

> **EN对应 · Chapters Nineteen to Twenty-One**
>
> **Nineteen: AI-Assisted Translation — The Infrastructure Most Worth Breaking Through.** Back to the ditch described in Chapter Ten: the Chinese Buddhist canon is extremely rich, and a great many potential readers worldwide cannot read classical Chinese Buddhist texts directly.
>
> **This is the single largest structural obstacle to the global transmission of Chinese Buddhism, and the one most likely to be substantively changed by technology.** AI translation is not an ordinary communication tool here—it changes the variable of accessibility directly, and accessibility is the precondition of every transmission strategy. Without readable translations, meditation instruction, education, dialogue, and academic exchange all have nowhere to begin.
>
> A 2024 study compared the translation ability of ChatGPT-4, ERNIE Bot 4, and Gemini Advanced on classical Chinese Buddhist texts, using texts from CBETA and Chinese Buddhist literature and analysing them through human evaluation (Grade B, Xiang Wei, *Religions* 15(12):1559, 2024). Purpose-built Buddhist translation models have also been released: the Dharmamitra project published a model for Chinese-to-English translation of Buddhist texts (`buddhist-nlp/mbart-buddhist-chinese-to-eng`), and its model card states two things candidly—the usual problems that NMT systems have with named entities (names of persons and places), and that the model "shows a tendency to hallucinate at times."
>
> That admission is worth more than any evaluation score. **A project willing to write on its model card that it hallucinates is more trustworthy than one claiming 95 percent accuracy.**
>
> Hence: what is worth building is not "a ChatGPT that translates Buddhist scriptures" but a **multilingual Buddhist translation workbench**.
>
> ```
> Source text → Automatic segmentation → Term recognition
> → Retrieval of related scriptures → Retrieval of comparable translations
> → AI translation candidates → Comparison across editions → Translator revision
> → Expert review → Terminology database update → Publication
> → New data fed back into the model
> ```
>
> In this pipeline the AI handles candidate generation and consistency checking, the translator handles judgment, and the expert handles final authorization. The three responsibilities do not overlap.
>
> **One design decision easily made wrongly.** Most translation projects pursue a single "correct" rendering for each term. The goal sounds proper; it is in fact harmful.
>
> Two thousand years of Chinese translation history is itself a history of coexisting renderings. For the same Sanskrit term, Kumārajīva rendered it as "ultimately empty," Xuanzang as "ultimately nonexistent," Paramārtha as "genuinely empty." This is not a matter of someone translating incorrectly; it is the preservation of differences in understanding across periods. Forced uniformity erases that layer of information.
>
> The better approach is to design the terminology database in **layers**: a bottom layer of "recommended renderings," above it a set of coexisting alternatives, each annotated with its source—who translated it, in what period, on what basis. The machine outputs the recommended rendering by default while retaining the alternatives and their rationales. **In this way both the convenience of uniformity and the richness of coexistence are preserved.** (Judgment)
>
> Two technical preconditions follow: the terminology layer must be open, citable, and versioned; and translations must be graded—unreviewed, reviewed, authoritative. A reader encountering a machine translation should be able to see at once how many rounds of human review it has passed.
>
> **A coverage dashboard.** Turn BDK's "65 percent" into an auditable metric—a page-by-page or chapter-by-chapter count of English translation coverage for the Chinese canon: which texts have been translated, how much, by whom, in what state, at what quality. Technically easy, organizationally hard, because it requires all parties to disclose their progress. But its value is proportional to its difficulty: **without that ruler, translation planning can only be ordered by feel.**
>
> **A companion task that gets overlooked: writing textbooks.** Tibetan Buddhism's success in the West rests heavily on systematic English teaching materials—textbooks, lecture notes, glossaries, and exercises, from introductory to advanced, adoptable directly by university courses. Chinese Buddhism has almost nothing of the kind in English. A religion professor wanting to offer a course on Chinese Buddhism finds no suitable textbook; an interested Western reader wanting a systematic introduction finds no clear guide.
>
> The result: **the entry point into Chinese Buddhism in Western academia is very narrow.** Scholars can study it, but students cannot get in; and if students cannot get in, in twenty years there will be no scholars.
>
> Hence a recommendation that deserves to rank immediately after translation and ahead of everything else: **organize a group of genuine scholars and monastics to write three to five English introductory textbooks and course packages for Chinese Buddhism**—a history, an introduction to doctrine, a practice manual, and a glossary. Small investment, very high leverage. (Judgment)
>
> **One counter-intuitive judgment.** Many assume that as AI translation spreads, the demand for translation talent will fall. This report judges the opposite: **demand will rise.**
>
> Translation has two stages. What AI handles well is the first—turning classical Chinese into fluent modern language. The second stage, review, cannot merely be replaced; it becomes more critical. When a machine produces thousands of draft passages a day, **the only scarce thing is a person able to judge whether a given rendering is correct.**
>
> And judging whether a Buddhist translation is correct demands precisely the hardest capacities to train: command of classical Chinese, of Sanskrit and Tibetan, of doctrine, of differences among schools, and of the target language. **AI makes first drafts cheap—which makes review more expensive.** (Judgment)
>
> The implication for monastic education is concrete. If in ten years the bottleneck is review, then the training begun today should aim at **producing reviewers, not producers of first drafts.**
>
> **Twenty: Four Tiers of Practice Support — Learning Assistance Is Not Practice.** This is the part of the field requiring the most caution.
>
> The list of things AI does well is long: studying doctrine, daily reading plans, terminology, scriptural Q&A, study notes, group-practice reminders, time management for meditation, reflective journals, learning-path recommendations, multilingual study of meaning, and event administration. But none of these equates to "AI has practice experience," and fluency of AI-generated language must never be mistaken for realization or depth of insight.
>
> **Tier one, knowledge AI**—answering "what are the twelve links of dependent origination?" A straightforward educational task. Low risk, direct benefit, deserving of vigorous development.
>
> **Tier two, companion AI**—"remind me to sit for twenty minutes today." Behavioural support, a tool function.
>
> **Tier three, reflective AI**—the user types: "Today at work I got very angry because someone rejected me." The AI can help sort through what happened; which parts are fact; which are the user's own judgments; how the emotion arose; and how one might reflect according to the dharma one has already learned.
>
> This tier is a "mirror for reflection," useful, with one boundary line: **it helps users see themselves through the teachings they have already studied; it does not deliver conclusions on their behalf.** Once the AI begins telling him "you should let go of attachment" or "this shows your self-grasping is strong," it has turned from mirror into magistrate—and it is not qualified for that office.
>
> **Tier four, high-risk religious guidance**—for example: "Should I stop taking my medication and just sit in meditation?" "Am I already enlightened?" "If my teacher requires this, must I obey?" "I'm having severe unusual experiences—am I entering some advanced state of concentration?"
>
> These share one feature: **a wrong answer causes harm.** In this tier the AI should make no judgment at all; human mentorship, referral to professional services, and risk-escalation mechanisms must be in place. Research on digital spiritual companionship supports this caution: users readily disclose highly private information to chatbots while worrying about privacy, misinformation, data security, and dependency (Grade B, Kneese, Vecchione, and Marwick, *Communication and Change* 1(1), 2025).
>
> Above the four tiers runs a single line worth marking on its own:
>
> **AI can assist study. It cannot replace practice.**
>
> Study is acquiring knowledge, understanding doctrine, establishing right view—all of which can be carried by language, and therefore by a machine. Practice is direct realization, the transformation of body and mind, and decisions made in concrete circumstances—none of which is transmitted through language, and therefore none of which can be transmitted by a language model.
>
> "AI can help you study the dharma" is accurate. "AI can help you practise" has already crossed the line. And "AI can guide your practice" is not merely over the line—it is dangerous. (Judgment)
>
> **Twenty-One: The "AI Master" — The Direction Requiring the Most Caution.** Technically, an AI can fully simulate the verbal style of a famous monk. Three levels must be distinguished: **simulating his style** ≠ **answering from his writings** ≠ **speaking on his behalf**. The first is stylistic imitation, with limited risk. The second is sourced question and answer, a knowledge service. **The third is the simulation of religious authority, and the risk is extreme.**
>
> Hence the preferable design is a **"knowledge agent for a traditional figure,"** not an **"AI master persona."** Compare two sentences:
>
> > "According to the *Platform Sūtra of the Sixth Patriarch* and existing scholarship, explain Huineng's thought."
>
> > "I am Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch. Let me tell you how you should practise."
>
> The first is more robust both epistemically and ethically. It confines the answer to what the texts say and what scholars have studied, and it does not impersonate a subject.
>
> Experimental Buddhist dialogue systems have appeared in 2025–2026: a dedicated AI for the Pure Land thinker Shinran (the "Shinran Bot" released in September 2023 by Professor Seiji Kumagai's team at Kyoto University with Terraverse, alongside a "Vasubandhu Bot"), Buddhist teaching AIs, and RAG-based scriptural Q&A platforms that cite the scripture, chapter, and verse. Such projects have research value, but most remain experimental or self-described. **Between "can generate religious dialogue" and "possesses religious authority" lies an impassable distance.**
>
> **The real ethical risk.** The most dangerous question ahead is probably not "does AI have buddha-nature" but: **has a person begun to take the AI's output as the dharma itself?**
>
> Five specific risks: **authority illusion** (AI language is highly certain, and that certainty is not truth; a fluent paragraph is read as a reliable conclusion); **anthropomorphization** (users may treat an AI that keeps responding as a spiritual other who "understands me"—always there, always answering, never tiring, never judging, a combination that amounts to a dangerous attraction); **dependency** (people may rely on AI for reflection while real relationships with teachers and friends diminish—and practice is precisely something that must happen within relationships); **doctrinal flattening** (LLMs tend to produce a plausible-sounding synthesis, while enormous differences genuinely exist among Buddhist traditions; kneading Tiantai, Huayan, Yogācāra, Chan, and Pure Land into one "what Buddhism says" is a technical convenience and a doctrinal distortion); and **data privacy** (a practice journal may contain physical condition, emotions, family relations, traumatic experience, sexuality and marriage, religious belief, and psychological state—such data cannot be handled by the data logic of an ordinary content platform; Buddhist AI should set a higher privacy standard than educational AI generally).
>
> Gathering the five into one place, the core is a single sentence:
>
> **The real risk of Buddhist AI is not that a machine has a soul. It is that a person hands authority to an algorithm.** (Judgment)
>
> The authority of the patriarchs was built on long practice in precepts, concentration, and wisdom, and on the sanction of the monastic community. That authority cannot be transcribed into parameters, and should not be simulated as an object one can question at will. What technology can do is organize the texts clearly, mark the sources plainly, and explain the doctrine coherently—**it makes the dharma hearable; it does not speak the dharma in anyone's place.**
>
> The distinction looks fine. It determines the direction of the entire Buddhist AI enterprise.

## 二十二、度量、算法与身体的边界

### 把「多少人看见」换成「多少人留下」

传播效果不能再只看粉丝数、点赞数、播放量。更有意义的是建立一条**佛法传播漏斗**：

```
曝光 → 观看 → 理解 → 第一次学习 → 持续学习 → 线上共修
→ 线下参与 → 长期修学 → 志愿服务 → 慈善实践 → 社区参与
```

这带来一个重大改变：**把「传播量」变成「学习与实践的转化率」。**

一条视频一百万播放，不一定比一个只有三千人但形成了持续学习社群的课程更有宗教教育价值。数据科学真正能帮佛教组织回答的问题是：哪些内容能吸引新人？哪些能形成持续学习？哪些语言市场需求最大？哪些课程流失严重？哪些新成员最终进入了线下社群？什么内容适合青年？不同国家的用户走的是不同路径吗？

但有一条界线：**可以测量传播行为，不能轻易把「悟性、功德、证量」变成平台 KPI。** 一旦寺院开始用转化率考核法师，它就已经不是在弘法了。

### 推荐算法与权力结构

数字宗教研究近年越来越强调：平台不是中性的传播管道。二〇二六年一篇综述综合分析了八十八项研究，指出平台基础设施、算法可见性与网络化公众正在改变宗教权威、仪式实践与社群形成（B 级，AlMazaedh 等，*Frontiers in Sociology* 11:1802281, 2026）。

这对汉传非常重要，因为传播结构正在变。

过去的传播结构是**寺院→法师→信众**。数字平台之后变成**寺院→法师→平台算法→用户**。而 AI 时代可能变成**经典／法师／寺院→AI 知识层→AI 推荐与问答→用户**。

也就是说，**新的「中介者」可能不再只是平台，而是 AI 本身。**

因此需要维护的，不只是流量，而是三样透明：**知识来源透明、推荐逻辑透明、教义边界透明。** 当一个信众的问题由 AI 回答时，「这个答案从哪里来」必须是他能查到的。

### VR、AR 与身体的边界

VR/AR 适合做的事很清楚：佛教艺术教育、石窟与寺院数字重建、历史场景可视化、佛教博物馆、经变图教学、建筑与艺术史教学、远程文化体验，以及某些类型的禅修环境辅助。

已有研究系统评价了 VR 正念干预的效果，发现其在状态正念、负性情绪等方面具有一定短期积极效果，但不同研究的方法、对象与干预方式差异很大。一项二〇二四年发表于 *Digital Health* 的系统综述与证据图纳入二十二项研究，指出需要更大型、更严格的研究（B 级，Wieczorek 等，*Digit Health* 10, 2024）。

这里有一处需要特别指出：流传材料称该研究为「二〇二六年的一项 Meta 分析，纳入三十五项研究、约一千五百五十名参与者」。经核查，实际是二〇二四年发表的**系统综述加证据图**（非 Meta 分析），纳入**二十二项**研究。年份、研究设计、样本量三处均不相符。这是核查中发现的最典型的一类错误——**数字精确到具体，却是错的**，比模糊的概括更容易骗过读者。

因此必须严格区分两句话。**VR 可以辅助进入某种环境或注意状态**——这是可以研究、也已有初步证据的。**VR 能够制造禅定、悟境或证果**——这不是一个科学命题。

脑电、心率变异性、呼吸、心率、睡眠、注意力、生理反馈、神经反馈——这些都值得研究。但目前的证据**不足以支持「利用这些指标直接识别高级佛教修行境界」**。二〇二四年一项关于正念神经反馈的系统综述发现，EEG/fMRI 相关研究仍存在样本量、对照设计与迁移效应不足等问题，尤其缺少充分的假神经反馈对照；因此不能简单把神经信号变化解释为更高层次的修行成就（B 级，Treves、Greene、Bajwa 等，正式版见 *Imaging Neuroscience* 2:imag-2-00396, 2024；流传材料所引 PubMed 39314394 为预印本）。

稳妥的方向是**「生理反馈辅助训练」**，而不是**「脑电测量证果」**。

这一区分关乎的不只是科学严谨，还关乎教法本身。**把修行境界等同于某个生理指标，是用一个可测量的东西替换一个不可测量的东西——而替换掉的，恰恰是修行。**（判断）

## 二十三、项目生态：谁在做什么

当前值得关注的方向有五类，此处只列名目与定位，具体数据与链接见附录三。

**BDRC** 是佛教数字底座。它的思路是先把佛教数据建好，聊天机器人是以后的事。二〇二六年的开放数据集计划尤其值得重视。

**CBETA＋DILA＋SAT** 构成汉传佛教的数字学术底座，分别代表结构化经典、综合检索、版本与页面核验三个方向。三者未来非常值得进一步整合。

**Dharmamitra / MITRA** 已把佛教多语搜索、文本复用检测与机器翻译作为明确研究方向，从实验演示进入了学术生产流程。

**84000** 提供了一个值得汉传借鉴的范式：AI 作为专业翻译团队的增强工具，而不是替代专业译者。

**新一代佛教 RAG 与知识图谱项目**近年陆续出现，把佛典聚合、RAG、知识图谱、跨语种对读结合起来。此类项目显示「佛教搜索引擎」正在向「AI 研究环境」演化，但其中一些项目的规模、准确率、数据覆盖范围仍主要来自项目方自身介绍，**目前不能与 CBETA、SAT、BDRC 这类长期专业项目直接等量齐观。**

汉传自己的动作也已出现：佛光山的「人間雲」（二〇二五年二月三日发表，语料为《星云大师全集》与《人间福报》二十五年新闻库）、Vurbo.ai 术语翻译系统、灵隐寺的古籍 OCR 与「如是佛典」、法鼓山数典的知识图谱。这些是真实进展，起点是好的，规模与 CBETA、BDRC 相比仍然很小。

**这里有一个战略判断：汉传不需要也来不及在每一项上都自建。** 它需要的是——在少数几件只有它能做的事上做到不可替代，其余部分接入别人的基础设施。（判断）

只有汉传才有的东西是什么？是汉文大藏经的权威版本与校勘、是汉传诸宗的义理体系与术语、是两千年的注疏传统、是完整的比丘尼戒统、是人间佛教的现代实践经验。其余——OCR 引擎、向量数据库、RAG 框架、大模型——都应当用现成的，而不是重造。

## 二十四、五至十年路线

把前面的判断落成时间表，大致分三段。细节见附录三。

**二〇二六至二〇二七：数据底座阶段。** 优先事项是 CBETA／DILA／SAT 等资源的协同、权威佛典数据标准、人物与寺院与法脉的知识图谱、佛教多语术语库、佛典版本与引用标准、高质量 OCR、多语平行语料、专业 RAG。这一阶段最重要的目标只有一个：**保证数据可信**。「智能」是以后的事。

**二〇二八至二〇三〇：知识智能化阶段。** 佛学 AI 研究助手、AI 翻译工作台、多语智能检索、学术文献 Agent、佛教知识图谱、个人佛学知识库、AI 课程助手、多语自动字幕、AI 数字博物馆、全球佛教知识门户。这一阶段最重要的变化是：**从「用户搜索佛典」转向「用户与佛教知识对话」。**

**二〇三一至二〇三五：智能生态阶段。** 若前两阶段的数据与治理基础建设成功，才可能发展多模态佛教 AI、个人终身佛学导师系统、AI 与 VR 结合的佛教教育、全球多语佛教课程自动适配、跨传统比较研究 Agent、佛教历史虚拟重建、个性化学习路径、国际佛教知识网络。

但即使到了第三阶段，有一条底线建议始终保持：**AI 是工具、助手、媒介；人类导师、学者、僧团与真实社群仍然是核心。**

### 六项原则

**一、原典优先。** 任何重大教义性结论都尽可能回到原典。

**二、出处优先。** 答案必须尽可能提供经名、卷次、章节或页码、版本。

**三、传统分层。** 系统不能把早期佛教、中观、唯识、天台、华严、禅宗、净土混成一个「佛教统一答案」。

**四、解释透明。** 必须告诉用户：这是经典原文，这是古代注疏，这是现代学术解释，这是 AI 综合。

**五、人类导师在环。** 涉及戒律、心理危机、强烈宗教体验、师徒关系、医疗问题、高风险修行者，必须设置人工介入。

**六、数据最小化。** 修行日志、个人经历、情绪与敏感身份数据，应遵循最小采集原则，不应「为了做个性化 AI」而无限收集。

### 十项评测

传统 AI 只测试「答案像不像」，佛学 AI 应当测试十项：

| 指标 | 测什么 |
|------|--------|
| 原典准确率 | 引用的经典是否真的存在 |
| 出处准确率 | 卷、页、品、版本是否准确 |
| 版本识别率 | 是否把不同版本混为一谈 |
| 宗派区分率 | 是否正确区分不同传统 |
| 引文完整率 | 是否遗漏上下文 |
| 翻译准确率 | 专业术语是否稳定 |
| 幻觉率 | 是否生成不存在的经典、人物、引文 |
| 可追溯率 | 用户能否点击回原典 |
| 专家一致率 | 佛教学者对答案的认可度 |
| 多语一致率 | 中英日韩等版本的核心意义是否一致 |

**这十项里没有一项是「回答听起来多有智慧」。** 这不是疏忽，是立场：佛学 AI 的合格线应当由可核验的准确性划定，而不是由文本的感染力划定。（判断）

> **EN对应 · Chapters Twenty-Two to Twenty-Four**
>
> **Twenty-Two: Measurement, Algorithms, and the Boundaries of the Body.**
>
> **Replacing "how many saw it" with "how many stayed."** Transmission effectiveness can no longer be measured by follower counts, likes, and views. What matters more is a **dharma transmission funnel**:
>
> ```
> Exposure → Viewing → Understanding → First study → Continued study
> → Online group practice → In-person participation → Long-term study
> → Volunteer service → Charitable practice → Community engagement
> ```
>
> This brings a major shift: **from "transmission volume" to "the conversion rate into study and practice."** A video with a million views is not necessarily of greater religious-educational value than a course of three thousand that formed a continuing study community. The questions data science can genuinely help Buddhist organizations answer are: what content attracts newcomers? What content produces sustained study? Which language markets have the greatest demand? Which courses lose people badly? Which new members eventually enter in-person communities? What content suits young people? Do users in different countries follow different paths?
>
> One line must be drawn: **transmission behaviour can be measured; insight, merit, and realization must not lightly be turned into platform KPIs.** Once a temple begins evaluating its monastics by conversion rates, it is no longer transmitting the dharma.
>
> **Recommender algorithms and the structure of power.** A 2026 review of digital religion, synthesizing 88 studies, argues that platform infrastructure, algorithmic visibility, and networked publics are changing religious authority, ritual practice, and community formation (Grade B, AlMazaedh et al., *Frontiers in Sociology* 11:1802281, 2026).
>
> This matters greatly, because the structure of transmission is changing. It once ran **temple → monastic → devotee**. After digital platforms it ran **temple → monastic → platform algorithm → user**. In the AI era it may run **scripture / monastic / temple → AI knowledge layer → AI recommendation and Q&A → user**. That is, **the new intermediary may no longer be the platform alone. It may be the AI itself.**
>
> What must therefore be maintained is not merely traffic but three kinds of transparency: **transparency of knowledge provenance, transparency of recommendation logic, and transparency of doctrinal boundaries.** When a devotee's question is answered by an AI, "where did this answer come from" must be something he can look up.
>
> **VR, AR, and the boundaries of the body.** What VR/AR suits is clear enough: Buddhist art education, digital reconstruction of cave sites and temples, visualization of historical scenes, museums, teaching from transformation tableaux, architectural and art-historical instruction, remote cultural experience, and certain kinds of environmental support for meditation.
>
> Studies have systematically reviewed VR-based mindfulness interventions, finding some short-term positive effects on state mindfulness and negative affect, but with wide variation in method, population, and intervention. A 2024 systematic review and evidence map in *Digital Health* included **22** studies and called for larger and more rigorous research (Grade B, Wieczorek et al., *Digit Health* 10, 2024).
>
> One point requires special note. Circulating materials describe this research as "a 2026 meta-analysis including 35 studies and about 1,550 participants." On verification it is a **2024** publication, a **systematic review with evidence mapping** rather than a meta-analysis, including **22** studies. Year, design, and sample size are all three wrong. This is the most typical error found—**a figure precise in its specifics but incorrect**, likelier to slip past a reader than a vague generalization.
>
> Two sentences must therefore be strictly distinguished. **VR can help a person enter a certain environment or state of attention**—this can be studied, and preliminary evidence exists. **VR can produce meditative absorption, enlightenment, or fruition**—this is not a scientific proposition.
>
> Brain electrical activity, heart rate variability, respiration, heart rate, sleep, attention, physiological feedback, neurofeedback—all worth studying. But current evidence **does not support "using these indicators to identify advanced Buddhist states of practice."** A 2024 systematic review of mindfulness-based neurofeedback found that EEG and fMRI studies still suffer from inadequate sample sizes, control designs, and attention to transfer effects, and notably lack adequate sham-neurofeedback controls; neural signal changes therefore cannot simply be interpreted as higher levels of attainment (Grade B, Treves, Greene, Bajwa et al., final version in *Imaging Neuroscience* 2:imag-2-00396, 2024; the PubMed record cited in circulating materials is a preprint).
>
> The sound direction is **"physiological feedback as training support,"** not **"EEG measurement of attainment."**
>
> The distinction bears on more than scientific rigour. It bears on the teaching itself. **To equate a state of practice with a physiological index is to replace something unmeasurable with something measurable—and what gets replaced is precisely the practice.** (Judgment)
>
> **Twenty-Three: The Project Landscape.** Five directions are worth attention; figures and links are in Appendix Three.
>
> **BDRC** is the Buddhist digital foundation. Its approach is not to build a chatbot but to build high-quality Buddhist data first. Its 2026 open-dataset initiative deserves particular attention.
>
> **CBETA + DILA + SAT** form the digital scholarly foundation for Chinese Buddhism, representing respectively structured canonical texts, integrated search, and edition and page verification. Further integration of the three is well worth pursuing.
>
> **Dharmamitra / MITRA** has made multilingual Buddhist search, textual reuse detection, and machine translation explicit research directions, moving from experimental demonstration into scholarly production.
>
> **84000** offers a paradigm worth borrowing: AI as an enhancement tool for a professional translation team, not a replacement for professional translators.
>
> **A newer generation of Buddhist RAG and knowledge-graph projects** shows the "Buddhist search engine" evolving toward an "AI research environment," but the scale, accuracy, and data coverage of some remain project self-descriptions, and **cannot currently be equated with long-standing professional projects such as CBETA, SAT, and BDRC.**
>
> Chinese Buddhism's own moves have begun: Foguang Shan's "Humanistic Cloud" (announced 3 February 2025, with the *Complete Works of Master Hsing Yun* and twenty-five years of the *Merit Times* archive as its corpus), the Vurbo.ai terminology system, Lingyin Temple's OCR work and the Rushi Fodian platform, and Dharma Drum's knowledge graphs. These are real advances and a good beginning; their scale remains small beside CBETA or BDRC.
>
> **A strategic judgment follows: Chinese Buddhism need not, and cannot, build everything itself.** What it needs is to become irreplaceable in the few things only it can do, and to connect to others' infrastructure for the rest. (Judgment)
>
> What does only Chinese Buddhism have? Authoritative editions and critical collations of the Chinese canon; the doctrinal systems and terminologies of its schools; two thousand years of commentary; the complete bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage; and the modern practical experience of Humanistic Buddhism. Everything else—OCR engines, vector databases, RAG frameworks, large models—should be adopted off the shelf, not rebuilt.
>
> **Twenty-Four: A Five- to Ten-Year Roadmap.** Reduced to a timetable, the preceding judgments fall into three stages; details are in Appendix Three.
>
> **2026–2027: the data foundation stage.** Priorities are coordination among CBETA, DILA, and SAT; authoritative canonical data standards; knowledge graphs of persons, temples, and lineages; a multilingual Buddhist terminology database; edition and citation standards; high-quality OCR; multilingual parallel corpora; and professional RAG. The most important thing at this stage is not "intelligence" but **ensuring the data can be trusted.**
>
> **2028–2030: the knowledge intelligence stage.** AI research assistants for Buddhist studies, AI translation workbenches, multilingual intelligent retrieval, scholarly literature agents, Buddhist knowledge graphs, personal Buddhist knowledge bases, AI course assistants, automatic multilingual subtitling, AI digital museums, and a global Buddhist knowledge portal. The key change is **a shift from "users search the canon" to "users converse with Buddhist knowledge."**
>
> **2031–2035: the intelligent ecosystem stage.** Only if the data and governance foundations of the first two stages succeed can there be multimodal Buddhist AI, lifelong personal study-mentor systems, AI-and-VR Buddhist education, automatic adaptation of global multilingual Buddhist courses, cross-traditional comparative research agents, virtual reconstruction of Buddhist history, personalized learning paths, and an international Buddhist knowledge network.
>
> Even in the third stage, one baseline should hold: **AI is a tool, an assistant, a medium; human teachers, scholars, monastic communities, and real communities remain the core.**
>
> **Six principles.** **One: canon first.** Every significant doctrinal conclusion should return to the canon wherever possible. **Two: source first.** Answers must provide, wherever possible, the title, fascicle, chapter or page, and edition. **Three: stratify by tradition.** The system must not blend early Buddhism, Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, Tiantai, Huayan, Chan, and Pure Land into a single "unified Buddhist answer." **Four: transparent interpretation.** It must tell users: this is canonical text, this is classical commentary, this is modern scholarship, this is AI synthesis. **Five: human mentor in the loop.** Where precepts, psychological crisis, intense religious experience, teacher–student relations, medical questions, or high-risk practice are involved, human intervention must be provided. **Six: data minimization.** Practice journals, personal histories, emotional and sensitive identity data should follow a principle of minimal collection, not be gathered without limit "to build a personalized AI."
>
> **Ten evaluation metrics.** Conventional AI tests only whether an answer sounds right. Buddhist AI should test ten things:
>
> | Metric | What it measures |
> |---|---|
> | Canonical accuracy | Does the cited scripture actually exist |
> | Source accuracy | Are fascicle, page, chapter, and edition correct |
> | Edition recognition | Are different editions conflated |
> | School discrimination | Are traditions correctly distinguished |
> | Quotation completeness | Is context omitted |
> | Translation accuracy | Is terminology stable |
> | Hallucination rate | Are nonexistent scriptures, figures, or quotations generated |
> | Traceability | Can the user click back to the canon |
> | Expert agreement | Do Buddhist scholars accept the answer |
> | Multilingual consistency | Do Chinese, English, Japanese, and Korean versions agree in core meaning |
>
> **Not one of the ten is "how wise the answer sounds."** That is not an oversight but a position: the pass mark for Buddhist AI should be set by verifiable accuracy, not by rhetorical appeal. (Judgment)

# 第四部　应该如何

> 前面三部是诊断。这一部是处方。
>
> 处方有一条基本纪律：**先看清楚手里有什么，再决定做什么。** 汉传讨论自身前途时，最容易走的弯路，是把力气全用在补短上：不如藏传有系统，不如南传直接，不如日本学术化。补到最后，变成一个处处像别人、处处不像自己的东西。
>
> 所以这一部从资产盘点开始，而不是从问题清单开始。

### 以何推进：这些处方凭什么算是佛法的解法

下面的建议大多是具体的、琐屑的：建术语库、写年报、编教材、设计课程阶梯、培养审校的人。它们看起来像管理咨询，不像佛法。所以先说明它们背后的道理——**若这些建议不落在佛法的见地上，它们就只是一套经营策略，做与不做都不影响根本。**

**其一，缘起。** 问题不是命定的，是条件的产物。汉传今天的困境有它具体的条件：母体的社会结构变了、语言隔了一层、监督机制没有长出来、典籍没有被翻译。**改条件就是改结果。**（判断）这条见地看似平常，实则决定了整份处方的性质——它反对两种态度：一种是「末法时代本该如此」的宿命论，一种是「人心不古」的道德论。两者都把问题归给不可改变的东西，因而都不产生行动。

**其二，惭愧。** 面对已经发生的失败，佛法给的第一反应不是辩解，也不是自罚，是惭愧——承认它，然后改。这个传统在自我陈述上有一个习惯：把道场数目算得宽一些，把信众数目估得高一些，把「覆盖多少国家」与「有多少会员」混着说。**一个把自家典籍数量说大了近一倍的传统，在别人指出之前，并没有觉得自己在说什么不实的话。** 这不是某一个人的品行问题，是一种集体性的、善意的、因此也更难纠正的自宽。惭愧所对的，正是这种自宽。

**其三，方便。** 形式可随方就圆，核心不可动摇。第三十章那条「为了让人进来，还是为了好卖」的判准，依据就是方便与随俗的分野——方便是为了让法到达，随俗是为了让自己好过。

**其四，不二。** 传统与现代、僧与俗、自利与利他、出世与入世，在汉传的见地里本不是对立的两边。因此第二十六章「从输出教团转向提供公共产品」不是退让，第二十八章「把传承交给本地人」也不是失去——**若它们是对立的两边，这些建议就都是牺牲；正因它们不是，这些建议才是出路。**（判断）

**其五，无尽。** 这件事没有完成的一天。众生无尽，烦恼无尽，所以与之相应的工程也无尽。这也是本文只给出「五至十年路线」而不给「蓝图」的原因——蓝图意味着竣工，而这件事不会竣工。

五条见地放在这里，是希望读者在读到后面那些琐屑的建议时，不至于以为它们与佛法无关。**恰恰相反：正因为问题在条件上，所以要在条件上解决；正因为要惭愧，所以要把数字改对；正因为有方便，所以形式可以变；正因为是不二，所以不必在传统与现代之间选边；正因为无尽，所以可以慢慢做。**

## 二十五、先看手里有什么

汉传手里有七样东西，是别人没有或不全有的。

**第一，汉文大藏经。** 这是世界上体量最大的佛典语料之一。更需要说明的是它的独特价值：**大量印度本土已失传的论典，只保存在汉译里。** 唯识、中观、说一切有部的大批论书，梵文原典或已佚失、或只存残片，而汉译本完整。这一点的分量，学界清楚，教内反而常常不清楚。

**第二，完整的比丘尼戒统。** 这是全球唯一，前文已详。

**第三，教下诸宗的义理体系。** 天台的判教与一念三千、华严的法界缘起与十玄门、唯识的八识与三性——这是佛教思想史上最精密的哲学建构之一，其复杂度与系统性，与任何哲学传统相比都不逊色。

**第四，两千年的注疏传统。** 这是一个被严重低估的资产。历代注疏不只是「解释」，它是一部完整的**理解史**——同一部经，在不同朝代被怎样理解、争论、修正。对当代诠释学、对跨文化理解理论，这是极好的原始材料。

**第五，人间佛教的现代化经验。** 一个传统如何在不丢本质的前提下完成现代化？汉传做了一百年，有成有败，有佛光山、慈济这样的成功样本，也有少林这样的教训。**这份经验在全球范围内是稀缺的**——藏传在现代化上走的弯路不多，因为它进入西方时已经是「异域传统」的身份；南传的现代化主要发生在禅修技术的市场化上。**只有汉传完整地经历过「一个古老传统如何在现代社会中重新找到自己的位置」这件事，并且积累了一百年的账。**（判断）

**第六，丛林制度。** 一个自我管理、自我维持、自我传承的僧团共同体制度。现代组织理论对它的兴趣，比对它的了解大得多。

**第七，华严与天台的思想资源。** 「事事无碍」对系统思维与生态哲学，「一念三千」对心与环境的关系，唯识的「识」对意识研究——这些是汉传对当代议题最有对话潜力的部分。前面第九章说过，这些恰恰是海外最少被讲清楚的。

七样里，六样是**内容**，只有一样是**制度**（戒统）。而内容型资产有一个共同的瓶颈：**要被世界取用，必须先过语言这一关。**

这个盘点得到一个明确的战略结论：**汉传的优势在「深」，不在「广」；在「内容」，不在「渠道」。** 因此它的正确策略不是铺摊子，是**把深厚的东西变成别人拿得走、用得上的形态**。（判断）

## 二十六、定位：从输出教团到提供公共产品

汉传在海外最被认可、争议最小的贡献，几乎无一例外是非传教性的公共产品：比丘尼戒统、慈济的人道与医疗、法鼓山的禅修训练与居士传承、佛经翻译委员会的翻译与出版、大学与学术合作。

这不是偶然，也不是退而求其次。它有一条很深的历史根据。

**佛教入华，靠的从来不是传教团。** 它靠的是译经——一种纯粹的公共知识产品；靠的是医疗、赈济、义学、造桥铺路。汉地接受佛教的方式很特别：先用上了这些东西，很久以后才慢慢接受它背后的东西。没有谁曾被一个教团说服。

**换句话说，汉传自己就是「公共产品路线」的产物。** 它今天在海外最有效的方式，恰恰是它当年被接受的方式。（判断）

由此得出一条具体建议：**对外传播的基本单位，应当从「分院」改为「模块」。**

一座海外分院，是一个需要长期供养、需要僧才、需要当地信众的组织实体，它的成败取决于太多不可控因素。而一个「模块」是可复制、可授权、可独立运转的单元。可以考虑的模块包括：

**英语禅七课程。** 标准化、可复制、可供不同道场使用，附带完整的教材、仪轨说明与带领者培训。这需要重新设计一套为英语使用者从零开始的课程，不是把中文课程翻译一遍。

**国际传戒合作机制。** 汉传手里唯一的完整比丘尼戒统，可以在多边框架下向有此需要的传统开放——一九九八年菩提伽耶戒会已经开了一个头，但它是一次性的，尚未机制化。

**灾害救援与跨宗教伙伴机制。** 慈济的模式已经证明可行：以无宗派偏见的救援行动进入非佛教社会，赢得信任，再让这份信任自然回流到佛教本身。

**术语库与译经平台。** 见第二十七章。

**可认证的学修课程。** 见第二十八章。

**寺院治理模板。** 财务披露、独立申诉、交棒程序的现成范本，供新建道场参考。这一模块看起来最不「佛教」，实际上最解决海外传播的实际障碍——**信任。**

把传播单位从「分院」改成「模块」，还带来一个额外好处：**它绕开了政治与身份的关口。** 一个道场是「某个地方的佛教据点」，容易被放进「文化外交」的框架里审视；一套救援机制、一个术语库、一门课程，是工具。工具不问来处。

## 二十七、语言与译经：第一优先

如果要为汉传的全球传播排一个优先级，第一位没有悬念。

理由很直接：**没有可读的译本，禅修教学、教育、学术对话、跨宗教交流，全部无从谈起。** 第十章与第十九章已经从两个方向论证过——汉文佛典绝大多数没有英译，而 AI 已经把这件事的成本降到了历史最低。

具体该做三件事。

**第一，建立分层的术语标准。** 要建的是一个**开放的、可引用的、有版本号的术语层**，不是一本「标准词典」。它至少要允许多译并存，每个译名标注来源（谁译的、哪个时代、依据什么），并且有版本号可供引用。学者写论文时可以注「据某某术语库二〇二七版」——这句话是学术规范的前提。**没有版本号，就没有人敢引；没有人引，它就不成为标准。**（判断）

**第二，AI 初译加专家校订，加分级标注。** 流程见第十九章。这里要补的是**分级标注制度**：每一份译文都标明它经过了几道人工校订——未校、已校、权威三级。这个制度看起来只是质量标注，实际上它解决了一个更根本的问题：**它让「不完美」变得可用。** 如果没有分级，一份机器译文要么被当作成品（危险），要么被弃置不用（浪费）。有了分级，未校译文可以作为研究线索、作为初稿、作为检索入口，同时读者知道自己在用什么。

**第三，建立覆盖率仪表盘。** 把 BDK 那个「百分之六十五」变成可审计的指标。这件事的技术难度很低，组织难度很高，因为它要求各方公开自己的进度。但它的价值与难度成正比：**没有这把尺子，翻译规划就只能凭感觉排序。**

### 一件被忽略的配套工作

译经之外，还有一件同样重要却几乎无人做的事：**写教科书。**

藏传在西方的成功，很大程度上靠的是成体系的英文教材——从入门到进阶，有课本、有讲义、有术语表、有练习，学校的课程可以直接采用。汉传在英文世界里几乎没有这样的东西。一位美国大学的宗教学教师想开一门汉传佛教的课，找不到合适的教科书；一位对汉传感兴趣的西方读者想系统入门，找不到一本讲得清楚的导论。

**结果是：汉传在西方学界的「入口」极窄。** 学者可以研究它，但学生进不来；学生进不来，二十年后就没有学者了。

所以有一个建议，值得排在译经之后、其他所有事情之前：**组织一批真正的学者与法师，写三五本英文的汉传佛教入门教材与课程包**——一本通史、一本教理导论、一本禅修实践手册、一本术语详解。用最好的英文写，配最高质量的中文引文，由大学出版社出版。**这件事投入不大，杠杆极高。**（判断）

### 一个反直觉的判断

很多人认为，AI 翻译普及之后，译经人才的需求会下降。本文的判断相反：**需求会上升。**

原因是翻译工作分两段。AI 能胜任的是「初译」这一段——把古汉语转成通顺的现代语言。而「审校」这一段不但不能被替代，反而变得更关键：当机器每天产出成千上万段初稿时，**唯一稀缺的是能够判断「这段译文对不对」的人。**

而判断一段佛典译文对不对，需要的恰恰是最难培养的能力：通古汉语、通梵藏、通教理、通宗派差异、还要通目标语言。**AI 让初译变便宜，等于让审校变得更贵。**（判断）

这对僧伽教育的含义是具体的：如果十年后佛典翻译的瓶颈在审校，那么今天开始的译经人才培养，方向应当是**培养能审校的人**，而不是培养能初译的人。

## 二十八、人才：学修次第与交棒

海外道场最缺什么？前面引的那份教内调研给出的答案是：**弘法人员的外语能力。**

这个答案往下追一层，会追到一个更根本的缺口：**汉传缺一套可以用现代语言说明白、并且可以教、可以学、可以验证的学修次第。**

对照是明显的。藏传有一部五部大论、一个格西学位、一套辩经制度——任何一个对藏传感兴趣的人，都能立刻知道「学藏传要学什么、学多久、学到什么程度算数」。南传有巴利三藏、有戒定慧的明确次第、有内观禅修的标准化课程。而一个想问「学汉传要学什么」的人，得到的回答往往是「看你跟哪个师父、进哪个法门」。

**这个答案在教内是成立的，对教外是无效的。** 因为教外的人要的是入口，而「看你跟哪个师父」不是入口，是入口之后的事。

### 汉传其实不缺次第

这里有一个重要的辨正：汉传不是没有次第，是**没有把次第整理成对外的形态**。

汉传手里有的是什么？智顗的《摩诃止观》，把止观修行的次第讲得极细；杜顺的《五教止观》，把五教与观行的对应关系梳理清楚；宗密在《禅源诸诠集都序》里对三种禅（息妄修心宗、泯绝无寄宗、直显心性宗）的判摄；法藏、澄观对法界观的层层展开；净土宗对信愿行与九品往生的次第安立。**这些是相当系统的学修架构，其精细程度不输任何传统。**

问题在于，它们大多以古汉语书写、以传统注疏体例呈现、预设读者已有相当的佛学基础。要变成「一位英语使用者可以从零开始跟着走的路」，需要一次彻底的再表述：把次第抽出来，把次第所依据的教理讲清楚，把每一阶段的目标、方法、验证方式写明白。

这不是降低标准，是**换一种方式说同一件事**。

### 三条人才路径

**本地出生的教师。** 这需要时间，但方向必须明确：培养本地人成为教师，而不只是培养本地人成为信众。法鼓山把法脉传给西方人，是目前走得最远的一步；其余教团在这一步上仍多犹豫。

**居士领导。** 汉传的传统是僧主俗从，但在西方语境下，居士在组织、学术、传播上的作用远大于传统格局所允许的。法鼓山的居士法嗣路径已经开了一个口子。这里的分寸需要谨慎——居士承担组织与教学，与僧团承担传法与戒法，是两条不同的线，不宜混同，但两条线都需要有人走。

**双语僧才。** 这是最缺的，也是投入最大的。一所国际化的佛学院，二十年才能产出稳定的双语僧才队伍。**没有捷径。**（判断）

### 交棒

圣严二〇〇九年、惟觉二〇一六年、星云二〇二三年相继圆寂，证严已近九十岁。

这些教团的组织能力都很强，但它们的权威结构都建立在创办学人的个人德行与魅力之上。**这不是缺陷，是正常的历史阶段**——所有的宗教组织都经历过创始人时代。问题只在于，创始人之后会发生什么。

制度上的答案并不复杂：董事会制、多住持制、明确的任期与继承程序、独立的监督机制。难的是两个地方：一是**权力下放的真实性**——制度可以写下来，权柄可以不放；二是**宗教权威与组织权力的区分**——住持的权威来自戒定慧与僧团印可，董事的权威来自章程与选举，这两者在丛林制度里从未分开过，需要重新厘清。

> **EN对应 · Part Four: What Should Be Done**
>
> *The first three parts are diagnosis. This part is prescription. One discipline governs it: **look clearly at what we hold before deciding what to do.** The most common error Chinese Buddhism makes about its own future is not a lack of confidence but spending all its effort on making up shortfalls—less systematic than Tibetan, less direct than Theravāda, less academic than Japanese. Make up all three and what results looks like everyone and like nothing.*
>
> ### On What Basis These Prescriptions Count as Dharma
>
> **First, dependent origination.** Problems are not fated; they are products of conditions. The predicament has its specific conditions: the social structure of the mother body has changed; language has become a barrier; oversight mechanisms never grew; the canon was never translated. **Changing the conditions changes the outcome.** (Judgment) The insight looks ordinary; in fact it determines the character of the whole prescription—it rejects two attitudes: the fatalism of "the dharma-ending age is simply like this," and the moralism of "people are not what they were." Both assign the problem to something unchangeable, and so neither produces action.
>
> **Second, shame (慚愧).** Confronted with a failure already past, the first Buddhist response is neither self-justification nor self-punishment but shame—acknowledge it, then correct it. This tradition has a habit in its self-description: counting its temples generously, estimating its adherents upward, running "present in N countries" together with "how many members." **A tradition that overstated its own canonical holdings by nearly double did not, before being told, feel it was saying anything untrue.** This is not a question of anyone's character. It is a collective, well-intentioned, and therefore harder-to-correct self-indulgence. Shame is what such self-indulgence answers to.
>
> **Third, skillful means (方便).** Forms may bend to circumstance; the core may not be moved. The criterion in Chapter Thirty—"so the other person can come in, or so that we can sell better"—rests on the distinction between skillful means and accommodation: skillful means gets the dharma across; accommodation makes things easier for oneself.
>
> **Fourth, non-duality (不二).** Tradition and modernity, monastic and lay, self-benefit and benefit of others, supramundane and engaged—in Chinese Buddhist understanding these were never opposing sides. Hence "from exporting orders to providing public goods" (Chapter Twenty-Six) is not a retreat, and "handing the transmission to local people" (Chapter Twenty-Eight) is not a loss. **Were they opposing sides, these proposals would be sacrifices; precisely because they are not, they are the way out.** (Judgment)
>
> **Fifth, inexhaustibility (無盡).** This work has no day of completion. Sentient beings are inexhaustible, afflictions are inexhaustible, and the work that answers them is inexhaustible too. This is why the report offers a "five- to ten-year roadmap" rather than a "blueprint"—a blueprint implies completion, and this will not be completed.
>
> **Twenty-Five: Take Stock of What We Hold.** Chinese Buddhism holds seven things that others lack, wholly or in part.
>
> **First, the Chinese Buddhist canon**—one of the largest corpora of Buddhist literature in the world. Its distinctive value needs stating: **a great many treatises lost in India survive only in Chinese translation.** Large numbers of Yogācāra, Madhyamaka, and Sarvāstivāda works exist in complete Chinese versions while their Sanskrit originals are lost or survive only in fragments. Scholars know the weight of this. Within the tradition itself it is often not known.
>
> **Second, the complete bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage.** Unique in the world, as described earlier.
>
> **Third, the doctrinal systems of the schools**—Tiantai's classification and its "three thousand realms in a single thought"; Huayan's dharma-realm origination and ten profound gates; Yogācāra's eight consciousnesses and three natures. Among the most rigorous philosophical construction in Buddhist intellectual history, standing comparison with any philosophical tradition.
>
> **Fourth, two thousand years of commentary.** A badly underrated asset. Successive commentaries are not merely "explanation"; they constitute a complete **history of understanding**—how the same scripture was understood, argued over, and revised across dynasties. For contemporary hermeneutics and for theories of cross-cultural understanding, exceptional primary material.
>
> **Fifth, a century of experience in modernizing.** How does a tradition modernize without losing its essence? Chinese Buddhism has been at this for a hundred years, with successes and failures—Foguang Shan and Tzu Chi as successes, Shaolin as a caution. **That experience is scarce globally.** Tibetan Buddhism took few detours in modernization, because when it entered the West it arrived already marked as an exotic tradition; Theravāda's modernization occurred mainly in the marketization of a meditation technique. **Only Chinese Buddhism has been through the whole of "how does an ancient tradition find its place again in a modern society," and has a century of accounts to show for it.** (Judgment)
>
> **Sixth, the monastic system**—a self-governing, self-sustaining, self-transmitting communal institution. Modern organizational theory is more interested in it than informed about it.
>
> **Seventh, the intellectual resources of Huayan and Tiantai.** "Unobstructed interpenetration of phenomena" speaks to systems thinking and ecological philosophy; "three thousand realms in a single thought" to the relation between mind and environment; Yogācāra's "consciousness" to consciousness research. As Chapter Nine noted, these are precisely what is least clearly explained abroad.
>
> Of the seven, six are **content** and only one is **institution**. And content assets share one bottleneck: **to be taken up by the world, they must first pass through language.**
>
> The inventory yields a clear strategic conclusion: **Chinese Buddhism's strength lies in depth, not breadth; in content, not channels.** Its correct strategy is therefore not to spread wide but to **turn deep things into forms others can carry away and use.** (Judgment)
>
> **Twenty-Six: From Exporting Orders to Providing Public Goods.** The contributions for which Chinese Buddhism is most recognized abroad and least contested are almost without exception non-proselytizing public goods: the bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage, Tzu Chi's humanitarian and medical work, Dharma Drum's meditation training and lay transmission, the Buddhist Text Translation Society's translation and publishing, and university and scholarly collaboration.
>
> This is neither accident nor second best. It has a deep historical basis.
>
> **Buddhism entered China not through missionary orders** but through translation—a pure public knowledge good—and through medicine, famine relief, education, and road and bridge building. China received Buddhism not by being persuaded by a mission but by **using what it provided, and only later accepting what lay behind it.**
>
> **In other words, Chinese Buddhism is itself a product of the public goods route.** Its most effective method abroad today is precisely the method by which it was once received. (Judgment)
>
> Hence a concrete proposal: **the basic unit of transmission abroad should shift from the branch temple to the module.**
>
> An overseas branch temple requires long-term support, monastic personnel, and a local congregation; its fate depends on too many uncontrollable factors. A module is copyable, licensable, and able to run independently. Candidate modules: **English-language Chan retreat curricula** (standardized, replicable, usable by different temples, with complete materials, ritual explanations, and leader training—not the translation of an existing Chinese course but the redesign of a course for English speakers starting from zero); **an international ordination cooperation mechanism** (the complete bhikṣuṇī lineage could be opened, in a multilateral framework, to traditions that need it; the 1998 Bodh Gaya ordination made a beginning but was a one-off and has not been institutionalized); **disaster relief and interfaith partnership mechanisms** (the Tzu Chi model has proven workable—entering non-Buddhist societies through relief delivered without sectarian preference, earning trust, and letting that trust flow back naturally to Buddhism itself); **a terminology database and translation platform** (Chapter Twenty-Seven); **certifiable study programmes** (Chapter Twenty-Eight); and **temple governance templates** (ready models for financial disclosure, independent grievance procedures, and succession—the module that looks the least "Buddhist" and in practice addresses the most practical obstacle: **trust**).
>
> Shifting the unit from branch temple to module brings a further advantage: **it bypasses the gateways of politics and identity.** A temple is "a Buddhist foothold somewhere," readily examined within a framework of cultural diplomacy. A relief mechanism, a terminology database, a course—these are tools. Tools are not asked where they come from.
>
> **Twenty-Seven: Language and Translation — The First Priority.** If priorities for Chinese Buddhism's global transmission must be ranked, the first has no competitor. The reason is direct: **without readable translations, meditation instruction, education, scholarly dialogue, and interreligious exchange all have nowhere to begin.** Chapters Ten and Nineteen have argued this from two directions—the overwhelming majority of the Chinese canon has no English translation, and AI has brought the cost of changing this to a historic low.
>
> Three things should be done. **First, build a layered terminology standard**—not "a standard dictionary" but an **open, citable, versioned terminology layer**: it must permit coexisting renderings; each rendering must carry its source (who translated it, in what period, on what basis); and it must be versioned and citable. A scholar should be able to write "according to Terminology Database X, 2027 edition." **Without a version number no one dares cite it; uncited, it never becomes a standard.** (Judgment)
>
> **Second, AI first draft plus expert revision, plus graded labelling.** Every translation should state how many rounds of human review it has passed—unreviewed, reviewed, authoritative. The system looks like quality labelling. In fact it solves a deeper problem: **it makes imperfection usable.** Without grading, a machine translation is either treated as finished product (dangerous) or discarded (wasteful). With grading, an unreviewed translation can serve as a research lead, a first draft, or a search entry point—while the reader knows what he is using.
>
> **Third, a coverage dashboard.** Turn BDK's "65 percent" into an auditable metric. Technically easy, organizationally hard, because it requires all parties to disclose their progress. But its value is proportional to its difficulty: **without that ruler, translation planning can only be ordered by feel.**
>
> **A companion task that gets overlooked: writing textbooks.** Tibetan Buddhism's success in the West rests heavily on systematic English teaching materials, adoptable directly by university courses. Chinese Buddhism has almost nothing of the kind. **The result: the entry point into Chinese Buddhism in Western academia is very narrow.** Scholars can study it, but students cannot get in; and if students cannot get in, in twenty years there will be no scholars.
>
> Hence a recommendation that deserves to rank immediately after translation and ahead of everything else: **organize a group of genuine scholars and monastics to write three to five English introductory textbooks and course packages for Chinese Buddhism**—a history, an introduction to doctrine, a practice manual, and a glossary. **Small investment, very high leverage.** (Judgment)
>
> **One counter-intuitive judgment.** Many assume that as AI translation spreads, the demand for translation talent will fall. This report judges the opposite: **demand will rise.** Translation has two stages: what AI handles well is the first; the second, review, becomes more critical, because when a machine produces thousands of draft passages a day, **the only scarce thing is a person able to judge whether a given rendering is correct.** And judging whether a Buddhist translation is correct demands the hardest capacities to train: command of classical Chinese, of Sanskrit and Tibetan, of doctrine, of differences among schools, and of the target language. **AI makes first drafts cheap—which makes review more expensive.** (Judgment) If in ten years the bottleneck is review, then the training begun today should aim at **producing reviewers, not producers of first drafts.**
>
> **Twenty-Eight: Personnel — Study Sequences and Succession.** What do overseas temples lack most? The in-house survey cited earlier answers: **foreign-language ability among those who teach the dharma.** Follow that down one level and it reaches a more fundamental gap: **Chinese Buddhism lacks a sequence of study and practice that can be explained in modern language, and that can be taught, learned, and verified.**
>
> The contrast is stark. Tibetan Buddhism has the five great treatises, the geshe degree, and a debate curriculum—anyone interested can learn at once what one studies, for how long, and to what standard. Theravāda has the Pali canon, an explicit sequence of precepts, concentration, and wisdom, and standardized vipassanā courses. A person asking "what does one study in Chinese Buddhism" is typically told, "it depends which teacher you follow and which practice door you enter."
>
> **That answer works inside the tradition. It does not work outside it.** An outsider wants an entry point, and "it depends which teacher you follow" is not an entry point. It is what comes after one.
>
> **Chinese Buddhism is not actually short of sequences.** Zhiyi's *Great Calming and Contemplation* lays out the stages of practice in extraordinary detail. Dushun's *Five Teachings of Calming and Contemplation* maps the correspondence between the five teachings and contemplative practice. Zongmi's *Preface to the Collected Writings on the Source of Chan* surveys three kinds of Chan. Fazang and Chengguan unfold the contemplation of the dharma-realm layer by layer. Pure Land establishes the sequence of faith, vow, and practice with the nine grades of rebirth. **These are highly systematic architectures, as finely articulated as any tradition's.**
>
> The problem is that they are mostly written in classical Chinese, presented in the form of traditional commentary, and assume a reader who already has considerable background. Turning them into "a path an English speaker can follow from zero" requires a thorough restatement: extract the sequence, explain the doctrine it rests on, and set out clearly the goal, method, and mode of verification at each stage. This is not a lowering of standards. It is **saying the same thing in another way.**
>
> **Three paths for personnel.** **Locally born teachers**—this takes time, but the direction must be clear: train local people to become teachers, not merely followers. Dharma Drum's transmission of the lineage to Westerners is the furthest anyone has gone; other orders remain hesitant. **Lay leadership**—Chinese Buddhist tradition puts monastics first and laity after, but in Western contexts laypeople play a far larger role than the traditional arrangement allows; the balance requires care, since laypeople taking on organization and teaching, and the sangha taking on transmission and precepts, are two different lines that should not be conflated but both need people walking them. **Bilingual monastic talent**—the scarcest and most expensive; an international Buddhist college needs twenty years to produce a stable cohort. **There is no shortcut.** (Judgment)
>
> **Succession.** Sheng Yen died in 2009, Weijue in 2016, Hsing Yun in 2023, and Cheng Yen is nearly ninety. These orders are organizationally strong, but each rests its authority on the founder's personal virtue and charisma. **This is not a defect; it is a normal historical stage**—every religious organization passes through a founder era. The only question is what follows.
>
> The institutional answers are not complicated: a board, multiple abbots, explicit terms and succession procedures, independent oversight. The difficulty lies in two places. One is **whether the delegation of power is genuine**—institutions can be written down while the reins are not released. The other is **distinguishing religious authority from organizational authority**—an abbot's authority comes from precepts, concentration, and wisdom and from the sangha's sanction; a trustee's comes from bylaws and election. In the monastic system the two were never separated and now must be disentangled.

## 二十九、治理与寺院经济

### 三条制度补课

第六章已说明：过去十年两起最大的事件，暴露的都不是道德问题。它们暴露的是一件更基本的事：**监督机制未能运转**。补课的方向因此也是制度的。

**第一，财务审计披露。** 这一条听起来最世俗，实际上最要紧。做法不必复杂：统一的年报格式、定期公布、可被外部核对。**不需要做到上市公司的透明度，只需要做到「外界能看懂、能追问」。** 一个愿意公布年报的寺院，与一个不愿意的寺院，在海外募款与建立信任上的差别是实质性的。

**第二，独立申诉渠道。** 学诚案始于两名前僧人的举报；举报之所以外流，是因为内部没有可走的路。所谓独立，不必然是外部机构介入——它可以是一个由不同寺院僧众共同组成、独立于被申诉人所在寺院的评议机制。

**第三，交棒机制。** 见第二十八章。

第六章已经指出一个方向：汉传要补的这一课，答案在自家的戒律里。**把丛林制度里本来就有的东西重新长出来**，就够了。汉传戒律里的羯磨法，本就是一套决策与处理程序：什么事需要白一羯磨，什么事需要白四羯磨，犯了什么程度的戒当如何处置，都有明文。**这套程序比许多现代组织的议事规则更精密，问题只在它长期未被用于财务与人事这类新问题上。**（判断）

### 寺院经济：从香火到公共产品

商业化的问题，往往被简化成「不要贪钱」的道德劝诫。这个劝诫是对的，但不解决问题，因为寺院要维持，就必须有经济来源。

真正的问题因此变成：**依赖什么样的经济来源，是可持续的、也是不损害宗教性的？**

**香火经济**依赖人流与仪式服务：门票、香火、法会、超度、纪念品。它的特点是现金、即时、与人流强绑定，因此天然趋向商业化——人流是可以营销的，营销需要投入，投入需要回报，回报需要更多人流量。这个循环一旦启动，很难自行停止。少林就是走到了这个循环的端点上。

**公共产品经济**是另一条路：教育（佛学院、课程、教材）、出版（译经、书籍、音像）、文化（展览、艺术、学术活动）、社会服务（慈善、医疗、临终关怀）。这些可以收费，且收费不损害宗教性——因为收费的理由是「提供了有价值的服务」，不是「你拜了就要给钱」。

这条路的难点在于它**要求人才**。香火经济只需要场地与仪式；公共产品经济需要教师、编辑、译者、管理者。**这就是为什么商业化如此难以摆脱：它是低门槛的默认选项。**（判断）

### 把寺庙热接住

大陆的青年「寺庙热」（见第十一章）是这十年来最大的一个入口，而它正在被浪费。

不是没有人在做。据腾讯佛教频道，二〇一四年以来已有一百余座寺院举办过主题禅修营。但相对于三万三千多座有僧团的寺院，一百余座是极小的比例；而这些禅修营大多是一次性活动，活动结束，参与者也就散了。

**可以设计的是一条阶梯，而不是一场活动。**

**入门**：一次禅修营、一门线上课程、一本写得明白的小册子。目标是让对方理解「佛教在讲什么」，而不是要求他接受。

**持续**：固定的每周共修、可以反复参加的课程、有反馈的学习小组。**这一级是全部阶梯里最关键的一级，也是目前最缺的一级。**（判断）从「偶尔来」到「每周来」，中间隔着的不是兴趣，是**一个他认识的、会问他「上周怎么没来」的人**。

**深入**：系统的教理学习、禅修进阶、经论研读。

**承担**：成为志工、成为教学的辅助者、成为社群的组织者。

这条阶梯的每一级都可以做得很好，也可以虚应故事。区别只在于：**有没有人真的在管这件事。** 而这就要求寺院里有一个岗位，专门负责「来的人下一步去哪里」。目前多数寺院没有这个岗位。

## 三十、本土化的边界

星云大师的「走出去→国际化→本土化」是汉传海外传播最清晰的一条路线。它成立，而且有佛光山自身作为证据。

但本土化有一个从未被认真回答的问题：**边界在哪里？**

### 一个实用的检验

先给一个判准，它比任何理论都实用：

**看这个改变是为了「让对方能进来」，还是为了「让自己好卖」。**

前者是本土化，后者是稀释。设一个英文共修班，是为了让不懂中文的人能开始——这是前者；把禅修课包装成减压课程、把因果轮回讲成心理暗示，是为了好卖——这是后者。这个判准的好处是它可操作：每一次调整都可以问一遍。

### 可变与不可变

**可以变的**——语言（用英语、西班牙语、越南语说法）、组织形式（董事会、基金会、公司化的管理结构）、仪式的表达方式（唱念的语言与曲调、法会的流程编排）、教育方法（课程化、学期制、线上教学）、生活起居（饮食、衣着、作息对当地的适应）。

**不可变的**——戒律的核心（四根本戒无所谓文化差异）、传承的完整性（法脉不能为了好看而拼凑）、义理的基本判断（不能为了迎合而把「无我」讲成「找到真我」）。

这条线不是绝对的，中间有大量需要判断的地带。但有一类改变，是明确越界的：**当改变的目的是让受众不必改变自己时。** 佛法的传播从来要求某种改变——不是文化上的改变，是见解与行为上的改变。一个不需要任何人改变任何事的「佛教」，已经不是佛教了。（判断）

### 关于「去中国化」

这是最敏感也最实际的一问：汉传在西方，要不要去掉中国文化的成分？

回答是：**文化形式可以变，法脉不能断；而汉传之所以是汉传，不在于它说中文，在于它持有汉传的传承与判教立场。**

一个说流利英语、采用西方组织方式、课程按学期开设、学员多半是白人、但完整持有汉传传承与义理、并且讲得出天台、华严、唯识所以然来的教团，比一个说中文、穿汉服、上香拜忏一如国内、却讲不出自家宗派义理的教团，**更「汉传」。**（判断）

这个判断有现实意义。许多海外道场为了保持「传统」，把大量的组织资源投在维持语言与仪式的原样上，结果是：本地人进不来，二代留不住，最后只剩下第一代移民，随着他们老去而自然消亡。**以守护传统为名，行断送传承之实。** 这是海外最普遍的一种自我损耗。（判断）

反过来，也有另一种风险：为了本土化而把义理抽空，最后只剩一个「静坐俱乐部」。这一种肉眼可见，反而容易警觉。**两种风险中，前一种才是更普遍的、也更难察觉的。**

## 三十一、数字基础设施与谁来做

第三部已经说明：佛教 AI 的核心竞争力在语料、版本、出处、术语、传统区分，而不在模型大小。对汉传来说，这意味着一个明确的行动纲领。

**要做三件事。** 其一，把汉文佛典的权威数据做成开放、可引用、有版本的标准。这件事不必新建，整合 CBETA、SAT、DILA 以及灵隐寺等已有的工作即可，再加上统一的元数据规范与版本控制。其二，把术语层与平行语料开放出来。其三，建立可验证的 AI 与覆盖率仪表盘，把「可信佛学 AI」的标准工作流（第十八章）作为公开规范发布，让所有做佛教 AI 的团队都可以遵循。

**这三件事做完，汉传在全球佛学知识秩序里的位置就确定了。** 反过来，如果十年后这三件事仍由别人在做，那么汉传就只能适配别人定下的秩序。（判断）

### 谁来做

这是全文最想强调的一点。

上面这些事，**都不需要一个新教团，需要一个联盟。**

理由很清楚：CBETA 有数据与学术信誉，但没有教团资源；佛光山有国际网络与资金，但不以数字人文为专长；灵隐寺有技术团队与古籍，但范围限于自家项目；学术机构有方法，但不掌握经典与僧团。**任何一家单独做，都会缺一角；合起来，恰好完整。**

组织形态建议采用一个轻量的结构：一个**佛学 AI 与数字人文研究中心**，由一个高校、佛学院、大型佛教组织或跨机构联盟承办。最小可行团队要包含的不止 AI 工程师：佛学家、文献学家、数字人文学者、NLP／LLM 工程师、数据工程师、产品与体验人员、多语翻译专家、伦理治理人员。

其中最重要的一条是：**佛学专家必须真正进入 AI 开发流程，而不是在最后验收时被请来签字。** 这条听起来是常识，实际最容易落空，因为它意味着佛教学者要在项目里花时间做工程性的工作——整理语料、标注术语、校验出处——而这些工作不算学术成果。**因此还需要一个配套机制：让这些工作算成果。** 这一点如果解决不了，所有「专家进入流程」的呼吁都会落空。（判断）

治理上采用三委员会结构：**学术委员会**负责经典、版本、历史、术语与研究规范；**技术委员会**负责数据、模型、RAG、评测与安全；**伦理治理委员会**负责隐私、权威模拟、宗教伦理、未成年人、心理风险与数据使用边界。

## 三十二、国际参与：找那个交换结构

第七章说过，地缘现实绕不开。这一节不谈回避。要谈的是：**在哪些地方可以不零和**。

汉传与藏传、南传之间，其实存在一个很清楚的互补结构。

**汉传可以给出去的**——完整的比丘尼戒统（全球唯一）；大乘社会参与的组织经验（慈济、佛光山的百年实践）；人间佛教「入世而不失出世」的现代样本；汉文大藏经中那些印度已失传的论典。

**汉传可以学进来的**——藏传的学修次第与学位体系（第二十八章）；南传的禅修技术标准化与大众化方法；日本佛教的学术化与宗学教育体系（本文因语种限制未能展开）。

这个结构之所以重要，是因为它把「竞争」变成了「交换」。**当双方各有对方没有的东西时，合作就不需要以善意为基础。**（判断）

值得注意的是，汉传在其中的位置是「给得多、要得少」——比丘尼戒统是三大语系里只有汉传有的，而学修次第则是汉传可以自己建、也可以学。**这个位置其实比想象中有利，前提是汉传自己要意识到手里有什么。**（判断）

三条具体建议。**其一，把多边平台用足**——联合国系统（慈济已有经社理事会咨商地位）、大学、国际佛教组织，这三类平台的政治敏感度低于国与国之间的场合。**其二，把世界佛教论坛的议程打开**——增加非中国籍的共同主办与议程共治，重心放在共识度最高的三块：遗产保护、佛典数字化、人道救援。这三块的共同点是：没有人会反对，而做成了所有人都受益。**其三，扩大「低争议区」**——古籍数字化、术语标准、译经合作、遗产修复、灾害救援、临终关怀，这些领域不需要先解决政治问题就能推进，而它们积累的信任会外溢到其他领域。

### 一句实话

上述建议若要生效，有一个前提，说起来不太好听：**汉传需要让别人相信，它做这些事不是为了让别人欠它人情。**

这个前提无法靠声明解决，只能靠时间与一致性——同一件事做十年，不附加条件，不因政治气候而改变，别人才会慢慢相信它是真的在做这件事，而不是在做一个项目。**这是所有「软实力」讨论里最容易被忽略的一点：软实力长在别人身上，不长在自己身上，所以它无法被单方面生产出来。**（判断）

## 三十三、国内基本盘与分众路径

### 大陆：合规前提下的学修建设

大陆的处境有一层海外没有的约束。二〇二五年九月国家宗教事务局印发的《宗教教职人员网络行为规范》（国宗发〔2025〕12 号，共十八条）明确规定，教职人员应当依法规范使用生成式人工智能产品和服务，**不得利用人工智能技术传教**；在互联网上讲经讲道或从事宗教教育培训，可以且仅限于通过已取得《互联网宗教信息服务许可证》的宗教团体、宗教院校、寺观教堂依法自建的网站、应用程序、论坛等进行（A 级，[国家宗教事务局](https://www.sara.gov.cn/static/content/xxgk/2025-09-16/1417173278783471616.html)）。

这条规定划出了一条清晰的边界：**AI 在教内的角色是辅助学习，不是对外传教的通道。** 这恰与第二十章的判断一致——AI 可以辅助「学」，不能替代「修」；现在可以再加一句：在大陆，它也不能充当「传」。

据此，大陆一侧最值得做的事有三个方向。一是**遗产保护与学术基础设施**——把 AI 用于古籍整理、校勘、术语库、检索工具，这些是纯粹的知识工作，不涉传教。二是**寺院内部的教育与管理**——课程建设、僧才培养、档案管理、信众服务。三是**学修阶梯的建设**——把「寺庙热」接住，靠的不是线上弘法，是线下的课程、共修与人的陪伴。

**这三个方向有一个共同点：它们都不依赖新政策，现在就可以做。**（判断）

### 分众路径

| 人群 | 有效入口 | 关键条件 | 主要风险 |
|------|----------|----------|----------|
| 华人及东南亚裔二代 | 双语共修、青年社群、社会服务、节庆 | 本地语言人才；平等治理；青年与女性领导 | 去宗教化（华裔过半无宗教） |

这张表有一处需要说明：四类人群的「关键条件」各不相同，但**「本地语言人才」这一项在四类中都出现**——有的显写，有的隐含。这不是巧合。

**这些事没有一件能上头条。**（判断）

## 结语

汉传佛教说不清自己有多少人。

一个流传了两千年、遍布五大洲、有数万座寺院和数十万僧尼的传统，没有一份关于自己人口的可靠数字。这不是谁检索不力：全球没有一份可审计的汉传统计，也没有哪个机构在负责这件事。

而在核对种种数字的过程中，浮现出另一个同样值得记住的事实：**汉传手里握着世界上唯一完整的比丘尼戒统，而它自己的对外叙述里，几乎不提这件事。**

两件事指向同一个方向。我们的问题，首先不在于能力不足，而在于**对自己的认识不足**——不知道有多少人，不知道自己有什么，不知道自己在别人眼里是什么样子。

这不算坏消息。能力不足要几十年才补得上，认识不足往往只需要认真数一遍。

本文的另一半内容是关于 AI 的。前面用了很长的篇幅说明：佛教 AI 的竞争力不在模型大小，而在语料、版本、出处、术语、传统区分；AI 可以辅助「学」，不能替代「修」；真正的风险不是机器人有灵魂，是人把权威交给算法；最值得突破的基础设施是翻译，而 AI 让初译变便宜、让审校变得更贵。

把这些收成一句：**AI 对汉传佛教的全部意义，是让「把话说清楚」这件事第一次变得可负担。**

把话说清楚——把典籍译清楚、把术语定清楚、把出处标清楚、把次第讲清楚、把自己的家底数清楚。这件事不需要开悟，不需要神通，也不需要一个庞大的组织。它需要的是几十年如一日的、不显眼的工作，和一批愿意做这种工作的人。

而它一旦做成，汉传佛教在全球的位置就不同了。不是因为它更响，是因为它**终于能被听懂了**。

在这件事上，我们手里其实有条件。这是本文最想说的一句话。

>
> **The incense economy** depends on foot traffic and ritual services: tickets, incense, dharma assemblies, funerary rites, souvenirs. It is cash-based, immediate, and tightly bound to traffic—and therefore tends naturally toward commercialization. Traffic can be marketed; marketing costs money; money needs returns; returns need more traffic. Once the loop starts it is hard to stop. Shaolin reached the end of it.
>
> **The public-goods economy** is the other road: education (colleges, courses, textbooks), publishing (translation, books, recordings), culture (exhibitions, art, academic events), and social service (charity, medicine, hospice care). These can be charged for without harming religious character, because the justification is "a valuable service was provided," not "you worshipped, so you pay." The difficulty is that it **requires talent.** The incense economy needs only premises and ritual. The public-goods economy needs teachers, editors, translators, managers. **This is why commercialization is so hard to escape: it is the low-threshold default.** (Judgment)
>
> **Catching the temple boom.** The youth "temple boom" on the mainland (see Chapter Eleven) is the largest entry point of the past decade, and it is being wasted. Some are working at it: according to Tencent's Buddhism channel, more than 100 temples have held themed meditation camps since 2014. But against more than 33,000 temples with monastic communities, a hundred-odd is a very small proportion; and most such camps are one-off events. The event ends, and the participants disperse.
>
> **What can be designed is a ladder, not an event. Introduction**: a retreat, an online course, a clearly written pamphlet—the goal being to let the person understand what Buddhism teaches, not to demand that he accept it. **Continuity**: a regular weekly gathering, a course that can be taken repeatedly, a study group with feedback. **This is the most critical level, and the one most absent.** (Judgment) Between "comes occasionally" and "comes every week" stands not interest but **a person he knows, who will ask him why he missed last week.** **Depth**: systematic doctrinal study, advanced meditation, close reading of texts. **Responsibility**: becoming a volunteer, an assistant teacher, an organizer.
>
> Every level can be done well or gone through the motions. The difference lies in **whether anyone is actually minding it**—which requires a position in the temple responsible for "where does the person who came go next." Most temples do not have it.
>
> **Thirty: The Boundaries of Localization.** Master Hsing Yun's "go out → internationalize → localize" is the clearest route Chinese Buddhism has set. It holds, and Foguang Shan itself is the evidence. But localization has one question never seriously answered: **where is the boundary?**
>
> **A practical test.** Ask whether a given change is made **so the other person can come in, or so that we can sell better.** The first is localization; the second is dilution. Starting an English practice group so that people who do not read Chinese can begin—that is the first. Packaging meditation as stress reduction and explaining karma and rebirth as psychological suggestion—that is the second. The advantage of this test is that it is operable: every adjustment can be run through it.
>
> **What can change**—language; organizational form (boards, foundations, corporate structures); modes of ritual expression; educational method (courses, semesters, online teaching); daily life (adaptation of food, clothing, and schedule). **What cannot change**—the core of the precepts; the integrity of the lineage; the basic determinations of doctrine (one may not, to please, render "non-self" as "finding your true self").
>
> The line is not absolute, and a large middle ground requires judgment. But one class of change clearly crosses it: **change made so that the audience need not change at all.** The transmission of the dharma has always required a kind of change—not cultural change, but a change in view and conduct. A "Buddhism" that requires no one to change anything is no longer Buddhism. (Judgment)
>
> **On "de-Sinicization."** Culture forms may change; the lineage may not be broken. And what makes Chinese Buddhism Chinese is not that it speaks Chinese, but that it holds the Chinese transmission and its doctrinal standpoint. An order that speaks fluent English, adopts Western organizational forms, runs courses by semester, and has a mostly white membership—but fully holds the Chinese lineage and doctrine and can explain Tiantai, Huayan, and Yogācāra on their own terms—is **more "Chinese Buddhist"** than an order that speaks Chinese, wears Chinese robes, and performs liturgies exactly as at home, yet cannot explain the doctrine of its own school. (Judgment)
>
> The judgment has practical weight. Many overseas temples, in order to preserve "tradition," invest a large share of organizational resources in keeping language and ritual unchanged. The result: locals cannot enter, the second generation does not stay, and what remains is the first generation of immigrants, fading as they age. **In the name of guarding the tradition, the transmission is ended.** This is the most widespread form of self-erosion abroad. (Judgment) The opposite risk exists—hollowing out the doctrine until nothing remains but a sitting club—but that one is visible and therefore easier to guard against. **Of the two, the first is more common and much harder to notice.**
>
> **Thirty-One: Digital Infrastructure and Who Should Build It.** Part Three established that the core competitiveness of Buddhist AI lies in corpora, editions, provenance, terminology, and the distinguishing of traditions—not in model size. Three things to do: **make authoritative Chinese canonical data open, citable, and versioned** (not new construction but the integration of existing work at CBETA, SAT, DILA, and Lingyin Temple, with unified metadata standards and version control); **open up the terminology layer and parallel corpora**; and **build verifiable AI and a coverage dashboard**, publishing the standard workflow for trustworthy Buddhist AI as an open specification that any Buddhist AI team may follow. **Once these three are done, Chinese Buddhism's place in the global order of Buddhist knowledge is settled.** Conversely, if in ten years the three are still being done by others, Chinese Buddhism will be adapting to an order someone else defined. (Judgment)
>
> **Who should build it.** None of the above **requires a new order. It requires an alliance.** CBETA has data and scholarly credibility but no order's resources. Foguang Shan has an international network and funding but no specialization in digital humanities. Lingyin Temple has a technical team and rare books but a scope limited to its own project. Academic institutions have method but hold neither the canon nor the sangha. **Any one of them acting alone is missing a corner; together they are exactly complete.**
>
> The organizational form suggested is light: a **centre for Buddhist AI and digital humanities**, hosted by a university, a Buddhist college, a large Buddhist organization, or a cross-institutional consortium. The minimal viable team is not an AI team alone but: Buddhologists, textual scholars, digital humanists, NLP and LLM engineers, data engineers, product and UX staff, multilingual translation specialists, and ethics and governance staff.
>
> The most important rule: **Buddhist specialists must genuinely enter the AI development process, not be invited to sign off at the end.** This sounds like common sense and is in practice the easiest thing to lose, because it means Buddhist scholars spending project time on engineering work—organizing corpora, annotating terminology, verifying sources—none of which counts as scholarship. **A companion mechanism is therefore required: make that work count.** Unless this is resolved, every call for "specialists in the process" will come to nothing. (Judgment)
>
> Governance takes a three-committee structure: an **academic committee** for canon, editions, history, terminology, and research norms; a **technical committee** for data, models, RAG, evaluation, and safety; and an **ethics and governance committee** for privacy, simulation of authority, religious ethics, minors, psychological risk, and the boundaries of data use.
>
> **Thirty-Two: International Engagement — Finding the Exchange Structure.** Chapter Seven established that geopolitical realities cannot be sidestepped. This section is about **where the game need not be zero-sum.**
>
> A clear complementary structure exists among Chinese, Tibetan, and Theravāda Buddhism. **What Chinese Buddhism can give**—the complete bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage; a century of organizational experience in Mahāyāna social engagement; a modern example of Humanistic Buddhism that is engaged without losing the supramundane; and the treatises preserved in the Chinese canon that were lost in India. **What Chinese Buddhism can take in**—Tibetan Buddhism's study sequences and degree system (Chapter Twenty-Eight); Theravāda's techniques for standardizing and popularizing meditation; Japanese Buddhism's academic and sectarian educational systems (not developed here, owing to the language limits noted earlier).
>
> The structure matters because it turns competition into exchange. **When each side holds what the other lacks, cooperation does not need goodwill as its foundation.** (Judgment) Notably, Chinese Buddhism's position is one of giving more and needing less. **The position is more advantageous than it appears, provided Chinese Buddhism realizes what it holds.** (Judgment)
>
> Three proposals: **use multilateral platforms fully** (the UN system, universities, international Buddhist organizations are all less politically sensitive than state-to-state settings); **open the agenda of the World Buddhist Forum** (add non-Chinese co-hosts and shared agenda governance, focusing on the three areas of highest consensus—heritage preservation, digitization, humanitarian relief—which no one objects to and everyone benefits from); and **widen the low-controversy zone** (digitization of rare books, terminology standards, translation cooperation, heritage restoration, disaster relief, hospice care—fields that do not require political questions to be settled first, and whose accumulated trust spills over into others).
>
> **One plain statement.** If the above is to work, one precondition applies, and it is not flattering to say: **Chinese Buddhism needs others to believe it is not doing these things in order to put them in its debt.** That precondition cannot be met by declaration, only by time and consistency—doing the same thing for ten years, without conditions, without shifting with the political weather. **This is the point most often missed in all discussions of soft power: soft power grows on other people, not on oneself, and therefore cannot be produced unilaterally.** (Judgment)
>
> **Thirty-Three: The Domestic Base and Audience-Specific Paths. The mainland: study and practice under compliance.** The mainland labours under one constraint the overseas field does not. In September 2025 the State Administration for Religious Affairs issued the *Norms for the Online Conduct of Religious Personnel* (Guozongfa [2025] No. 12, eighteen articles), which provides that religious personnel shall use generative AI products and services in accordance with the law and **may not use AI technology to proselytize**; and that lecturing online or conducting religious education may be done only through websites, applications, and forums lawfully built by religious bodies, colleges, and temples holding an *Internet Religious Information Service Licence* (Grade A, [State Administration for Religious Affairs](https://www.sara.gov.cn/static/content/xxgk/2025-09-16/1417173278783471616.html)).
>
> The provision draws a clear boundary: **AI's role within the religion is to assist study, not to serve as a channel for transmitting outward.** This aligns with Chapter Twenty's judgment—AI can assist study, it cannot replace practice; and here we may add: on the mainland, it cannot serve as transmission either.
>
> Three directions are therefore worth pursuing: **heritage protection and scholarly infrastructure** (using AI for collation, textual criticism, terminology databases, retrieval tools—purely knowledge work, not proselytizing); **internal education and administration** (curriculum, monastic training, archives, service to adherents); and **building the study ladder** (catching the temple boom requires not online proselytizing but offline courses, group practice, and human accompaniment). **All three share one feature: they require no new policy. They can be done now.** (Judgment)
>
> **Audience-specific paths.**
>
> | Audience | Effective entry | Key condition | Principal risk |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Chinese and Southeast Asian second generation | Bilingual group practice, youth communities, social service, festivals | Local-language talent; egalitarian governance; youth and women's leadership | De-religionization (over half of Chinese Americans claim no religion) |
> | Buddhist countries (South and Southeast Asia) | Heritage protection, education, medicine, digitization, ordination cooperation | Multilateral, non-state framing | Sino-Indian rivalry, politicization |
> | Western converts and the "spiritual but not religious" | Chan retreats, translation, lay transmission | Local teachers; precept and financial transparency | Zen brand obscuring; scandal |
> | Academics and digital users | Open corpora, machine translation and search, terminology databases | Evaluation benchmarks, open licences | AI hallucination; intra-religious disputes over authority |
>
> One point about the table: the "key condition" differs across the four audiences, but **"local-language talent" appears in all four**—sometimes stated, sometimes implied. That is not coincidence.
>
> **Scenarios and leading indicators.**
>
> | Scenario | Condition | Consequence | Leading indicators |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | ① Cultural contraction (baseline) | Present trends continue | Identity keeps declining; overseas presence sustained by immigration; orders dependent on individual figures | CGSS/CFPS identification rates; second-generation participation |
> | ② Parallel tracks | Taiwanese orders take the NGO route, the mainland the cultural-diplomacy route, little coordination | Each grows but they constrain each other; trust costs stay high | Governance disclosure rates at new overseas temples; number of multilateral projects |
> | ③ Infrastructure-driven restart | Open corpora and verifiable AI become common standards; governance remedies in place | The textual channel becomes Chinese Buddhism's main route to the world | English translation coverage; open corpus scale; number of English-speaking teachers |
>
> Of the three, ③ is neither inevitable nor the easiest. It requires the unglamorous work repeatedly named above: translating, fixing terminology, building corpora, writing textbooks, training reviewers, publishing annual reports, handing the transmission to local people.
>
> **None of it makes a headline.** (Judgment)
>
> ### Conclusion
>
> Chinese Buddhism cannot say how many people it has.
>
> A tradition that has run for two thousand years, spread across five continents, and holds tens of thousands of temples and hundreds of thousands of monastics has no reliable figure for its own population. This is not a failure of anyone's searching: no auditable census of Chinese Buddhism exists anywhere, and no institution is charged with producing one.
>
> And in the course of checking one figure after another, a second fact surfaced, equally worth remembering: **Chinese Buddhism holds the only complete bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage in the world, and its own accounts of itself abroad almost never mention it.**
>
> The two point the same way. Our problem is not first a shortage of capacity. It is **a shortage of self-knowledge**—not knowing how many we are, not knowing what we hold, not knowing how we appear to others.
>
> That is not bad news. A shortage of capacity takes decades to remedy; a shortage of self-knowledge often requires only a careful count.
>
> The other half of this report concerns AI. It has argued at length that the competitiveness of Buddhist AI lies not in model size but in corpora, editions, provenance, terminology, and the distinguishing of traditions; that AI can assist study but cannot replace practice; that the real risk is not a machine with a soul but a person handing authority to an algorithm; and that the infrastructure most worth breaking through is translation—where AI makes first drafts cheap and makes review more expensive.
>
> Compressed into one sentence: **the whole significance of AI for Chinese Buddhism is that it makes "saying things clearly" affordable for the first time.**
>
> Saying things clearly—translating the texts clearly, fixing the terminology clearly, marking the sources clearly, explaining the sequence clearly, counting our own holdings clearly. This does not require enlightenment or supernatural powers or a large organization. It requires decades of unremarkable work, and a group of people willing to do it.
>
> And once it is done, Chinese Buddhism's place in the world will be different. Not because it is louder, but because it **can finally be understood.**
>
> On this, the conditions are in our hands. That is the one thing this report most wants to say.
